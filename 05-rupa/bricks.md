# Chapter 3: Sarvānāmāni — Pronouns

## Goal
Understand what sarvānāmas (pronouns) are, learn the Pāṇinian list of pronominal words, master the declension of key pronouns (tad, etad, yad, kim, idam, asmad, yuṣmad, sarva), and use them correctly in sentences.

## What is a Sarvānāma?

**Sarvānāma** (सर्वनाम) literally means "a name for all" — a word that stands in place of a noun (nāma). Just as in English we say "he" instead of repeating "Rāma," Sanskrit uses pronouns to replace nouns already known from context.

> **Definition:** yasya nāmnaḥ sthāne yat padam upayujyate tat sarvānāma ityucyate
> "That word which is used in place of a noun is called a sarvānāma."

### Pronouns vs. Nouns

A noun (nāma-pada) is formed from a **prātipadika** (nominal stem) + **sup** (case suffix). A pronoun likewise takes case endings, but its declensional forms often differ significantly from regular noun paradigms. These special forms must be learned separately.

## Pāṇini's List of Sarvānāmas

Pāṇini defines sarvānāmas by enumeration (Aṣṭādhyāyī 1.1.27: sarvādīni sarvānāmāni). He gives a list of **35 pronominal words**. The most important ones for daily use are:

| # | Sarvānāma | Meaning |
|---|-----------|---------|
| 1 | sarva | all, every |
| 2 | viśva | all, entire |
| 3 | ubhaya | both |
| 4 | tad | that, he/she/it |
| 5 | etad | this (proximate) |
| 6 | yad | which, who (relative) |
| 7 | kim | who? what? (interrogative) |
| 8 | idam | this |
| 9 | adas | that (remote) |
| 10 | eka | one |
| 11 | anya | other |
| 12 | anyatara | either |
| 13 | itara | the other |
| 14 | dvitaya | of two kinds |
| 15 | tatva | -ness (pronominal in certain usages) |
| 16 | pūrva | former, east |
| 17 | para | other, subsequent |
| 18 | avara | near, inferior |
| 19 | dakṣiṇa | right, south |
| 20 | uttara | upper, north |
| 21 | apara | another |
| 22 | adhara | lower |
| 23 | sva | own, self |

Of these, the most frequently encountered in sentences are: **tad, etad, yad, kim, idam, asmad, yuṣmad,** and **sarva**.

## Key Concepts

### Liṅga, Vacana, Vibhakti

Like nouns, pronouns inflect for:
- **Liṅga** (gender): puṃliṅga (masculine), strīliṅga (feminine), napuṃsakaliṅga (neuter)
- **Vacana** (number): ekavacana (singular), dvivacana (dual), bahuvacana (plural)
- **Vibhakti** (case): all 8 cases (prathamā through sambodhana)

### Gender Independence of asmad and yuṣmad

A critical point: **asmad** (I/we) and **yuṣmad** (you) do NOT change for gender. Whether the speaker is male, female, or referring to neuter objects, the forms remain the same.

> aham chātraḥ (I am a student — male speaker)
> aham chātrā (I am a student — female speaker)
> The pronoun **aham** stays identical in both.

### The Concept of Vivakṣā

**Vivakṣā** (विवक्षा) means "the desire/intent of the speaker." This is important for understanding when to use **sarva** vs. **sarve**:

- **sarve sanskṛtam paṭhanti** = "All (of them) study Sanskrit" — here the speaker intends to show the individual members (avayava-vivakṣā), using the plural.
- **sarvam jagat** = "the entire world" — here the speaker treats the collective as one unit (samudāya-vivakṣā), using the singular neuter.

The choice between singular/plural forms of sarva depends on whether you view the group as a collective whole or as individual members.

## Declension of tad (that, he/she/it)

The pronoun **tad** is the most common. Its forms differ markedly from regular -a stem nouns.

## Declension of asmad (I, we) and yuṣmad (you)

These are **unique** — they have no gender distinction and use completely irregular stems.

### asmad (first person)

| Vibhakti | Ekavacana | Dvivacana | Bahuvacana |
|----------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Prathamā | aham | āvām | vayam |
| Dvitīyā | mām (mā) | āvām (nau) | asmān (naḥ) |
| Tṛtīyā | mayā | āvābhyām | asmābhiḥ |
| Caturthī | mahyam (me) | āvābhyām (nau) | asmabhyam (naḥ) |
| Pañcamī | mat | āvābhyām | asmat |
| Ṣaṣṭhī | mama (me) | āvayoḥ (nau) | asmākam (naḥ) |
| Saptamī | mayi | āvayoḥ | asmāsu |

### yuṣmad (second person)

| Vibhakti | Ekavacana | Dvivacana | Bahuvacana |
|----------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Prathamā | tvam | yuvām | yūyam |
| Dvitīyā | tvām (tvā) | yuvām (vām) | yuṣmān (vaḥ) |
| Tṛtīyā | tvayā | yuvābhyām | yuṣmābhiḥ |
| Caturthī | tubhyam (te) | yuvābhyām (vām) | yuṣmabhyam (vaḥ) |
| Pañcamī | tvat | yuvābhyām | yuṣmat |
| Ṣaṣṭhī | tava (te) | yuvayoḥ (vām) | yuṣmākam (vaḥ) |
| Saptamī | tvayi | yuvayoḥ | yuṣmāsu |

**Note:** Forms in parentheses are enclitic (shortened) alternatives that cannot appear at the beginning of a sentence.

## Declension of sarva (all, every)

**sarva** follows the sarvānāma declension pattern, which differs from regular -a stem nouns in specific vibhaktis (notably prathamā neuter singular: **sarvam** not **sarvaḥ**, and saptamī singular: **sarvasmin** not **sarve**).

### Puṃliṅga (Masculine)

| Vibhakti | Ekavacana | Dvivacana | Bahuvacana |
|----------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Prathamā | sarvaḥ | sarvau | sarve |
| Dvitīyā | sarvam | sarvau | sarvān |
| Tṛtīyā | sarveṇa | sarvābhyām | sarvaiḥ |
| Caturthī | sarvasmai | sarvābhyām | sarvebhyaḥ |
| Pañcamī | sarvasmāt | sarvābhyām | sarvebhyaḥ |
| Ṣaṣṭhī | sarvasya | sarvayoḥ | sarveṣām |
| Saptamī | sarvasmin | sarvayoḥ | sarveṣu |

| Tṛtīyā | sarvayā | sarvābhyām | sarvābhiḥ |
| Caturthī | sarvasyai | sarvābhyām | sarvābhyaḥ |

## How Pronominal Declension Differs from Regular Nouns

The sarvānāma declension (sarvādi pattern) differs from regular -a stem nouns in these key vibhaktis:

| Vibhakti | Regular -a noun (deva) | Sarvānāma (sarva) | Difference |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|------------|
| Prathamā N. sg. | devam (not applicable — deva is M) | sarvam | N. sg. ends in -am (not -aḥ) |
| Caturthī sg. | devāya | sarvasmai | -smai instead of -āya |
| Pañcamī sg. | devāt | sarvasmāt | -smāt instead of -āt |
| Saptamī sg. | deve | sarvasmin | -smin instead of -e |
| Ṣaṣṭhī pl. | devānām | sarveṣām | -eṣām instead of -ānām |
| Saptamī pl. | deveṣu | sarveṣu | (same in this case) |

The **-smai, -smāt, -smin** endings in caturthī, pañcamī, and saptamī singular are the hallmark of pronominal declension.

## Usage in Sentences

### Demonstrative pronouns (tad, etad, idam)

| Sanskrit | Translation |
|----------|-------------|
| saḥ bālakaḥ paṭhati | That boy studies. |
| sā bālikā gacchati | That girl goes. |
| tat pustakam atra asti | That book is here. |
| eṣaḥ mama mitraḥ | This is my friend. |
| eṣā sundarī nagarī | This is a beautiful city. |
| ayam bālakaḥ krīḍati | This boy plays. |
| iyam bālikā hasati | This girl laughs. |
| idam mandiram prācīnam | This temple is ancient. |

### Relative-correlative (yad...tad)

| Sanskrit | Translation |
|----------|-------------|
| yaḥ paṭhati saḥ jānāti | He who studies, he knows. |
| yā sevāṃ karoti sā sukhaṃ labhate | She who serves, she obtains happiness. |

### Interrogative (kim)

| Sanskrit | Translation |
|----------|-------------|
| kaḥ atra āgacchati? | Who comes here? |
| kā eṣā bālikā? | Who is this girl? |
| kim etat? | What is this? |
| kasya etat pustakam? | Whose book is this? |

### Personal pronouns (asmad, yuṣmad)

| Sanskrit | Translation |
|----------|-------------|
| aham chātraḥ asmi | I am a student. |
| tvam krīḍā-pāṭavaḥ asi | You are a sportsperson. |
| vayam sanskṛtam paṭhāmaḥ | We study Sanskrit. |
| yūyam kutra gacchatha? | Where do you all go? |
