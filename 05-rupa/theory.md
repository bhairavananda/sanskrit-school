# Stage 3: Rūpa — Case, Number, and Gender (Declension)

## Goal
Learn to transform nouns through all cases (vibhaktis), numbers, and genders. Decline devotional vocabulary actively so that words become usable in sentences.

### What is a Stem?

Every noun in Sanskrit has a **prātipadika** (nominal stem) — a finished subassembly, the base form before any case ending is attached. When you look up a word like "deva" or "nadī" in its dictionary form, you are seeing the prātipadika.

The prātipadika's **final sound** determines which declension family it belongs to. A stem ending in -a follows one pattern, a stem ending in -ī follows another, and so on. This is the single most important fact for learning declension: identify the final sound, and you know which pattern to apply.

**Stem type and grammatical gender are separate dimensions.** An -a stem can be masculine (deva) OR neuter (phala). Stems ending in -i and -u occur in all three genders — masculine, feminine, and neuter. You must know both the stem type and the gender to select the correct paradigm.

The course teaches **one representative model per family**. Learn the model (deva for masculine -a, nadī for feminine -ī, viṣṇu for masculine -u, etc.), then apply the same pattern to any noun of the same type. "rāma" declines exactly like "deva." "lakṣmī" declines exactly like "nadī." The endings never change within a family.

The core formula of this stage is: **prātipadika + sup = inflected noun.**

The **prātipadika** is a finished subassembly — it carries meaning but cannot yet participate in a sentence. The **sup** (noun connector) is the case ending that plugs the subassembly into a specific grammatical role. The **vibhakti** is the semantic relationship (subject, object, instrument, etc.) that results from applying a particular sup ending.

Think of it this way: stem + intended grammatical relationship → correct sup ending → inflected form → sandhi at the seam → surface form.

## What is Rūpa?

**Rūpa** (रूप) means "form." In Sanskrit, nouns change their form (ending) depending on their role in a sentence. This system of changes is called **declension** (śabda-rūpa).

## The Eight Cases (Vibhaktis)

| # | Vibhakti | Name | Function | Question | Example |
|---|---------|------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Prathamā | Nominative | Subject | Who/What? | śivaḥ (Śiva [does]) |
| 2 | Dvitīyā | Accusative | Object | Whom/What? | śivam (Śiva [I worship]) |
| 3 | Tṛtīyā | Instrumental | By/With | By whom? | śivena (by Śiva) |
| 4 | Caturthī | Dative | For/To | For whom? | śivāya (for Śiva) |
| 5 | Pañcamī | Ablative | From | From whom? | śivāt (from Śiva) |
| 6 | Ṣaṣṭhī | Genitive | Of | Whose? | śivasya (of Śiva) |
| 7 | Saptamī | Locative | In/On | Where? | śive (in Śiva) |
| 8 | Sambodhana | Vocative | Address | O...! | he śiva! |

## The Three Numbers

| Vacana | Meaning | Example (prathamā of deva) |
|--------|---------|---------------------------|
| Ekavacana | Singular | devaḥ |
| Dvivacana | Dual | devau |
| Bahuvacana | Plural | devāḥ |

#### Neuter -a stems

Neuter -a stems (phala, jñāna, puṣpa) share cases 3-7 with their masculine counterparts. The key differences are in the nominative and accusative:

| | Masculine (deva) | Neuter (phala) |
|---|---|---|
| Prathamā eka | devaḥ | phalam (-am) |
| Prathamā dvi | devau | phale (-e) |
| Prathamā bahu | devāḥ | phalāni (-āni) |
| Dvitīyā eka | devam | phalam (-am) |
| Dvitīyā dvi | devau | phale (-e) |
| Dvitīyā bahu | devān | phalāni (-āni) |

In neuter stems, prathamā and dvitīyā are always identical — the subject form and the object form are the same. This is true across all neuter paradigms, not just -a stems.

#### A note on -i stems

The reference guide includes a masculine -i paradigm (agni, muni). Feminine -i stems (mati, gati, bhūmi) and neuter -i stems (vāri, akṣi) follow slightly different patterns from the masculine. The masculine paradigm in the reference guide is the core model — once you know it, the feminine and neuter variants require learning only a handful of differing forms.

#### Gender variants of -u stems

Feminine -u stems (dhenu — cow, tanu — body) and neuter -u stems (madhu — honey, vastu — thing) follow the same general pattern as masculine -u but differ in specific endings, particularly in the nominative, accusative, and instrumental. The masculine viṣṇu paradigm above is the core model for the -u family.

## Model Paradigm: Feminine -ū stem (vadhū — bride)

Long-vowel -ū stems are typically feminine. The paradigm of vadhū serves as the model.

| Vibhakti | Eka | Dvi | Bahu |
|----------|-----|-----|------|
| Prathamā | vadhūḥ | vadhvau | vadhvaḥ |
| Dvitīyā | vadhūm | vadhvau | vadhūḥ |
| Tṛtīyā | vadhvā | vadhūbhyām | vadhūbhiḥ |
| Caturthī | vadhvai | vadhūbhyām | vadhūbhyaḥ |
| Pañcamī | vadhvāḥ | vadhūbhyām | vadhūbhyaḥ |
| Ṣaṣṭhī | vadhvāḥ | vadhvoḥ | vadhūnām |
| Saptamī | vadhvām | vadhvoḥ | vadhūṣu |
| Sambodhana | he vadhu! | he vadhvau! | he vadhvaḥ! |

## Sarvānāma — Pronoun Subassemblies

Pronouns (sarvānāma) are among the most essential words in Sanskrit. They replace noun subassemblies (prātipadika) entirely with their own unique sup patterns.

### First Person (asmad — I/we)

| Vibhakti | Eka | Dvi | Bahu |
|----------|-----|-----|------|
| Prathamā | aham | āvām | vayam |
| Dvitīyā | mām (mā) | āvām (nau) | asmān (naḥ) |
| Tṛtīyā | mayā | āvābhyām | asmābhiḥ |
| Caturthī | mahyam (me) | āvābhyām (nau) | asmabhyam (naḥ) |
| Pañcamī | mat | āvābhyām | asmat |
| Ṣaṣṭhī | mama (me) | āvayoḥ (nau) | asmākam (naḥ) |
| Saptamī | mayi | āvayoḥ | asmāsu |

Parenthetical forms (mā, me, nau, naḥ) are enclitic (shortened) forms used in everyday speech and poetry.

## Key Insight

The case system is what makes Sanskrit a free-word-order language. Since the function of each word is encoded in its ending, you can rearrange words freely:
- śivāya puṣpam dadāmi
- puṣpam śivāya dadāmi
- dadāmi śivāya puṣpam

All three mean: "I give a flower to Śiva."

## Milestone Check

Can you decline śiva, puṣpa, mālā, and nadī through all 8 cases in singular from memory?

Can you also decline viṣṇu, mātṛ, and bhagavat? Can you use aham, tvam, and saḥ/sā in simple devotional sentences?

### Tiered Roadmap

These seven vowel-stem families (-a masculine, -a neuter, -ā, -ī, -ū, -i, -u) are your **core prātipadika types** (finished subassembly shapes). They cover the vast majority of nouns you will encounter in stotra, mantra, and devotional texts.

In **Stage 9**, you will meet **consonant-stem families** (-an, -in, -ant/-at, -man) — the prātipadika types for philosophical and epithet vocabulary. Words like "ātman" (self), "yogin" (practitioner), "bhagavant" (the blessed one), and "brahman" (the absolute) all belong to these families.

By **Stage 12**, you will encounter **advanced stem types** (-as/-is/-us, root consonant stems) and irregular paradigms. These include words like "manas" (mind), "havis" (oblation), and "vāc" (speech).

The principle never changes: **learn the model, then generate.**

### Prātipadika Types and the Sup Connector System

You now know **7 prātipadika types** (finished subassembly shapes) and the **sup connector system** (noun connectors) that plugs them into sentences.

| Prātipadika Type (Subassembly) | Examples | Sup Connectors (Noun Endings) |
|------------|----------|---------------|
| -a stem (M) | deva, rāma | All 8 sup endings; the foundational subassembly |
| -a stem (N) | phala, jñāna | Same sups as M for cases 3-7; nom/acc sups differ |
| -ā stem (F) | mālā, gaṅgā | Feminine -ā sup endings |
| -ī stem (F) | nadī, lakṣmī | Feminine -ī sup endings; -y- glide at the seam |
| -ū stem (F) | vadhū, camū | Feminine -ū sup endings; -v- glide at the seam |
| -i stem (M) | agni, muni | -i class sup endings (see reference) |
| -u stem (M) | viṣṇu, guru | -u class sup endings |
| Ṛ-stem | mātṛ, pitṛ | Kinship/agent sup endings |
| Consonant-stem (-at) | bhagavat, mahat | -at class sup endings; titles and adjective-nouns |
| Pronoun subassemblies (1st/2nd/3rd) | aham/mām/mama, tvam, saḥ/sā/tat | Replace noun subassemblies entirely; unique sup patterns |

That is **7 vowel-stem prātipadika types** plus the ṛ-stem and one consonant-stem preview. Consonant-stem types (-an, -in, -man, -ant) arrive in Stage 9.

**Try snapping**: Attach a pronoun subassembly to a verb from Stage 6: "aham devam namāmi" (I bow to the deity). Now swap the pronoun: "saḥ devam namati" (he bows to the deity). Now swap the noun subassembly to ṛ-stem: "aham mātaram namāmi" (I bow to the mother). In each case: prātipadika + sup → inflected form → sandhi at the seam → the word you hear.
