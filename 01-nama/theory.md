# Stage 1: Nāma — Basic Vocabulary

## Goal
Learn foundational Sanskrit nouns related to devotion, deity forms, pūjā items, and sacred qualities. By the end, you should be able to describe your iṣṭadevatā using at least 20 words.

## What is a Nāma?

In Sanskrit, **nāma** (नाम) means "name" or "noun." Every object, deity, quality, and concept has a nāma. Sanskrit nouns have three properties:
- **Liṅga** (gender): puṃliṅga (masculine), strīliṅga (feminine), napuṃsakaliṅga (neuter)
- **Vacana** (number): ekavacana (singular), dvivacana (dual), bahuvacana (plural)
- **Vibhakti** (case): prathamā (nominative) is the basic "naming" form

At this stage, we only learn words in their **prathamā ekavacana** (nominative singular) form — the dictionary form.

## Gender Patterns

Most Sanskrit nouns follow predictable gender patterns:

| Ending | Typical Gender | Examples |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| -aḥ | Masculine | devaḥ, śivaḥ, puṣpaḥ |
| -ā | Feminine | devī → but also: latā, mālā, gaṅgā |
| -am | Neuter | phalam, jalam, dhāma |
| -ī | Feminine | devī, nadī, lakṣmī |

Note: These are tendencies, not absolute rules.

## Core Vocabulary Categories

### 1. Deities (Devatāḥ)
- **śivaḥ** (शिवः) — the auspicious one, Śiva
- **viṣṇuḥ** (विष्णुः) — the all-pervading, Viṣṇu
- **devī** (देवी) — the goddess
- **gaṇeśaḥ** (गणेशः) — lord of the gaṇas
- **sarasvatī** (सरस्वती) — goddess of knowledge
- **lakṣmī** (लक्ष्मी) — goddess of prosperity
- **kṛṣṇaḥ** (कृष्णः) — the dark one, Kṛṣṇa
- **rāmaḥ** (रामः) — Rāma
- **hanumān** (हनुमान्) — Hanumān
- **sūryaḥ** (सूर्यः) — the sun deity

### 2. Body and Form (Śarīram)
- **mukham** (मुखम्) — face
- **netraṃ** (नेत्रम्) — eye
- **karaḥ** (करः) — hand
- **pādaḥ** (पादः) — foot
- **śiraḥ** (शिरः) — head
- **hṛdayam** (हृदयम्) — heart
- **kaṇṭhaḥ** (कण्ठः) — throat
- **jaṭā** (जटा) — matted locks

### 3. Pūjā Items (Pūjā-sāmagrī)
- **puṣpam** (पुष्पम्) — flower
- **dhūpaḥ** (धूपः) — incense
- **dīpaḥ** (दीपः) — lamp
- **naivedyam** (नैवेद्यम्) — food offering
- **jalam** (जलम्) — water
- **candanam** (चन्दनम्) — sandalwood paste
- **akṣatāḥ** (अक्षताः) — unbroken rice
- **camphor** → **karpūram** (कर्पूरम्)
- **mālā** (माला) — garland
- **pātraṃ** (पात्रम्) — vessel

### 4. Sacred Objects and Places
- **mandiraṃ** (मन्दिरम्) — temple
- **mūrtiḥ** (मूर्तिः) — image, form
- **yantraṃ** (यन्त्रम्) — sacred diagram
- **ghaṇṭā** (घण्टा) — bell
- **āsanam** (आसनम्) — seat
- **śaṅkhaḥ** (शङ्खः) — conch
- **rudrākṣaḥ** (रुद्राक्षः) — rudrākṣa bead
- **liṅgam** (लिङ्गम्) — the Śiva-liṅga
- **padmam** (पद्मम्) — lotus
- **nadī** (नदी) — river

### 5. Nature and Cosmos
- **sūryaḥ** (सूर्यः) — sun
- **candraḥ** (चन्द्रः) — moon
- **agniḥ** (अग्निः) — fire
- **vāyuḥ** (वायुः) — wind
- **ākāśaḥ** (आकाशः) — sky/space
- **pṛthivī** (पृथिवी) — earth
- **jalam** (जलम्) — water
- **parvatah** (पर्वतः) — mountain
- **vanam** (वनम्) — forest
- **tārakā** (तारका) — star

## The Game: "Name the Divine"

Practice by looking at an image of your iṣṭadevatā and naming everything you see:
- What is the deity holding? (triśūlam, padmam, veṇuḥ…)
- What is the deity wearing? (mālā, vasanam…)
- What surrounds the deity? (agniḥ, padmam, parvatah…)
- What qualities does the deity embody? (preview of Stage 2)

## Key Principle: Nāma as Mantra

In the bhakti tradition, the name itself carries śakti. Learning nāma is not merely vocabulary acquisition — it is the first act of recognition. When you say "śivaḥ," you are not labeling an object; you are invoking a presence.
