# Stage 9: Give & Take

## The Pattern

**mahyam X dehi** — "Give me X"

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| मह्यम् X देहि | mahyam X dehi | Give me X |
| तुभ्यम् Y ददामि | tubhyam Y dadāmi | I give you Y |
| एतत् गृहाण | etat gṛhāṇa | Take this |
| किम् ददामि? | kim dadāmi? | What shall I give? |

---

## How It Works

When you give something, two things matter: *what* you're giving and *to whom*. The "to whom" part uses the dative case — the "for/to" form. You only need a few: **mahyam** (to me), **tubhyam** (to you), **tasmai** (to him), **tasyai** (to her). These are the dative forms of the pronouns you already know.

The verb **dā** (to give) has a distinctive conjugation: **dadāmi** (I give), **dadāsi** (you give), **dadāti** (he/she gives). The imperative "give!" is **dehi** (casual) or **dadātu** (polite). For "take," use the root **grah**: the imperative is **gṛhāṇa** (take!). Between *dehi* and *gṛhāṇa*, you can handle every exchange from chai stalls to gift-giving.

## Example Phrases

1. मह्यम् जलं देहि।
   mahyam jalaṃ dehi.
   *Give me water.*

2. तुभ्यम् एतत् पुस्तकं ददामि।
   tubhyam etat pustakaṃ dadāmi.
   *I give you this book.*

3. एतत् फलं गृहाण।
   etat phalaṃ gṛhāṇa.
   *Take this fruit.*

4. तस्मै धनं देहि।
   tasmai dhanaṃ dehi.
   *Give him money.*

5. मह्यम् एकं चायं देहि।
   mahyam ekaṃ cāyaṃ dehi.
   *Give me one tea.*

6. अहं तुभ्यं किं ददामि?
   ahaṃ tubhyaṃ kiṃ dadāmi?
   *What shall I give you?*

7. तस्यै पुष्पं ददातु।
   tasyai puṣpaṃ dadātu.
   *Please give her a flower.*

8. मह्यम् समयं देहि।
   mahyam samayaṃ dehi.
   *Give me time.*

9. एतत् सर्वं गृहाण।
   etat sarvaṃ gṛhāṇa.
   *Take all of this.*

10. तुभ्यम् किम् आवश्यकम्?
    tubhyam kim āvaśyakam?
    *What do you need? (What is necessary for you?)*

---

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **अ:** मह्यम् एकं चायं देहि।
> A: mahyam ekaṃ cāyaṃ dehi.
> *Give me one tea.*

> **ब:** एतत् गृहाण। दशरूप्यकाणि देहि।
> B: etat gṛhāṇa. daśarūpyakāṇi dehi.
> *Take this. Give ten rupees.*

> **अ:** एतत् गृहाण।
> A: etat gṛhāṇa.
> *Here, take this.*

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **अ:** तुभ्यम् किं ददामि?
> A: tubhyam kiṃ dadāmi?
> *What shall I give you?*

> **ब:** मह्यम् एकं फलं देहि।
> B: mahyam ekaṃ phalaṃ dehi.
> *Give me one fruit.*

> **अ:** एतत् आम्रं गृहाण।
> A: etat āmraṃ gṛhāṇa.
> *Take this mango.*

> **ब:** धन्यवादः!
> B: dhanyavādaḥ!
> *Thank you!*

## Variations

1. **mahyam X dadātu** — Polite form: "Please give me X." Use *dadātu* instead of *dehi* when speaking to elders, strangers, or anyone you want to be respectful toward.

2. **X yaccha** — Another word for "give" from the root *yam*. The imperative *yaccha* is common in classical texts. *mahyam jalaṃ yaccha* = "hand me water."

3. **kim dadāmi? / kim dehi?** — Useful pair. "What shall I give?" (offering) vs. "What do you want?" (asking what they need).

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **mahyam vs. mama:** Both mean "me" but in different cases. *mama* = "of me" (possession). *mahyam* = "to/for me" (giving). "I have a book" = *mama pustakam asti*. "Give me a book" = *mahyam pustakaṃ dehi*. Don't swap them.

2. **dehi is casual:** Using *dehi* with a teacher or elder sounds rude — like barking "give!" Use *dadātu* (please give) for politeness.
