# Stage 21: I Think & Opinions

## Pattern Name: Expressing What You Think

---

## The Pattern

**aham manye** — "I think"
**mama matena** — "in my opinion"
**mama dṛṣṭyā** — "from my perspective"
**nūnam** — "surely / certainly"
**sambhavati** — "it's possible"

---

## How It Works

Sanskrit has a rich toolkit for marking how sure you are about what you're saying. At the confident end, *nūnam* ("surely") and *niścayena* ("certainly") plant your flag firmly. In the middle, *mama matena* ("in my opinion," literally "by my thought") and *mama dṛṣṭyā* ("from my view") soften a claim into a personal take. And *sambhavati* ("it is possible") or *syāt* ("it might be") let you hedge gracefully.

The verb *manye* ("I think") is special -- it's an ātmanepada form (middle voice), so it conjugates differently from most verbs you've seen. You don't say *manāmi*; you say *manye* (I think), *manyase* (you think), *manyate* (he/she thinks). The instrumental case does the heavy lifting in *mama matena* -- *matena* is the instrumental of *mata* (thought/opinion), so you're literally saying "by my opinion." This is the same instrumental-of-means pattern you've seen before, now applied to abstract ideas.

## Example Phrases

1. **अहं मन्ये एतत् उचितम् अस्ति।**
   *ahaṃ manye etat ucitam asti.*
   I think this is appropriate.

2. **मम मतेन संस्कृतं सुन्दरतमा भाषा अस्ति।**
   *mama matena saṃskṛtaṃ sundaratamā bhāṣā asti.*
   In my opinion, Sanskrit is the most beautiful language.

3. **मम दृष्ट्या एतत् कार्यं कठिनम् अस्ति।**
   *mama dṛṣṭyā etat kāryaṃ kaṭhinam asti.*
   From my perspective, this task is difficult.

4. **नूनं सः आगमिष्यति।**
   *nūnaṃ saḥ āgamiṣyati.*
   Surely he will come.

5. **सम्भवति वर्षा भविष्यति।**
   *sambhavati varṣā bhaviṣyati.*
   It's possible it will rain.

6. **अहं न मन्ये तत् सत्यम् इति।**
   *ahaṃ na manye tat satyam iti.*
   I don't think that is true.

7. **तव मतेन किम् उत्तमम्?**
   *tava matena kim uttamam?*
   In your opinion, what is best?

8. **निश्चयेन सा उत्तीर्णा भविष्यति।**
   *niścayena sā uttīrṇā bhaviṣyati.*
   Certainly she will pass.

9. **स्यात् सः गृहे अस्ति।**
   *syāt saḥ gṛhe asti.*
   He might be at home.

10. **सत्यम् एव — अहम् अपि एवम् एव मन्ये।**
    *satyam eva — aham api evam eva manye.*
    Indeed -- I also think exactly so.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Agreeing and disagreeing**

-- मम मतेन अयं चलचित्रः उत्तमः अस्ति।
   *mama matena ayaṃ calacitraḥ uttamaḥ asti.*
   In my opinion, this movie is excellent.

-- अहं न एवं मन्ये। मम दृष्ट्या सः मन्दः आसीत्।
   *ahaṃ na evaṃ manye. mama dṛṣṭyā saḥ mandaḥ āsīt.*
   I don't think so. From my perspective, it was slow.

-- सम्भवति, किन्तु कथा तु सुन्दरा आसीत्, नूनम्।
   *sambhavati, kintu kathā tu sundarā āsīt, nūnam.*
   Possibly, but the story was beautiful, surely.

-- आम्, तत् तु अहम् अङ्गीकरोमि।
   *ām, tat tu aham aṅgīkaromi.*
   Yes, that I do accept.

**Dialogue 2: Making plans**

-- श्वः किं करिष्यामः? तव मतेन कुत्र गच्छावः?
   *śvaḥ kiṃ kariṣyāmaḥ? tava matena kutra gacchāvaḥ?*
   What shall we do tomorrow? In your opinion, where should we go?

-- मम मतेन उद्यानं गच्छावः।
   *mama matena udyānaṃ gacchāvaḥ.*
   In my opinion, let's go to the park.

-- सम्भवति वर्षा भविष्यति। स्यात् गृहे एव तिष्ठावः।
   *sambhavati varṣā bhaviṣyati. syāt gṛhe eva tiṣṭhāvaḥ.*
   It's possible it will rain. Perhaps we should just stay home.

---

## Variations

1. **Polite disagreement with kṣamyatām, kintu...:**
   *kṣamyatām, kintu mama matena etat na ucitam* — "Forgive me, but in my opinion this is not right."

2. **Strengthening with eva:**
   *nūnam eva saḥ āgamiṣyati* — "He will most certainly come."

3. **Asking for opinion with kim manyase:**
   *tvam kim manyase?* — "What do you think?"

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Saying manāmi instead of manye:** The verb *man* (to think) is ātmanepada. The correct first person is *manye*, not *manāmi*. This is one of those verbs where the ending just has to be memorized.

2. **Overusing nūnam for hedging:** *Nūnam* means "surely/certainly" -- it strengthens a claim. If you want to hedge ("maybe"), use *sambhavati* or *syāt* instead. Saying *nūnam sambhavati* ("certainly it's possible") sends mixed signals.
