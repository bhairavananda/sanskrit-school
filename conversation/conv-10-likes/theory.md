# Stage 10: Likes & Preferences

## The Pattern

**mama X rocate** — "I like X" (literally: "X pleases me")

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| मम X रोचते | mama X rocate | I like X |
| मम X प्रियम् | mama X priyam | X is dear to me |
| मम X न रोचते | mama X na rocate | I don't like X |
| तव किं रोचते? | tava kiṃ rocate? | What do you like? |

---

## How It Works

Sanskrit flips the "like" construction compared to English. You don't "like" something — rather, something "pleases" you. So "I like fruit" is literally "of-me fruit pleases" — **mama phalaṃ rocate**. The subject is the thing liked, not the person. This is why *rocate* stays the same regardless of who's doing the liking — it agrees with the thing, not you.

For a simpler alternative, use **priyam** (dear/beloved): **mama phalaṃ priyam** = "fruit is dear to me" = "I like fruit." This is an easy, elegant way to express preference. To negate, just drop in **na**: **mama phalaṃ na rocate** = "I don't like fruit."

## Example Phrases

1. मम फलं रोचते।
   mama phalaṃ rocate.
   *I like fruit.*

2. तव किं रोचते?
   tava kiṃ rocate?
   *What do you like?*

3. मम संगीतं प्रियम्।
   mama saṃgītaṃ priyam.
   *Music is dear to me. / I love music.*

4. मम दुग्धं न रोचते।
   mama dugdhaṃ na rocate.
   *I don't like milk.*

5. तस्य क्रीडा रोचते।
   tasya krīḍā rocate.
   *He likes sports/games.*

6. तस्याः पुष्पाणि प्रियाणि।
   tasyāḥ puṣpāṇi priyāṇi.
   *She likes flowers. (Flowers are dear to her.)*

7. मम चायः अत्यन्तं रोचते।
   mama cāyaḥ atyantaṃ rocate.
   *I like tea very much.*

8. तव कः आहारः रोचते?
   tava kaḥ āhāraḥ rocate?
   *What food do you like?*

9. मम एतत् नगरं न रोचते।
   mama etat nagaraṃ na rocate.
   *I don't like this city.*

10. मम वर्षाकालः प्रियः।
    mama varṣākālaḥ priyaḥ.
    *I like the rainy season. (The rainy season is dear to me.)*

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **अ:** तव किं रोचते — चायः वा काफी वा?
> A: tava kiṃ rocate — cāyaḥ vā kāphī vā?
> *What do you like — tea or coffee?*

> **ब:** मम चायः रोचते। तव?
> B: mama cāyaḥ rocate. tava?
> *I like tea. You?*

> **अ:** मम काफी प्रिया। चायः न रोचते।
> A: mama kāphī priyā. cāyaḥ na rocate.
> *Coffee is dear to me. I don't like tea.*

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **अ:** तव एतत् भोजनं रोचते वा?
> A: tava etat bhojanaṃ rocate vā?
> *Do you like this food?*

> **ब:** आम्, अत्यन्तं रोचते! एतत् मिष्टान्नं प्रियम्।
> B: ām, atyantaṃ rocate! etat miṣṭānnaṃ priyam.
> *Yes, I like it very much! This dessert is dear to me.*

> **अ:** मम अपि! एतत् उत्तमम्।
> A: mama api! etat uttamam.
> *Mine too! This is excellent.*

## Variations

1. **mama X iṣṭam** — Another way to say "I like X." *iṣṭam* means "desired/wished for." mama phalam iṣṭam = "fruit is desired by me" = "I like fruit."

2. **mama X atīva rocate** — "I really like X." The word **atīva** or **atyantam** intensifies the liking. mama saṃgītam atīva rocate = "I really like music."

3. **mama X na priyam, Y priyam** — Contrasting preferences: "I don't like X, I like Y." mama dugdhaṃ na priyam, cāyaḥ priyaḥ = "I don't like milk, I like tea."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **The subject trap:** In *mama phalaṃ rocate*, the subject is *phalam* (fruit), not "I." So if you like multiple things, the verb changes: *mama phalāni rocante* (I like fruits — plural verb). The verb agrees with the thing liked.

2. **priyam must match gender:** When using *priyam*, it must agree with the noun. Masculine: *priyaḥ* (mama cāyaḥ priyaḥ). Feminine: *priyā* (mama kāphī priyā). Neuter: *priyam* (mama phalaṃ priyam). If in doubt, use *rocate* instead — it's simpler.
