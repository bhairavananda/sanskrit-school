# Stage 19: Comparing

## Pattern Name: "X is better / X is like Y"

---

## The Pattern

**X Y-āt uttamam asti** — "X is better than Y"
**X Y-iva asti** — "X is like Y"
**X Y-sadṛśam** — "X is similar to Y"
**sarvottamam** — "best of all"

---

## How It Works

When you want to say "better than," Sanskrit uses the ablative case on the thing you're comparing against. So "than Y" becomes *Y-āt* (for masculine/neuter -a stems). The word *uttama* (best/better) or *śreṣṭha* (excellent) sits next to the thing you're praising. For "like," you just drop *iva* right after the thing you're comparing to -- it works like a postposition. And *sadṛśam* ("similar to") takes the thing compared in the instrumental or genitive.

For superlatives ("the best of all"), you use *sarvottama* or *sarveṣu śreṣṭha*. The comparative suffix *-tara* means "more" and *-tama* means "most." So *madhura* (sweet) becomes *madhuratara* (sweeter) and *madhuratama* (sweetest). Simple lego: snap -tara or -tama onto the adjective.

## Example Phrases

1. **एतत् भोजनं तस्मात् भोजनात् उत्तमम् अस्ति।**
   *etat bhojanaṃ tasmāt bhojanāt uttamam asti.*
   This food is better than that food.

2. **चायं कॉफ़ीतः मधुरतरम् अस्ति।**
   *cāyaṃ kŏphītaḥ madhurataram asti.*
   Tea is sweeter than coffee.

3. **तव गृहं मम गृहात् विशालतरम् अस्ति।**
   *tava gṛhaṃ mama gṛhāt viśālataram asti.*
   Your house is bigger than my house.

4. **सा तस्याः मातुः इव सुन्दरा अस्ति।**
   *sā tasyāḥ mātuḥ iva sundarā asti.*
   She is beautiful like her mother.

5. **अयं नगरं स्वर्गसदृशम् अस्ति।**
   *ayaṃ nagaraṃ svargasadṛśam asti.*
   This city is like heaven.

6. **सर्वेषु फलेषु आम्रं सर्वोत्तमम् अस्ति।**
   *sarveṣu phaleṣu āmraṃ sarvottamam asti.*
   Among all fruits, mango is the best.

7. **संस्कृतं ग्रीकभाषातः प्राचीनतरम् अस्ति।**
   *saṃskṛtaṃ grīkabhāṣātaḥ prācīnataram asti.*
   Sanskrit is more ancient than Greek.

8. **अद्य हिमालयः मेघैः इव शोभते।**
   *adya himālayaḥ meghaiḥ iva śobhate.*
   Today the Himalayas look like clouds.

9. **एषः छात्रः सर्वेषु छात्रेषु बुद्धिमत्तमः अस्ति।**
   *eṣaḥ chātraḥ sarveṣu chātreṣu buddhimattamaḥ asti.*
   This student is the most intelligent among all students.

10. **पादचारः वाहनात् स्वास्थ्यकरतरः अस्ति।**
    *pādacāraḥ vāhanāt svāsthyakarataraḥ asti.*
    Walking is healthier than driving.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: At a restaurant**

-- किम् इच्छसि? पनीरं वा दालम्?
   *kim icchasi? panīraṃ vā dālam?*
   What do you want? Paneer or dal?

-- दालं पनीरात् लघुतरम् अस्ति। दालम् इच्छामि।
   *dālaṃ panīrāt laghutaram asti. dālam icchāmi.*
   Dal is lighter than paneer. I want dal.

-- आम्, किन्तु अत्र पनीरं सर्वोत्तमम् अस्ति!
   *ām, kintu atra panīraṃ sarvottamam asti!*
   Yes, but here the paneer is the best!

**Dialogue 2: Comparing cities**

-- वाराणसी कीदृशी अस्ति?
   *vārāṇasī kīdṛśī asti?*
   What is Varanasi like?

-- वाराणसी स्वप्नसदृशी अस्ति। सा दिल्लीतः शान्ततरा अस्ति।
   *vārāṇasī svapnasadṛśī asti. sā dillītaḥ śāntatarā asti.*
   Varanasi is like a dream. It is more peaceful than Delhi.

-- सा मम प्रियतमा नगरी अस्ति।
   *sā mama priyatamā nagarī asti.*
   It is my most favorite city.

## Variations

1. **Using śreṣṭha instead of uttama:**
   *etat pustakam anyebhyaḥ śreṣṭham asti* — "This book is better than the others."

2. **Double comparison with api (even):**
   *saḥ tvaттaḥ api uccataraḥ asti* — "He is even taller than you."

3. **Negative comparison with na tādṛśam:**
   *etat tat na tādṛśam asti* — "This is not like that."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Forgetting the ablative:** Learners often say *rāmaḥ śyāma uttamaḥ* instead of *rāmaḥ śyāmāt uttamaḥ*. The "-āt" ending on the compared thing is non-negotiable -- without it, you're just listing two people, not comparing them.

2. **Mixing up -tara and -tama:** Remember *-tara* is "more" (comparing two) and *-tama* is "most" (comparing many). Saying *sarvottaraḥ* when you mean "best of all" is a common slip -- you want *sarvottamaḥ*.
