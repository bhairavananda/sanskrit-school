# Stage 31: Blessings, Promises & Vivid Past — The Three Missing Lakāras

## The Pattern

Sanskrit has ten verb-forms (lakāras). You already use six of them. This stage gives you the last three that matter conversationally:

| Lakāra | What It Does | The Feel |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Luṅ** (aorist) | Punctual past — "it just happened" | Vivid, immediate, dramatic |
| **Liṭ** (perfect) | Narrative past — "it had happened / it came to pass" | Literary, story-opening, weighty |
| **Luṭ** (periphrastic future) | Definite future — "I am going to do it" | Scheduled, committed, certain |

The tenth lakāra (Leṭ, Vedic subjunctive) is archaic — you'll encounter it in the main track's Svara-Vidyā stage but never need it in conversation.

We also cover the **āśīrliṅ** (benedictive) — a special sub-mode of Liṅ used exclusively for blessings and wishes. This is the verb-form behind every "may you prosper" you've ever heard in Sanskrit.

## How It Works

### Luṅ — The Aorist: "It Just Happened"

The aorist expresses a **punctual, completed action** — something that happened as a single event, often just now. Where Laṅ (imperfect) narrates ongoing past action, Luṅ captures a flash.

Formation: **a-** prefix + modified root + special endings. The aorist has multiple formation types, but you only need the most common forms.

High-frequency aorist forms:

| Root | Meaning | 3rd sg. | 3rd pl. |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| bhū (be/become) | became | **abhūt** | abhūvan |
| kṛ (do) | did | **akārṣīt** | akārṣuḥ |
| gam (go) | went | **agamat** | agaman |
| dā (give) | gave | **adāt** | aduḥ |
| dṛś (see) | saw | **adrākṣīt** | adrākṣuḥ |
| vac (speak) | spoke | **avocat** | avocan |
| śru (hear) | heard | **aśrauṣīt** | aśrauṣuḥ |
| pat (fall) | fell | **apātat** | apātan |

### Example Phrases — Luṅ

1. **सा अपतत्!**
   *sā apātat!*
   She fell! (just now — vivid)

2. **किम् अभूत्?**
   *kim abhūt?*
   What happened? (the single most useful aorist phrase)

3. **सः अवोचत् — "अहं न गमिष्यामि" इति।**
   *saḥ avocat — "ahaṃ na gamiṣyāmi" iti.*
   He said (just now) — "I will not go."

4. **मेघः अगर्जत्, विद्युत् अभूत्।**
   *meghaḥ agarjat, vidyut abhūt.*
   Thunder roared, lightning struck. (vivid narration)

5. **तत् श्रुत्वा सः अद्राक्षीत्।**
   *tat śrutvā saḥ adrākṣīt.*
   Having heard that, he saw (it clearly). (dramatic realization)

---

### Liṭ — The Perfect: "It Had Come to Pass"

The perfect expresses a **completed past action with present relevance**, or a **narrative past** in elevated/literary speech. It's the verb-form that opens stories and describes accomplished facts.

Formation: **reduplication** of the root's first syllable + special endings. The perfect has its own unique conjugation.

High-frequency perfect forms:

| Root | Meaning | 3rd sg. | 3rd pl. |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| bhū (be) | has been / became | **babhūva** | babhūvuḥ |
| kṛ (do) | has done | **cakāra** | cakruḥ |
| gam (go) | has gone | **jagāma** | jagmuḥ |
| vac (speak) | has spoken | **uvāca** | ūcuḥ |
| dṛś (see) | has seen | **dadarśa** | dadṛśuḥ |
| vid (know) | has known | **viveda** / **veda** | viduḥ |
| śru (hear) | has heard | **śuśrāva** | śuśruvuḥ |
| as (be) | has been | **āsa** | āsuḥ |

### Example Phrases — Liṭ

6. **पुरा अत्र एकः राजा बभूव।**
   *purā atra ekaḥ rājā babhūva.*
   Long ago, there was a king here. (story opening — weighty)

7. **सः उवाच — "धर्मः एव जयति" इति।**
   *saḥ uvāca — "dharmaḥ eva jayati" iti.*
   He spoke — "Dharma alone prevails." (quotation in elevated register)

8. **सा तं ददर्श, सः तां ददर्श।**
   *sā taṃ dadarśa, saḥ tāṃ dadarśa.*
   She saw him, he saw her. (literary — mutual recognition)

9. **किं त्वम् इदं शुश्राव?**
   *kiṃ tvam idaṃ śuśrāva?*
   Have you heard this? (present relevance of past event)

10. **भगवान् उवाच — ...**
    *bhagavān uvāca — ...*
    The Lord spoke — ... (the Gītā's framing verb — this IS the perfect)

### Luṭ — The Periphrastic Future: "I'm Going To"

The periphrastic future expresses a **definite, scheduled, or committed future action**. Where Lṛṭ (simple future, kariṣyāmi) is general intention, Luṭ is firm commitment — "this WILL happen."

Formation: agent noun in **-tā** + present tense of **as** (to be).

| Person | Singular | Meaning |
|--------|----------|---------|
| 1st | **kartā asmi** | I am going to do (it's decided) |
| 2nd | **kartā asi** | You are going to do |
| 3rd | **kartā** (asti dropped) | He is going to do |

| Root | -tā form | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| kṛ (do) | kartā | kartā asmi = I'm going to do it |
| gam (go) | gantā | gantā asmi = I'm going to go |
| dā (give) | dātā | dātā asmi = I'm going to give |
| vac (speak) | vaktā | vaktā asmi = I'm going to speak |
| bhū (become) | bhavitā | bhavitā = it's going to happen |

### Example Phrases — Luṭ

11. **अहं श्वः गन्ता अस्मि।**
    *ahaṃ śvaḥ gantā asmi.*
    I am going to go tomorrow. (it's decided, not just a wish)

12. **सः वक्ता — अहं श्रोता।**
    *saḥ vaktā — ahaṃ śrotā.*
    He will be the speaker — I will be the listener. (roles assigned)

13. **एतत् भवितैव।**
    *etat bhavitaiva.* (bhavitā + eva)
    This is going to happen, period.

14. **त्वं कर्ता असि वा न?**
    *tvaṃ kartā asi vā na?*
    Are you going to do it or not? (pressing for commitment)

### Āśīrliṅ — The Benedictive: "May It Be So"

The āśīrliṅ is a special sub-form of the optative (Liṅ) used exclusively for **blessings, wishes, and benedictions**. It's the verb behind temple blessings, guru-blessings, and auspicious wishes.

Formation: root + **-yā-** + special endings (different from regular Liṅ).

Key benedictive forms:

| Root | Benedictive 3rd sg. | Meaning |
|------|---------------------|---------|
| bhū | **bhūyāt** | May it be! |
| kṛ | **kriyāt** | May it be done! |
| as | **syāt** (same as Liṅ) | May it be! |
| jīv | **jīvyāt** | May he live! |
| vṛdh | **vardhiṣīṣṭa** | May he prosper! |

### Example Phrases — Āśīrliṅ & Blessings

15. **भद्रं भूयात्!**
    *bhadraṃ bhūyāt!*
    May there be good! (universal blessing)

16. **तव मङ्गलं भूयात्!**
    *tava maṅgalaṃ bhūyāt!*
    May there be auspiciousness for you!

17. **चिरं जीव! शतं जीव!**
    *ciraṃ jīva! śataṃ jīva!*
    Live long! Live a hundred (years)! (imperative used as blessing)

18. **विद्या भूयात्, अविद्या नश्यतु!**
    *vidyā bhūyāt, avidyā naśyatu!*
    May knowledge arise, may ignorance perish!

19. **सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः!**
    *sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ!*
    May all beings be happy! (Loṭ used as blessing — the famous prayer)

20. **शुभम् अस्तु!**
    *śubham astu!*
    May it be auspicious! (astu = Loṭ of as, the everyday blessing)

## Mini-Dialogue 1: "What Happened?"

> **A:** किम् अभूत्? त्वं चिन्तितः इव भासि।
> *kim abhūt? tvaṃ cintitaḥ iva bhāsi.*
> What happened? You look worried.

> **B:** गुरुः उवाच — "परीक्षा श्वः भवितैव" इति।
> *guruḥ uvāca — "parīkṣā śvaḥ bhavitaiva" iti.*
> The guru said — "The exam is going to be tomorrow, for sure."

> **A:** अहो! अहं तु न शुश्राव। अधुना अध्येता अस्मि।
> *aho! ahaṃ tu na śuśrāva. adhunā adhyetā asmi.*
> Oh! I hadn't heard. Now I'm going to study.

> **B:** भद्रं भूयात्! सफलतां प्राप्नुयाः!
> *bhadraṃ bhūyāt! saphalatāṃ prāpnuyāḥ!*
> May there be good! May you attain success!

## Mini-Dialogue 2: "A Story Opening"

> **A:** एकां कथां शृणु। पुरा एकः मुनिः बभूव।
> *ekāṃ kathāṃ śṛṇu. purā ekaḥ muniḥ babhūva.*
> Listen to a story. Long ago, there was a sage.

> **B:** सः किं चकार?
> *saḥ kiṃ cakāra?*
> What did he do?

> **A:** सः वनं जगाम, तत्र एकं सर्पम् अद्राक्षीत्।
> *saḥ vanaṃ jagāma, tatra ekaṃ sarpam adrākṣīt.*
> He went to the forest, and there he saw a serpent.

> **B:** ततः किम् अभूत्?
> *tataḥ kim abhūt?*
> Then what happened?

## Variations

- **Luṅ vs Laṅ** — In conversation, Laṅ (imperfect) handles most past tense. Use Luṅ when you want **vividness** or **surprise**: "kim abhūt?" (what just happened?) vs "kim abhavat?" (what was happening?)
- **Liṭ as quotation frame** — "uvāca" is far more common than "avadat" in literary/devotional contexts. If you're quoting a guru, a text, or a deity, use uvāca.
- **Luṭ vs Lṛṭ** — "kartā asmi" (I'm going to do it — decided) vs "kariṣyāmi" (I will do it — general). The distinction is like English "I'm going to" vs "I will."
- **Everyday blessings** — Most daily blessings use Loṭ (imperative), not āśīrliṅ: "śubham astu" (may it be good), "sukhinaḥ bhavantu" (may they be happy). Reserve bhūyāt for formal/ritual blessings.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Overusing Liṭ in conversation.** The perfect is literary. Don't say "ahaṃ jagāma" in casual speech — say "ahaṃ agaccham" (Laṅ) or "ahaṃ agamat" (Luṅ for vividness). Save Liṭ for storytelling and elevated register.

2. **Confusing Luṭ agent noun with regular nouns.** "kartā" in Luṭ means "one who is going to do" — it's a verbal form, not just "doer." Don't use it as a standalone noun meaning "creator" in this context.
