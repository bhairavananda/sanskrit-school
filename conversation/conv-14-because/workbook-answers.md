# Stage 14: Because & Therefore — Workbook Answers

## Section A: Translate to Sanskrit

1. yataḥ śītalaṃ bhavati tataḥ ahaṃ uṣṇāni vastrāṇi dhārayāmi.
2. saḥ śrāntaḥ, ataḥ svapiti.
3. kṣudhā-kāraṇāt bālaḥ roditi.
4. jñānaṃ hi mahattamaṃ dhanam.
5. yataḥ tvaṃ paṭhitavān tataḥ utīrṇaḥ jātaḥ.

---

## Section B: Translate to English

1. Because the sun sets, therefore darkness comes.
2. Because of the lack of water, the trees dry up.
3. She is courageous, therefore she does not fear.
4. For life is impermanent.
5. Because you speak the truth, therefore I trust (you).

## Section D: Sample Mini-Dialogues

**1. Why learn Sanskrit?**

— tvaṃ kimarthaṃ saṃskṛtaṃ paṭhasi?
  Why are you studying Sanskrit?

— yataḥ saṃskṛtam atīva sundaram, tataḥ ahaṃ paṭhāmi. prācīnāni śāstrāṇi hi saṃskṛte santi.
  Because Sanskrit is very beautiful, therefore I study it. For the ancient texts are in Sanskrit.

— sādhu! aham api paṭhitum icchāmi.
  Good! I too want to study.

**2. Why is the shop closed?**

— eṣā āpaṇikā kimarthaṃ pidadhātī?
  Why is this shop closed?

— utsava-kāraṇāt āpaṇikā pidhānā. adya viśrāmadinam.
  Because of the festival, the shop is closed. Today is a rest day.

— ataḥ śvaḥ āgacchāmi.
  Therefore I will come tomorrow.

— ām, śvaḥ prātaḥ āpaṇikā bhaviṣyati.
  Yes, the shop will be open tomorrow morning.

## Section E: Sample Responses

**1. Favourite season:**

vasantaṛtuḥ mama priyaḥ. yataḥ puṣpāṇi vikasanti tataḥ sarvatra saundaryadarśanaṃ bhavati. śītala-kāraṇāt na kaṣṭaṃ bhavati, uṣṇa-kāraṇāt api na. vāyuḥ hi vasante sukhadaḥ bhavati. ataḥ vasantam ahaṃ prīṇāmi.

(Spring is my favourite. Because flowers bloom, therefore beauty is seen everywhere. There is no trouble from cold or heat. For the breeze in spring is pleasant. Therefore I love spring.)

**2. Reflection on *hi* placement:**

The rule that *hi* must come second (Wackernagel's position) reflects Sanskrit's broader tendency to place enclitic particles after the first stressed element of a clause. This creates a natural rhythm — the important word lands first and *hi* leans on it for support. It also prevents ambiguity about which clause *hi* belongs to, since it always attaches to what immediately precedes it.
