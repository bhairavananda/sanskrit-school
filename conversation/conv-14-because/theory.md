# Stage 14: Because & Therefore

## The Pattern

**yataḥ X tataḥ Y** — "because X, therefore Y"
**X-kāraṇāt Y** — "because of X, Y"
**ataḥ** — "therefore"
**hi** — "for / because" (enclitic)

You can now give *reasons*. This is the stage where your Sanskrit stops being a list of facts and starts building arguments.

---

## How It Works

Sanskrit has a beautiful correlative system: *yataḥ* ("since/because") in the first clause pairs with *tataḥ* ("therefore/so") in the second. Think of them as a matched set — the reason clause and the result clause, neatly linked.

For shorter explanations, you can skip the pair and just use **ataḥ** ("therefore") to introduce a conclusion. Or tack on **hi** ("for/because") as a lightweight enclitic — it always comes *second* in its clause, never first. There is also the **ablative of cause** using *-kāraṇāt*: you take a noun, add *kāraṇāt* ("from the reason of"), and you have an instant "because of X" phrase. For example, *varṣā-kāraṇāt* = "because of rain."

## Example Phrases

1. यतः वर्षा भवति ततः अहं गृहे तिष्ठामि।
   *yataḥ varṣā bhavati tataḥ ahaṃ gṛhe tiṣṭhāmi.*
   Because it is raining, therefore I stay at home.

2. रोगकारणात् सः विद्यालयं न गच्छति।
   *roga-kāraṇāt saḥ vidyālayaṃ na gacchati.*
   Because of illness, he does not go to school.

3. अतः वयं श्वः गमिष्यामः।
   *ataḥ vayaṃ śvaḥ gamiṣyāmaḥ.*
   Therefore we will go tomorrow.

4. सत्यं हि सदा जयति।
   *satyaṃ hi sadā jayati.*
   For truth always wins.

5. यतः त्वं मम मित्रम् असि ततः अहं साहाय्यं करोमि।
   *yataḥ tvaṃ mama mitram asi tataḥ ahaṃ sāhāyyaṃ karomi.*
   Because you are my friend, therefore I help.

6. उष्णकारणात् जलं पिबामि।
   *uṣṇa-kāraṇāt jalaṃ pibāmi.*
   Because of the heat, I drink water.

7. यतः सः परिश्रमं करोति ततः सफलः भवति।
   *yataḥ saḥ pariśramaṃ karoti tataḥ saphalaḥ bhavati.*
   Because he works hard, therefore he succeeds.

8. अलसः अस्ति, अतः न पठति।
   *alasaḥ asti, ataḥ na paṭhati.*
   He is lazy, therefore he does not study.

9. धर्मः हि मूलम्।
   *dharmaḥ hi mūlam.*
   For dharma is the root.

10. यतः मार्गः अवरुद्धः ततः वयम् अन्यमार्गेण गच्छामः।
    *yataḥ mārgaḥ avaruddhaḥ tataḥ vayam anyamārgeṇa gacchāmaḥ.*
    Because the road is blocked, therefore we go by another route.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Why aren't you coming?**

— किमर्थं त्वं न आगच्छसि?
  *kimarthaṃ tvaṃ na āgacchasi?*
  Why aren't you coming?

— यतः मम कार्यं न समाप्तम्, ततः गन्तुं न शक्नोमि।
  *yataḥ mama kāryaṃ na samāptam, tataḥ gantuṃ na śaknomi.*
  Because my work isn't finished, therefore I can't go.

— अतः श्वः मिलामः?
  *ataḥ śvaḥ milāmaḥ?*
  Then shall we meet tomorrow?

— आम्, श्वः अवश्यम्।
  *ām, śvaḥ avaśyam.*
  Yes, tomorrow for sure.

**Dialogue 2: Explaining a decision**

— भवान् किमर्थम् एतत् पुस्तकं क्रीतवान्?
  *bhavān kimartham etat pustakaṃ krītavān?*
  Why did you buy this book?

— एतत् हि उत्तमं पुस्तकम्। गुरुणा प्रशंसितम्।
  *etat hi uttamaṃ pustakam. guruṇā praśaṃsitam.*
  For this is an excellent book. Praised by the teacher.

— साधु, अतः अहम् अपि पठिष्यामि।
  *sādhu, ataḥ aham api paṭhiṣyāmi.*
  Good, therefore I too will read it.

## Variations

1. **yasmāt...tasmāt** — A more formal synonym pair for *yataḥ...tataḥ*, using ablative pronouns:
   *yasmāt saḥ dhārmikaḥ, tasmāt sukhaṃ labhate.* — Since he is righteous, therefore he gains happiness.

2. **tena** ("therefore/by that reason") — Another way to say "therefore," using the instrumental of *tat*:
   *sā paṇḍitā, tena sarvē tāṃ namanti.* — She is learned, therefore all bow to her.

3. **-hetoḥ** ("on account of") — Alternative to *-kāraṇāt*, using the genitive:
   *kāryahetoḥ āgataḥ.* — Came on account of work.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Putting *hi* first in a clause:** *Hi* is an enclitic — it must come second. Say *satyaṃ hi jayati*, never *hi satyaṃ jayati*. Think of it as a word that leans on the word before it.

2. **Mixing up *yataḥ* and *yatra*:** *Yataḥ* means "because/since." *Yatra* means "where." They sound similar but do completely different jobs. If you're giving a reason, reach for *yataḥ*.
