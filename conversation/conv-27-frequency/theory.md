# Stage 27: Always / Sometimes / Never

**Pattern: sadā · prāyaḥ · kadācit · viralam · na kadāpi · pratyaham · punaḥ punaḥ · ekadā**

---

## The Pattern

Frequency words — how often something happens:

> **sadā / sarvadā** (सदा / सर्वदा) = always
> **prāyaḥ** (प्रायः) = usually, mostly
> **kadācit** (कदाचित्) = sometimes
> **viralam** (विरलम्) = rarely
> **na kadāpi / na kadācit** (न कदापि / न कदाचित्) = never
> **pratyaham / pratidinam** (प्रत्यहम् / प्रतिदिनम्) = every day
> **punaḥ punaḥ** (पुनः पुनः) = again and again
> **ekadā** (एकदा) = once (as in "once upon a time" / "one time I...")

## How It Works

All of these are indeclinables — they never change form regardless of gender, number, or case. They typically sit before the verb, though they can open a sentence for emphasis. **sadā** and **sarvadā** are interchangeable. **prāyaḥ** is enormously useful — it lets you hedge, generalize, say "mostly" without committing to "always." **kadācit** by itself means "sometimes," but combined with **na** it becomes "never" — though **na kadāpi** is the stronger, more emphatic form of "never."

Think of these words as a spectrum: sadā → prāyaḥ → kadācit → viralam → na kadāpi. You can't talk about habits, routines, character, or patterns without them. Until now your Sanskrit has described single events. These words let you zoom out and generalize.

## Example Phrases

1. अहं सदा प्रातः उत्तिष्ठामि। (ahaṃ sadā prātaḥ uttiṣṭhāmi.)
   — "I always wake up early."

2. सा प्रायः समये आगच्छति। (sā prāyaḥ samaye āgacchati.)
   — "She usually comes on time."

3. अत्र कदाचित् वर्षा भवति। (atra kadācit varṣā bhavati.)
   — "Sometimes it rains here."

4. सः न कदापि असत्यं वदति। (saḥ na kadāpi asatyaṃ vadati.)
   — "He never lies."

5. अहं प्रत्यहं अभ्यासं करोमि। (ahaṃ pratyahaṃ abhyāsaṃ karomi.)
   — "I practice every day."

6. सः पुनः पुनः तां एव भूलं करोति। (saḥ punaḥ punaḥ tāṃ eva bhūlaṃ karoti.)
   — "He keeps making the same mistake again and again."

7. एकदा अहं काशीं अगच्छम्। (ekadā ahaṃ kāśīṃ agaccham.)
   — "Once I went to Kashi."

8. विरलं सः हसति। (viralaṃ saḥ hasati.)
   — "He rarely laughs."

9. मम पिता सर्वदा सत्यं वदति। (mama pitā sarvadā satyaṃ vadati.)
   — "My father always tells the truth."

10. बालकाः प्रायः क्रीडन्ति। (bālakāḥ prāyaḥ krīḍanti.)
    — "Children usually play."

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **शिष्यः** — गुरो, अहं कदाचित् ध्यानं विस्मरामि। (guro, ahaṃ kadācit dhyānaṃ vismarāmi.)
> "Teacher, I sometimes forget to meditate."
>
> **गुरुः** — प्रत्यहं कुरु। ततः न कदापि विस्मरिष्यसि। (pratyahaṃ kuru. tataḥ na kadāpi vismariṣyasi.)
> "Do it every day. Then you will never forget."
>
> **शिष्यः** — प्रयतिष्ये। किन्तु प्रायः आलस्यं मां बाधते। (prayatiṣye. kintu prāyaḥ ālasyaṃ māṃ bādhate.)
> "I'll try. But laziness usually troubles me."
>
> **गुरुः** — आलस्यं सदा बाधते। अभ्यासः एव औषधम्। (ālasyaṃ sadā bādhate. abhyāsaḥ eva auṣadham.)
> "Laziness always troubles. Practice itself is the medicine."

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **क:** एकदा अहं हिमालयं गतवान्। (ekadā ahaṃ himālayaṃ gatavān.)
> "Once I went to the Himalayas."
>
> **ख:** सत्यम्? त्वं पुनः पुनः तत्र गच्छसि किम्? (satyam? tvaṃ punaḥ punaḥ tatra gacchasi kim?)
> "Really? Do you go there again and again?"
>
> **क:** न, विरलम्। किन्तु सदा स्मरामि। (na, viralam. kintu sadā smarāmi.)
> "No, rarely. But I always remember it."
>
> **ख:** अहम् अपि प्रायः तत्र गन्तुम् इच्छामि। (aham api prāyaḥ tatra gantum icchāmi.)
> "I too usually want to go there."

## Variations

- **pratyaham** and **pratidinam** are interchangeable — both mean "every day." You can extend the pattern: **prativāram** (every week), **pratimāsam** (every month), **prativarṣam** (every year).
- **sadā** can combine with eva for extra force: **sadā eva** (always and forever). Similarly **na kadāpi eva** = absolutely never.
- **ekadā** is the storytelling word. It opens anecdotes and tales, just like "once" in English or "ek bār" in Hindi.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using na kadācit when you mean kadācit.** "Sometimes" and "never" are one negation apart. कदाचित् सः आगच्छति = "Sometimes he comes." न कदाचित् सः आगच्छति = "He never comes." Be careful with that न.

2. **Placing frequency words after the verb.** While not strictly wrong, the natural position is before the verb. सदा गच्छति sounds natural; गच्छति सदा sounds like an afterthought or poetic inversion.
