# Stage 7: Having & Existing

## The Pattern

**mama X asti** — "I have X"

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| मम X अस्ति | mama X asti | I have X |
| तव किम् अस्ति? | tava kim asti? | Do you have (something)? |
| अत्र X अस्ति | atra X asti | There is X here |
| न अस्ति | na asti | There isn't / I don't have |

---

## How It Works

Sanskrit doesn't have a verb for "have." Instead, you say something *belongs to* someone and *exists*. So "I have a book" becomes "Of-me a book exists" — **mama pustakam asti**. The "of-me" part uses what grammarians call the genitive case, but you already know these words: **mama** (my/of me), **tava** (your/of you), **tasya** (his/of him), **tasyāḥ** (her/of her).

To say something exists in a place, just swap the person for a location word: **atra** (here), **tatra** (there). So **atra jalam asti** = "there is water here." Negate with **na** before **asti**: **mama dhanaṃ na asti** = "I don't have money."

---

## Example Phrases

1. मम पुस्तकम् अस्ति।
   mama pustakam asti.
   *I have a book.*

2. तव गृहम् अस्ति वा?
   tava gṛham asti vā?
   *Do you have a house?*

3. अत्र जलम् अस्ति।
   atra jalam asti.
   *There is water here.*

4. तत्र फलानि सन्ति।
   tatra phalāni santi.
   *There are fruits there.*

5. मम समयः न अस्ति।
   mama samayaḥ na asti.
   *I don't have time.*

6. तस्य धनम् अस्ति।
   tasya dhanam asti.
   *He has money.*

7. तस्याः द्वे पुस्तके स्तः।
   tasyāḥ dve pustake staḥ.
   *She has two books.*

8. अत्र किम् अस्ति?
   atra kim asti?
   *What is here?*

9. मम मित्रम् अत्र अस्ति।
   mama mitram atra asti.
   *My friend is here.*

10. तव दुग्धम् अस्ति वा?
    tava dugdham asti vā?
    *Do you have milk?*

---

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **अ:** तव जलम् अस्ति वा?
> A: tava jalam asti vā?
> *Do you have water?*

> **ब:** आम्, मम जलम् अस्ति। तव?
> B: ām, mama jalam asti. tava?
> *Yes, I have water. You?*

> **अ:** मम जलम् न अस्ति।
> A: mama jalam na asti.
> *I don't have water.*

> **ब:** एतत् गृहाण!
> B: etat gṛhāṇa!
> *Take this!*

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **अ:** अत्र किम् अस्ति?
> A: atra kim asti?
> *What is here?*

> **ब:** अत्र फलानि सन्ति, दुग्धम् अस्ति च।
> B: atra phalāni santi, dugdham asti ca.
> *There are fruits and there is milk here.*

> **अ:** उत्तमम्! मम क्षुधा अस्ति।
> A: uttamam! mama kṣudhā asti.
> *Excellent! I'm hungry. (lit. I have hunger.)*

---

## Variations

1. **mama X nāsti** — Contracted form of *na asti*. Very common in speech: मम समयः नास्ति (mama samayaḥ nāsti) — "I don't have time."

2. **mama X asti, tava tu na asti** — Contrasting possession: "I have X, but you don't." The word **tu** means "but."

3. **kasya X asti?** — "Who has X?" Uses the genitive interrogative *kasya* (of whom).

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Word order trap:** Don't put *asti* first. While Sanskrit word order is flexible, *mama pustakam asti* sounds natural; *asti mama pustakam* sounds like a Vedic chant. Keep *asti* at the end.

2. **Forgetting santi for plurals:** When the thing you "have" is plural, *asti* becomes **santi**. One book = *pustakam asti*. Many books = *pustakāni santi*.
