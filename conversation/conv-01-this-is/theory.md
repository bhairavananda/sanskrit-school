# Stage 1: This Is / That Is

**Pattern Name:** etat ... asti / tat ... asti

---

## The Pattern

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| एतत् X अस्ति | etat X asti | This is X |
| तत् Y अस्ति | tat Y asti | That is Y |
| एतत् किम् अस्ति? | etat kim asti? | What is this? |

Point at something near you — **etat**. Point at something far away — **tat**. Slap **asti** ("is") at the end. Done.

---

## How It Works

Sanskrit has a simple copula verb **asti** (= "is"). To say "this is a book," you point with **etat** ("this"), name the thing (**pustakam**, "book"), and close with **asti**. For "that," swap in **tat**. To ask "what is this?", just drop **kim** ("what") where the noun would go: **etat kim asti?**

The thing you're pointing at sits in the nominative case — which for most neuter nouns just means the dictionary form. No extra endings to worry about yet.

## Example Phrases

1. एतत् पुस्तकम् अस्ति।
   etat pustakam asti.
   "This is a book."

2. तत् गृहम् अस्ति।
   tat gṛham asti.
   "That is a house."

3. एतत् जलम् अस्ति।
   etat jalam asti.
   "This is water."

4. तत् फलम् अस्ति।
   tat phalam asti.
   "That is a fruit."

5. एतत् किम् अस्ति?
   etat kim asti?
   "What is this?"

6. तत् पुष्पम् अस्ति।
   tat puṣpam asti.
   "That is a flower."

7. एतत् दुग्धम् अस्ति।
   etat dugdham asti.
   "This is milk."

8. तत् वृक्षः अस्ति।
   tat vṛkṣaḥ asti.
   "That is a tree."

9. एतत् यानम् अस्ति।
   etat yānam asti.
   "This is a vehicle."

10. तत् विद्यालयः अस्ति।
    tat vidyālayaḥ asti.
    "That is a school."

## Mini-Dialogue 1

**रामः (rāmaḥ):** एतत् किम् अस्ति?
etat kim asti?
"What is this?"

**सीता (sītā):** एतत् पुस्तकम् अस्ति।
etat pustakam asti.
"This is a book."

**रामः:** तत् किम् अस्ति?
tat kim asti?
"What is that?"

**सीता:** तत् कलमः अस्ति।
tat kalamaḥ asti.
"That is a pen."

## Mini-Dialogue 2

**बालकः (bālakaḥ):** तत् किम् अस्ति?
tat kim asti?
"What is that?"

**माता (mātā):** तत् चन्द्रः अस्ति।
tat candraḥ asti.
"That is the moon."

**बालकः:** एतत् किम् अस्ति?
etat kim asti?
"What is this?"

**माता:** एतत् दीपः अस्ति।
etat dīpaḥ asti.
"This is a lamp."

---

## Variations

1. **Dropping asti:** In casual speech, you can just say **etat pustakam** — the "is" is understood. Like pointing and saying "This — a book."

2. **Gender forms of etat/tat:** When pointing at a masculine or feminine noun, the demonstrative changes form: **eṣaḥ** (this, masc.), **eṣā** (this, fem.), **saḥ** (that, masc.), **sā** (that, fem.). For now, **etat/tat** (neuter) covers most objects.

3. **na asti (is not):** To negate, slip **na** before **asti**: **etat jalam na asti** — "This is not water."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Forgetting asti at the end.** While dropping it is fine in casual speech, keep it in while you're learning — it anchors the sentence structure.

2. **Mixing up etat and tat.** Think of it physically: **etat** = close enough to touch, **tat** = over there. If you can point with your finger and almost poke it, use **etat**.
