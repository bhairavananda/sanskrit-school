# Stage 16: But & Although — Workbook Answers

---

## Section A: Translate to Sanskrit

1. nagaram mahat kintu kolāhalapūrṇam.
2. yadyapi sā yuvatiḥ tathāpi atīva buddhimatī.
3. ahaṃ khāditum icchāmi parantu kṣudhā nāsti.
4. saḥ saṃskṛtaṃ vadati kintu na likhati.
5. yadyapi parīkṣā kaṭhinā āsīt tathāpi sarve chātrāḥ utīrṇāḥ abhavan.

---

## Section B: Translate to English

1. The village is peaceful but far from the city.
2. Although she is a newcomer, nevertheless she knows everyone.
3. I am sorry, however I am not able to help.
4. Although it is night, nevertheless the stars shine.
5. The food is enough, but there is no water.

## Section D: Sample Mini-Dialogues

**1. Film debate:**

— mama matē citrapaṭaḥ sundaraḥ āsīt, kintu antyaḥ bhāgaḥ durbalaḥ.
  In my opinion the film was beautiful, but the ending was weak.

— ahaṃ na rocitavān. kathā mandā āsīt, parantu saṅgītam uttamam āsīt.
  I didn't like it. The story was slow, however the music was excellent.

— sādhu! saṅgītaṃ sundaraṃ kintu kathā api āvaśyakī, nanu?
  True! The music is beautiful, but the story is also necessary, right?

— āṃ, dvayam api āvaśyakam.
  Yes, both are necessary.

**2. Studying a hard subject:**

— kimarthaṃ tvaṃ vyākaraṇam adhīṣe? kaṭhinaṃ nanu?
  Why do you study grammar? Isn't it difficult?

— āṃ, yadyapi vyākaraṇaṃ kaṭhinaṃ tathāpi atīva ānanddāyakam.
  Yes, although grammar is difficult, it is nevertheless very enjoyable.

— kintu parikṣā api kaṭhinā bhavati!
  But the exam is also hard!

— sā tu kaṭhinā, parantu yadyapi kaṣṭena paṭhāmi tathāpi jñānasya ānandaḥ mahān.
  That is indeed hard, but although I study with difficulty, the joy of knowledge is great.

## Section E: Sample Responses

**1. Something both good and bad (example: summer):**

grīṣmaṛtuḥ sundaraḥ kintu atīva uṣṇaḥ. dīrghāḥ divāḥ bhavanti — tat mama rocate. parantu rātrau api uṣṇatā na kṣīyate. yadyapi grīṣme phalāni bahūni labhyante tathāpi uṣṇa-kāraṇāt bahiḥ gantuṃ na śaknomi.

(Summer is beautiful but very hot. The days are long — I like that. However, even at night the heat doesn't subside. Although many fruits are available in summer, I can't go out because of the heat.)

**2. Kintu vs. yadyapi...tathāpi:**

*Kintu* is a simple pivot — it flips from one statement to another, like "but" in English. It's quick and lightweight. *Yadyapi...tathāpi* is heavier and more dramatic — it first acknowledges a concession ("although this is true") and then deliberately overrides it ("nevertheless"). You choose *yadyapi...tathāpi* when you want the listener to feel the weight of both sides, and *kintu* when you just need a quick contrast.
