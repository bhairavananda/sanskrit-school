# Stage 16: But & Although — Workbook Questions

**Total: 40 marks**

---

## Section A: Translate to Sanskrit (10 marks, 2 each)

Use kintu, parantu, or yadyapi...tathāpi as appropriate.

1. The city is big, but noisy.
2. Although she is young, she is nevertheless very wise.
3. I want to eat, however I am not hungry.
4. He speaks Sanskrit, but does not write it.
5. Although the exam was difficult, nevertheless all students passed.

---

## Section B: Translate to English (10 marks, 2 each)

1. grāmaḥ śāntaḥ kintu nagarāt dūraḥ.
2. yadyapi sā navāgataḥ tathāpi sarvān jānāti.
3. ahaṃ khedāmi parantu sahāyyaṃ kartuṃ na śaknomi.
4. yadyapi rātriḥ tathāpi tārakāḥ prakāśante.
5. bhojanaṃ paryāptaṃ kintu jalaṃ nāsti.

---

## Section C: Fill in the Blank (10 marks, 2 each)

1. saḥ medhāvī _______ alasaḥ. (He is intelligent but lazy.)
2. _______ mārgaḥ dīrghaḥ _______ vayaṃ calāmaḥ eva. (Although the path is long, we still walk.)
3. ahaṃ tvaṃ draṣṭum āgatavān _______ tvaṃ gṛhe nāsīḥ. (I came to see you, however you were not at home.)
4. _______ vṛddhaḥ _______ dhāvituṃ śaknoti. (Although old, he can still run.)
5. phalaṃ madhuraṃ _______ mahārgham. (The fruit is sweet but expensive.)

---

## Section D: Build a Mini-Dialogue (5 marks, 2.5 each)

Write a 3-4 line dialogue in Sanskrit (IAST is fine) for each situation.

1. **Situation:** Two people argue about a film — one liked it (but admits a flaw), the other didn't like it (but admits a strength). Use kintu or parantu.

2. **Situation:** A student explains to a friend why they keep studying a hard subject. Use yadyapi...tathāpi at least once.

---

## Section E: Free Response (5 marks, 2.5 each)

1. Describe something you find both good and bad — food, weather, a place, a habit. Write 3-4 Sanskrit sentences using kintu/parantu and yadyapi...tathāpi.

2. What is the difference in feeling between *kintu* and *yadyapi...tathāpi*? When would you choose one over the other? Write 2-3 sentences — English or Sanskrit.
