# Stage 16: But & Although

## The Pattern

**X kintu Y** — "X, but Y"
**X parantu Y** — "X, however Y"
**yadyapi X tathāpi Y** — "although X, nevertheless Y"

You can now push back, qualify, and add nuance. No more agreeing with everything — time to say "but."

---

## How It Works

**Kintu** and **parantu** both mean "but/however" and work almost identically. They sit between two clauses and flip the direction. *Kintu* is slightly more common in conversational Sanskrit; *parantu* feels a shade more formal. Both are indeclinables — they never change form.

The power move is **yadyapi...tathāpi** ("although...nevertheless"). This is another correlative pair: *yadyapi* (literally *yadi + api* = "even if") opens the concession, and *tathāpi* (*tathā + api* = "even so") delivers the counter-punch. Use this when you want to acknowledge something and then push past it: "Although it rained, we still went."

You can also use **api** alone to mean "even/also" — it's the building block inside both *yadyapi* and *tathāpi*.

## Example Phrases

1. सः धनवान् किन्तु सुखी नास्ति।
   *saḥ dhanavān kintu sukhī nāsti.*
   He is rich, but not happy.

2. अहं गन्तुम् इच्छामि परन्तु समयः नास्ति।
   *ahaṃ gantum icchāmi parantu samayaḥ nāsti.*
   I want to go, however there is no time.

3. यद्यपि वर्षा भवति तथापि सः गच्छति।
   *yadyapi varṣā bhavati tathāpi saḥ gacchati.*
   Although it rains, nevertheless he goes.

4. भोजनं स्वादिष्टं किन्तु अतीव उष्णम्।
   *bhojanaṃ svādiṣṭaṃ kintu atīva uṣṇam.*
   The food is delicious, but very hot.

5. यद्यपि सा लघ्वी तथापि बलवती।
   *yadyapi sā laghvī tathāpi balavatī.*
   Although she is small, she is nevertheless strong.

6. मार्गः सुन्दरः परन्तु दीर्घः।
   *mārgaḥ sundaraḥ parantu dīrghaḥ.*
   The path is beautiful, however long.

7. यद्यपि अहं श्रान्तः तथापि कार्यं करोमि।
   *yadyapi ahaṃ śrāntaḥ tathāpi kāryaṃ karomi.*
   Although I am tired, I still do the work.

8. सा गायति, किन्तु नृत्यं न करोति।
   *sā gāyati, kintu nṛtyaṃ na karoti.*
   She sings, but does not dance.

9. यद्यपि गुरुः कठोरः तथापि शिष्याः तं प्रीणन्ति।
   *yadyapi guruḥ kaṭhoraḥ tathāpi śiṣyāḥ taṃ prīṇanti.*
   Although the teacher is strict, the students still love him.

10. पुस्तकं प्राचीनं किन्तु ज्ञानं नवीनम्।
    *pustakaṃ prācīnaṃ kintu jñānaṃ navīnam.*
    The book is old, but the knowledge is fresh.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Changing plans**

— अद्य वनं गच्छामः, किं भवति?
  *adya vanaṃ gacchāmaḥ, kiṃ bhavati?*
  Shall we go to the forest today?

— इच्छामि, किन्तु वर्षा आगच्छति इति भाति।
  *icchāmi, kintu varṣā āgacchati iti bhāti.*
  I'd like to, but it looks like rain is coming.

— यद्यपि वर्षा भवेत् तथापि वनं सुन्दरम् एव।
  *yadyapi varṣā bhavet tathāpi vanaṃ sundaram eva.*
  Even if it rains, the forest is still beautiful.

— सत्यम्! तर्हि गच्छामः।
  *satyam! tarhi gacchāmaḥ.*
  True! Then let's go.

**Dialogue 2: Acknowledging and countering**

— एतत् पुस्तकं कठिनम्।
  *etat pustakaṃ kaṭhinam.*
  This book is difficult.

— आम्, कठिनं परन्तु अतीव उपयोगि।
  *ām, kaṭhinaṃ parantu atīva upayogi.*
  Yes, difficult, however very useful.

— यद्यपि कठिनं तथापि पठनीयम् एव?
  *yadyapi kaṭhinaṃ tathāpi paṭhanīyam eva?*
  Although it's difficult, it should still be read?

— अवश्यम्! कठिनं किन्तु योग्यम्।
  *avaśyam! kaṭhinaṃ kintu yogyam.*
  Certainly! Difficult but worthwhile.

## Variations

1. **tu** — A lighter, more classical "but." Often found in verse and older prose. *Saḥ vadati, ahaṃ tu na śṛṇomi.* — He speaks, but I don't listen. Note: *tu* also sits in second position, like *hi* and *cet*.

2. **athāpi** ("even then / and yet") — Combines *atha* + *api* for a softer "nevertheless":
   *kṣudraḥ jīvaḥ, athāpi mahattvam asya.* — A tiny creature, and yet its greatness.

3. **tad api** ("even so") — Can replace *tathāpi* in less formal speech:
   *kaṣṭaṃ bhavati, tad api na tyajāmi.* — It's hard, even so I don't quit.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using *kintu* at the start of a sentence without a preceding clause:** *Kintu* links two ideas. You need something before it — even if it's implied from the previous speaker's line. Don't just open with *kintu* out of nowhere.

2. **Forgetting *tathāpi* after *yadyapi*:** The pair works together. If you say *yadyapi* and then just continue without *tathāpi*, the sentence feels unfinished, like saying "although..." in English and never delivering the "nevertheless." Always close the pair.
