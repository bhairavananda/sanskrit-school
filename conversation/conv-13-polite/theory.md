# Stage 13: Polite Phrases

## The Pattern

**kṛpayā / dhanyavādaḥ / kṣamyatām / svāgatam / śubhāśayāḥ / punaḥ milāmaḥ**

Every language has its social lubricant — the "please," "thank you," "sorry" that keep conversations smooth. Sanskrit has a rich set of these, and many of them reveal neat grammar hiding in plain sight.

---

## How It Works

Most of these phrases are **indeclinables** (avyaya) — words that never change form regardless of who is speaking or being addressed. *Kṛpayā* ("please," literally "by kindness") is frozen in the instrumental case. *Dhanyavādaḥ* ("thank you," literally "a statement of gratitude") is a compound noun. *Svāgatam* ("welcome," literally "well-arrived") is a past participle used as a greeting.

The interesting one is *kṣamyatām* ("please forgive / pardon"). This is a **passive imperative** — the verb *kṣam* ("to forgive") put into passive voice and then commanded. It literally means "let it be forgiven." You don't even need to say *who* should forgive — the construction handles it gracefully. Similarly, *punaḥ milāmaḥ* ("let us meet again") uses a first-person plural imperative — "let us meet."

---

## Example Phrases

1. कृपया उपविशतु।
   *kṛpayā upaviśatu.*
   Please sit down.

2. बहु धन्यवादाः।
   *bahu dhanyavādāḥ.*
   Thank you very much.

3. क्षम्यतां विलम्बाय।
   *kṣamyatāṃ vilambāya.*
   Sorry for the delay.

4. अस्माकं गृहे स्वागतम्।
   *asmākaṃ gṛhe svāgatam.*
   Welcome to our home.

5. नववर्षस्य शुभाशयाः!
   *navavarṣasya śubhāśayāḥ!*
   Good wishes for the new year!

6. पुनः मिलामः श्वः।
   *punaḥ milāmaḥ śvaḥ.*
   Let us meet again tomorrow.

7. कृपया मम सहायतां करोतु।
   *kṛpayā mama sahāyatāṃ karotu.*
   Please help me.

8. तव सहायतायै धन्यवादाः।
   *tava sahāyatāyai dhanyavādāḥ.*
   Thank you for your help.

9. क्षम्यतां, मार्गं ददातु।
   *kṣamyatāṃ, mārgaṃ dadātu.*
   Pardon me, please make way.

10. शुभं भवतु!
    *śubhaṃ bhavatu!*
    May it be auspicious! (a general good-wish)

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: At someone's home**

— स्वागतम्! कृपया उपविशतु।
  *svāgatam! kṛpayā upaviśatu.*
  Welcome! Please sit down.

— बहु धन्यवादाः। सुन्दरं गृहम्।
  *bahu dhanyavādāḥ. sundaraṃ gṛham.*
  Thank you very much. Beautiful home.

— कृपया जलं स्वीकरोतु।
  *kṛpayā jalaṃ svīkarotu.*
  Please accept some water.

— धन्यवादाः, अतीव दयालुः भवान्।
  *dhanyavādāḥ, atīva dayāluḥ bhavān.*
  Thank you, you are very kind.

**Dialogue 2: Bumping into someone**

— क्षम्यताम्! अहं न दृष्टवान्।
  *kṣamyatām! ahaṃ na dṛṣṭavān.*
  Sorry! I didn't see.

— न किञ्चित्, चिन्ता मास्तु।
  *na kiñcit, cintā māstu.*
  It's nothing, don't worry.

— पुनः मिलामः, शुभदिनम् अस्तु।
  *punaḥ milāmaḥ, śubhadinam astu.*
  See you again, have a good day.

— शुभाशयाः!
  *śubhāśayāḥ!*
  Good wishes!

## Variations

1. **Formal "please":** *kṛpayā* can be replaced with *dayā kṛtvā* ("having done kindness") for a more elaborate, formal register.
   *dayā kṛtvā idaṃ paṭhatu.* — Kindly read this.

2. **Casual "thanks":** Instead of the full *dhanyavādāḥ*, you can simply say *dhanyaḥ asmi* ("I am grateful") or even just *sādhu* ("good, well done") in casual settings.

3. **Stronger apology:** Add *mahān aparādhaḥ* ("great offence") before *kṣamyatām* for a serious apology:
   *mahān aparādhaḥ, kṣamyatām.* — It's a great fault, please forgive.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Saying *kṣamasva* when you mean "excuse me":** *Kṣamasva* is a direct imperative — "You forgive!" It sounds demanding. Use the softer passive *kṣamyatām* ("let it be forgiven") for polite requests. Save *kṣamasva* for intimate or devotional contexts.

2. **Forgetting the plural in *dhanyavādāḥ*:** The word ends in *-āḥ* (nominative plural) because you are offering multiple expressions of thanks. Writing *dhanyavādaḥ* (singular) is common but technically less standard in the set phrase.
