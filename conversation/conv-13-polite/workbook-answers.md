## Section D: Sample Mini-Dialogues

**1. Arriving at a friend's celebration:**

— svāgatam, mitra! āgaccha, upaviśa.
  Welcome, friend! Come, sit.

— dhanyavādāḥ nimantranāya! navavarṣasya śubhāśayāḥ!
  Thank you for the invitation! Good wishes for the new year!

— tava āgamanena ānandaḥ. kṛpayā modakaṃ svīkuru.
  Happy with your arrival. Please accept a sweet.

— bahu dhanyavādāḥ. punaḥ milāmaḥ!
  Thank you very much. See you again!

**2. Stepping on someone's foot in a market:**

— kṣamyatām! ahaṃ na dṛṣṭavān. kiṃ bhavān kuśalī?
  Sorry! I didn't see. Are you well?

— na kiñcit, cintā māstu.
  It's nothing, don't worry.

— kṛpayā kṣamantu. punaḥ milāmaḥ, śubhadinam astu!
  Please forgive me. See you again, may the day be good!

— dhanyavādāḥ, bhavate api śubhadinam.
  Thank you, a good day to you too.

## Section E: Sample Responses

**1. Greeting a respected teacher:**

gurōḥ pādayōḥ namāmi. svāgatam, ācārya. kṛpayā āsane upaviśatu. bhavataḥ darśanena vayam dhanyāḥ. dayā kṛtvā asmān anugrahnātu.

(I bow at the guru's feet. Welcome, teacher. Please sit on the seat. We are blessed by your presence. Kindly bestow grace upon us.)

**2. Reflection on passive imperative:**

The passive imperative *kṣamyatām* ("let it be forgiven") avoids placing a burden directly on the listener. Instead of commanding "You forgive me!", it softly requests that forgiveness happen — as if the act is bigger than either person. This reflects a cultural and linguistic preference for indirectness in sensitive situations, making the apology feel humble rather than demanding.
