# Stage 4: Wanting & Needing

**Pattern Name:** aham X icchāmi / mama X āvaśyakam

---

## The Pattern

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| अहम् X इच्छामि | aham X icchāmi | I want X |
| त्वम् X इच्छसि | tvam X icchasi | You want X |
| किम् त्वम् X इच्छसि? | kim tvam X icchasi? | Do you want X? |
| मम X आवश्यकम् | mama X āvaśyakam | I need X |

**icchāmi** handles desire. **āvaśyakam** handles need. Two tools, most of daily life covered.

---

## How It Works

The verb **icchati** ("to want/desire") conjugates like other verbs: **icchāmi** (I want), **icchasi** (you want), **icchati** (he/she wants). The thing you want goes in the accusative case — for most neuter nouns, that's just the dictionary form (e.g., **jalam** = "water"). For masculine nouns, the accusative singular swaps **-aḥ** to **-am**: **pustakaḥ** (book, nominative) becomes **pustakam** (book, accusative — the thing wanted).

For "I need," Sanskrit uses an impersonal construction: **mama jalam āvaśyakam** — literally "for-me water necessary." The word **mama** means "my/for me," and **āvaśyakam** means "necessary." The needed thing stays in nominative. No verb conjugation needed — neat and clean.

---

## Example Phrases

1. अहम् जलम् इच्छामि।
   aham jalam icchāmi.
   "I want water."

2. अहम् भोजनम् इच्छामि।
   aham bhojanam icchāmi.
   "I want food."

3. किम् त्वम् चायम् इच्छसि?
   kim tvam cāyam icchasi?
   "Do you want tea?"

4. सः सहायम् इच्छति।
   saḥ sahāyam icchati.
   "He wants help."

5. मम जलम् आवश्यकम्।
   mama jalam āvaśyakam.
   "I need water."

6. मम विश्रामः आवश्यकः।
   mama viśrāmaḥ āvaśyakaḥ.
   "I need rest."

7. अहम् फलम् इच्छामि।
   aham phalam icchāmi.
   "I want a fruit."

8. किम् त्वम् दुग्धम् इच्छसि?
   kim tvam dugdham icchasi?
   "Do you want milk?"

9. सा पुस्तकम् इच्छति।
   sā pustakam icchati.
   "She wants a book."

10. मम सहायः आवश्यकः।
    mama sahāyaḥ āvaśyakaḥ.
    "I need help."

## Mini-Dialogue 1

**सीता (sītā):** किम् त्वम् चायम् इच्छसि?
kim tvam cāyam icchasi?
"Do you want tea?"

**रामः (rāmaḥ):** न, अहम् जलम् इच्छामि।
na, aham jalam icchāmi.
"No, I want water."

**सीता (sītā):** किम् त्वम् फलम् इच्छसि?
kim tvam phalam icchasi?
"Do you want fruit?"

**रामः (rāmaḥ):** आम्, अहम् फलम् इच्छामि।
ām, aham phalam icchāmi.
"Yes, I want fruit."

## Mini-Dialogue 2

**बालकः (bālakaḥ):** मम भोजनम् आवश्यकम्। अहम् क्षुधितः अस्मि।
mama bhojanam āvaśyakam. aham kṣudhitaḥ asmi.
"I need food. I am hungry."

**माता (mātā):** किम् त्वम् फलम् इच्छसि?
kim tvam phalam icchasi?
"Do you want fruit?"

**बालकः:** न, अहम् भोजनम् इच्छामि!
na, aham bhojanam icchāmi!
"No, I want a (proper) meal!"

**माता:** प्रथमम् फलम् खाद।
prathamam phalam khāda.
"First, eat the fruit."

## Variations

1. **vāñchati — a fancier "want":** The verb **vāñchati** also means "to want/wish for," with a slightly more elevated tone. Same conjugation pattern: **aham pustakam vāñchāmi** = "I desire a book."

2. **mama ... na āvaśyakam — "I don't need":** Just slip **na** before **āvaśyakam**: **mama etat na āvaśyakam** = "I don't need this."

3. **Infinitive + icchāmi — "I want to...":** To say "I want to go," use **gantum icchāmi** (gantum = "to go"). The infinitive ends in **-tum**: **khāditum icchāmi** = "I want to eat."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing icchāmi and āvaśyakam constructions.** With **icchāmi**, the subject is "I" (aham) and the verb conjugates. With **āvaśyakam**, it's impersonal — you say **mama** ("my/for me"), not **aham**.

2. **Forgetting accusative with icchāmi.** The object of wanting takes accusative: **pustakam icchāmi** (not **pustakaḥ icchāmi**).
