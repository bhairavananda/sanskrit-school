# Stage 29: Clarifying & Repairing

**Pattern: punaḥ vadatu / na avagatam / ko'rthaḥ?**

---

## The Pattern

These are your **survival tools** for real conversation. When you mishear, blank on a word, or need something repeated, these phrases keep you in Sanskrit instead of bailing out to English.

| Phrase | IAST | Use |
|--------|------|-----|
| Say that again (polite) | punaḥ vadatu | Requesting repetition |
| I don't understand | na avagacchāmi | Present confusion |
| I didn't understand | na avagatam | Past confusion |
| What does it mean? | kim arthaḥ? / ko'rthaḥ? | Asking meaning |
| That is to say... | arthāt... | Rephrasing/clarifying |
| How do you say...? | kathaṃ ucyate? | Asking for a word |
| Speak slowly | śanaiḥ vadatu | Slowing pace |
| Sorry, say again | kṣamyatām, punaḥ vadatu | Polite repeat request |

---

## How It Works

**punaḥ vadatu** uses the polite imperative — technically the 3rd-person command form ("let him/her speak"), but used as a respectful "you" form, like Hindi's *āp*-style address. For casual "you," say **punar vada**.

**avagacchāmi** is *ava* + *gam* — literally "go into," meaning "understand." The past form **avagatam** (literally "it was understood") is a neat impersonal construction: "understanding happened (to me)." Add **na** and you get a clean "I didn't get that."

**ko'rthaḥ** is a sandhi of *kaḥ arthaḥ* — "what (is the) meaning?" You can prefix any word: **'dharma'-śabdasya ko'rthaḥ?** = "What is the meaning of the word 'dharma'?"

**śanaiḥ** is the instrumental plural of *śanai*, used as an adverb meaning "slowly." **arthāt** is the ablative of *artha*, functioning as "that is to say" or "meaning..."

## Example Phrases

1. **क्षम्यताम्, पुनः वदतु**
   kṣamyatām, punaḥ vadatu
   *Sorry, say that again.*

2. **न अवगतम्। कृपया शनैः वदतु**
   na avagatam. kṛpayā śanaiḥ vadatu
   *I didn't understand. Please speak slowly.*

3. **'धर्म'-शब्दस्य कोऽर्थः?**
   'dharma'-śabdasya ko'rthaḥ?
   *What is the meaning of the word 'dharma'?*

4. **'book' इति संस्कृते कथम् उच्यते?**
   'book' iti saṃskṛte katham ucyate?
   *How do you say 'book' in Sanskrit?*

5. **अर्थात् सः न आगमिष्यति**
   arthāt saḥ na āgamiṣyati
   *That is to say, he won't come.*

6. **त्वं किम् अवदः? न अवगच्छामि**
   tvaṃ kim avadaḥ? na avagacchāmi
   *What did you say? I don't understand.*

7. **किं पुनः वक्तुं शक्नोषि?**
   kiṃ punaḥ vaktuṃ śaknoṣi?
   *Can you repeat that?*

8. **इदानीं अवगतम्। धन्यवादः**
   idānīṃ avagatam. dhanyavādaḥ
   *Now I understood. Thank you.*

9. **एतत् पदं किम् अर्थं वहति?**
   etat padaṃ kim arthaṃ vahati?
   *What meaning does this word carry?*

10. **कृपया स्पष्टं वदतु**
    kṛpayā spaṣṭaṃ vadatu
    *Please speak clearly.*

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: The unknown word**

**छात्रः:** गुरो, 'विमर्शः' इति पदस्य कोऽर्थः?
chātraḥ: guro, 'vimarśaḥ' iti padasya ko'rthaḥ?
*Student: Teacher, what is the meaning of the word 'vimarśa'?*

**गुरुः:** विमर्शः अर्थात् 'विचारः' इति। 'reflection' इति आङ्ग्ले उच्यते।
guruḥ: vimarśaḥ arthāt 'vicāraḥ' iti. 'reflection' iti āṅgle ucyate.
*Teacher: Vimarśa, that is to say, 'thought/reflection.' In English it's called 'reflection.'*

**छात्रः:** इदानीम् अवगतम्। धन्यवादः गुरो।
chātraḥ: idānīm avagatam. dhanyavādaḥ guro.
*Student: Now I understand. Thank you, teacher.*

---

**Dialogue 2: Too fast!**

**सुमा:** ह्यः अहं नगरं गतवती तत्र मया बहूनि वस्तूनि क्रीतानि मम भगिनी अपि आगतवती आवां बहु विनोदम् अकुर्वाव...
sumā: hyaḥ ahaṃ nagaraṃ gatavatī tatra mayā bahūni vastūni krītāni mama bhaginī api āgatavatī āvāṃ bahu vinodam akurvāva...
*Suma: Yesterday I went to the city and I bought many things and my sister also came and we had lots of fun...*

**रामः:** शनैः शनैः! कृपया पुनः वदतु। त्वं बहु शीघ्रं वदसि!
rāmaḥ: śanaiḥ śanaiḥ! kṛpayā punaḥ vadatu. tvaṃ bahu śīghraṃ vadasi!
*Ram: Slowly, slowly! Please say that again. You speak too fast!*

**सुमा:** क्षम्यताम्! ह्यः... अहं... नगरं... गतवती। अवगतम्?
sumā: kṣamyatām! hyaḥ... ahaṃ... nagaraṃ... gatavatī. avagatam?
*Suma: Sorry! Yesterday... I... went... to the city. Understood?*

**रामः:** आम्, इदानीम् अवगतम्!
rāmaḥ: ām, idānīm avagatam!
*Ram: Yes, now I understand!*

## Variations

- **punar brūhi** — a more casual "say it again" using *brū* (speak) in the familiar imperative.
- **kim iti?** — literally "what, thus?" — a quick, colloquial "what was that?" or "come again?"
- **spaṣṭaṃ vadatu** — "speak clearly" — when the issue is clarity, not speed.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Saying *na jānāmi* when you mean *na avagacchāmi*.** *Na jānāmi* means "I don't know (the fact)." *Na avagacchāmi* means "I don't understand (what you said)." If someone asks you a question and you lack the knowledge, use *na jānāmi*. If you heard words but couldn't parse them, use *na avagacchāmi*.

2. **Forgetting that *vadatu* is already polite.** You don't need to pile on extra politeness. *Punaḥ vadatu* is perfectly respectful. Adding *kṛpayā* (please) makes it extra polite but isn't required.
