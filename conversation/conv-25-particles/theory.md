# Stage 25: Particles & Emphasis

**Pattern: eva · api · khalu · nanu · kila · tu · vā**

---

## The Pattern

Sanskrit has a set of tiny, unchanging words — particles — that color everything around them. They never change form (they're *avyaya*, indeclinables). But where you place them changes the entire meaning of a sentence.

> **word + eva** = ONLY that word / that word ITSELF / INDEED that word
> **word + api** = EVEN that word / that word ALSO / that word TOO
> **khalu** = certainly, indeed (sentence-level confidence)
> **nanu** = isn't it so? / surely (opens rhetorical questions)
> **kila** = reportedly, they say (marks hearsay)
> **tu** = but, on the other hand (mild contrast)
> **vā** = or

## How It Works

Without particles, Sanskrit sounds like a telegram. With them, it sounds like a person talking. These words don't describe actions or things — they express *attitude*. They tell the listener what you're emphasizing, what you're contrasting, what you heard secondhand, and what you think should be obvious.

The crucial thing is *position*. **eva** and **api** come right after the word they modify. **khalu** usually sits in second position in the sentence. **nanu** opens the sentence. **kila** follows the subject or verb it marks as hearsay. **tu** follows the word being contrasted. **vā** follows each option in an "or" construction.

## Example Phrases

Watch how one base sentence transforms:

**Base:** रामः गच्छति (rāmaḥ gacchati) — "Rama goes."

1. रामः एव गच्छति (rāmaḥ eva gacchati) — "ONLY Rama goes." / "Rama HIMSELF goes."
2. रामः अपि गच्छति (rāmaḥ api gacchati) — "Rama ALSO goes." / "Even Rama goes."
3. रामः खलु गच्छति (rāmaḥ khalu gacchati) — "Rama certainly goes."
4. रामः किल गच्छति (rāmaḥ kila gacchati) — "Rama reportedly goes." / "They say Rama goes."
5. ननु रामः गच्छति? (nanu rāmaḥ gacchati?) — "Doesn't Rama go?" / "Surely Rama goes?"
6. सः एव अवदत् (saḥ eva avadat) — "He HIMSELF said it."
7. अहम् अपि न जानामि (aham api na jānāmi) — "Even I don't know."
8. ननु एतत् सत्यम्? (nanu etat satyam?) — "Isn't that true?"
9. सः किल अगच्छत् (saḥ kila agacchat) — "They say he left."
10. त्वं वा अहं वा (tvaṃ vā ahaṃ vā) — "You or me."

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **अर्जुनः** — कः अत्र आगच्छति? (kaḥ atra āgacchati?)
> "Who's coming here?"
>
> **कृष्णः** — भीमः एव आगच्छति। (bhīmaḥ eva āgacchati.)
> "Only Bhima is coming."
>
> **अर्जुनः** — ननु नकुलः अपि आगच्छति? (nanu nakulaḥ api āgacchati?)
> "Doesn't Nakula come too?"
>
> **कृष्णः** — न, सः तु गृहे तिष्ठति। (na, saḥ tu gṛhe tiṣṭhati.)
> "No, he on the other hand stays home."

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **सीता** — रावणः किल शक्तिमान्। (rāvaṇaḥ kila śaktimān.)
> "They say Ravana is powerful."
>
> **रामः** — सत्यम् एव। किन्तु धर्मः खलु बलवत्तरः। (satyam eva. kintu dharmaḥ khalu balavattaraḥ.)
> "True indeed. But dharma is certainly stronger."
>
> **सीता** — ननु त्वम् अपि शक्तिमान् असि? (nanu tvam api śaktimān asi?)
> "Aren't you powerful too?"
>
> **रामः** — ईश्वरः एव जानाति। (īśvaraḥ eva jānāti.)
> "Only God knows."

## Variations

- **eva** can intensify time words: अधुना एव (adhunā eva) = "right now," तदा एव (tadā eva) = "right then"
- **api** after a verb makes it concessive: गच्छति अपि (gacchati api) = "even though he goes"
- **vā ... vā** creates "either ... or": फलं वा जलं वा (phalaṃ vā jalaṃ vā) = "fruit or water"

## Common Mistakes

1. **Putting eva/api in the wrong position.** "Only Rama goes" is रामः एव गच्छति, NOT एव रामः गच्छति. The particle follows the word it modifies. If you write गच्छति एव, it means "he only GOES" (emphasis on going, not on who).

2. **Confusing kila and khalu.** khalu = "certainly" (you believe it). kila = "reportedly" (someone else said it, you're not vouching). They sound vaguely similar but point in opposite directions of certainty.
