# Stage 18: First, Then, Finally

## The Pattern

**prathamaṃ X** — "first, X"
**tataḥ Y** — "then, Y"
**anantaraṃ Z** — "after that, Z"
**ante / antataḥ** — "finally / at last"
**pūrvam** — "before / earlier"

You can now tell stories, give directions, explain processes, and walk someone through a recipe. Sequencing is the skeleton of narrative.

---

## How It Works

All five of these words are **indeclinables** (avyaya) — they never change form, no matter what the subject or tense is. They simply slot into the sentence to mark order. Think of them as signposts along a road: *prathamam* plants the flag at the start, *tataḥ* moves you forward one step, *anantaram* takes another step, and *ante* or *antataḥ* signals the finish line.

**Pūrvam** ("before/earlier") lets you reference something that came before the current action. It can also combine with a noun in the ablative: *bhojanāt pūrvam* = "before the meal."

The beauty of these words is that they stack naturally. You can chain as many *tataḥ* steps as you need — *tataḥ...tataḥ...tataḥ...* — and it sounds perfectly normal, like "then...then...then" in spoken English. For variety, swap in *anantaram* or *tadanantaram* ("immediately after that") to break the rhythm.

## Example Phrases

1. प्रथमं हस्तौ प्रक्षालयतु, ततः भोजनं करोतु।
   *prathamaṃ hastau prakṣālayatu, tataḥ bhojanaṃ karotu.*
   First wash your hands, then eat.

2. ततः सः विद्यालयं गतवान्।
   *tataḥ saḥ vidyālayaṃ gatavān.*
   Then he went to school.

3. अनन्तरं वयं चायं पीतवन्तः।
   *anantaraṃ vayaṃ cāyaṃ pītavantaḥ.*
   After that, we drank tea.

4. अन्ते सर्वे गृहं प्रत्यागताः।
   *ante sarve gṛhaṃ pratyāgatāḥ.*
   Finally, everyone returned home.

5. भोजनात् पूर्वं प्रार्थनां कुर्मः।
   *bhojanāt pūrvaṃ prārthanāṃ kurmaḥ.*
   Before the meal, we pray.

6. प्रथमं श्रुत्वा ततः विचिन्त्य अनन्तरं वदतु।
   *prathamaṃ śrutvā tataḥ vicintya anantaraṃ vadatu.*
   First listen, then think, after that speak.

7. पूर्वम् अहं दिल्ल्यां वसामि स्म।
   *pūrvam ahaṃ dillyāṃ vasāmi sma.*
   Earlier, I used to live in Delhi.

8. प्रथमं तण्डुलान् प्रक्षालयतु, ततः जलं योजयतु, अन्ततः पचतु।
   *prathamaṃ taṇḍulān prakṣālayatu, tataḥ jalaṃ yojayatu, antataḥ pacatu.*
   First wash the rice, then add water, finally cook.

9. सः प्रथमं सस्मित, ततः अवदत्।
   *saḥ prathamaṃ sasmita, tataḥ avadat.*
   He first smiled, then spoke.

10. स्नानात् अनन्तरं वस्त्राणि धृतवान्।
    *snānāt anantaraṃ vastrāṇi dhṛtavān.*
    After the bath, he put on clothes.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Giving directions**

— क्षम्यतां, देवालयः कुत्र अस्ति?
  *kṣamyatāṃ, devālayaḥ kutra asti?*
  Excuse me, where is the temple?

— प्रथमम् ऋजु गच्छतु। ततः वामे परिवर्ततु। अनन्तरं सेतुं पारयतु। अन्ते देवालयः दृश्यते।
  *prathamam ṛju gacchatu. tataḥ vāme parivartatu. anantaraṃ setuṃ pārayatu. ante devālayaḥ dṛśyate.*
  First go straight. Then turn left. After that, cross the bridge. Finally, the temple is visible.

— बहु धन्यवादाः!
  *bahu dhanyavādāḥ!*
  Thank you very much!

**Dialogue 2: Morning routine**

— तव प्रातःकालः कथम् अस्ति?
  *tava prātaḥkālaḥ katham asti?*
  What is your morning like?

— प्रथमम् उत्तिष्ठामि, ततः मुखं प्रक्षालयामि। अनन्तरं योगं करोमि।
  *prathamam uttiṣṭhāmi, tataḥ mukhaṃ prakṣālayāmi. anantaraṃ yogaṃ karomi.*
  First I get up, then I wash my face. After that I do yoga.

— अन्ते प्रातःभोजनम्?
  *ante prātaḥbhojanam?*
  And finally, breakfast?

— आम्! स्नानात् अनन्तरं भोजनं करोमि, ततः विद्यालयं गच्छामि।
  *ām! snānāt anantaraṃ bhojanaṃ karomi, tataḥ vidyālayaṃ gacchāmi.*
  Yes! After bathing I eat, then I go to school.

## Variations

1. **tadanantaram** ("immediately after that") — A more emphatic form of *anantaram*:
   *saḥ āgataḥ, tadanantaraṃ sarvaṃ parivartatam.* — He arrived, and immediately after, everything changed.

2. **paścāt** ("afterwards / later") — Another sequencing word, useful for looser time connections:
   *prathamaṃ paṭha, paścāt krīḍa.* — First study, later play.

3. **ādau** ("in the beginning / at first") — A synonym for *prathamam*, slightly more literary:
   *ādau kaṣṭam āsīt, parantu ante sāphalam.* — At first it was hard, but in the end, successful.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Overusing *tataḥ* without *prathamam* or *ante*:** A chain of *tataḥ...tataḥ...tataḥ* is fine for two or three steps, but longer sequences feel flat. Anchor the beginning with *prathamam* and the end with *ante* or *antataḥ* to give the narrative shape.

2. **Confusing *pūrvam* and *prathamam*:** *Prathamam* means "first" in a sequence — step one. *Pūrvam* means "before" or "earlier" and often references a past time or a point before another action. They overlap but are not identical: *pūrvam ahaṃ tatra vasāmi sma* ("earlier I lived there") vs. *prathamaṃ tatra gacchāmaḥ* ("first we go there").
