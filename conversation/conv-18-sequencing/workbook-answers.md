# Stage 18: First, Then, Finally — Workbook Answers

---

## Section A: Translate to Sanskrit

1. prathamaṃ pustakaṃ udgāṭaya, tataḥ pāṭhaṃ paṭha.
2. śayanāt pūrvaṃ uṣṇaṃ dugdhaṃ pibāmi.
3. saḥ prathamaṃ āpaṇaṃ gatavān, tataḥ devālayaṃ gatavān, ante gṛhaṃ āgatavān.
4. bhojanāt anantaraṃ vayam udyāne abhramaṃ.
5. pūrvam ahaṃ saṃskṛtaṃ na jānāmi sma, kintu adhunā vadāmi.

---

## Section B: Translate to English

1. First bathe, then do worship.
2. Before the meal, wash your hands.
3. He first laughed, then cried, and finally left.
4. After that, we went to see the city.
5. At first it was difficult, afterwards it became easy.

## Section C: Fill in the Blank

1. **prathamaṃ** jalaṃ uṣṇaṃ kuru, **tataḥ** cāya-patrāṇi yojaya.
2. snānāt **anantaraṃ** vastrāṇi dhārayāmi.
3. **pūrvam** sā grāme avasata.
4. prathamaṃ śṛṇu, tataḥ vicāraya, **ante** vada. (also acceptable: **antataḥ**)
5. vidyālayāt **anantaraṃ** gṛhakāryaṃ karomi.

## Section D: Sample Mini-Dialogues

**1. How to make tea:**

— cāyaṃ kathaṃ racayāmi?
  How do I make tea?

— prathamaṃ jalaṃ pātre uṣṇaṃ kuru. tataḥ cāya-patrāṇi dugdhaṃ ca yojaya.
  First heat water in a pot. Then add tea leaves and milk.

— anantaraṃ kim?
  After that, what?

— anantaraṃ śarkarāṃ yojaya, antataḥ cālanyena cālaya. cāyaḥ siddhaḥ!
  After that add sugar, finally strain with a strainer. The tea is ready!

**2. What did you do yesterday?**

— hyaḥ tvaṃ kiṃ kṛtavān?
  What did you do yesterday?

— prathamaṃ prātaḥ yogam akārṣam. tataḥ vidyālayaṃ gatavān.
  First I did yoga in the morning. Then I went to school.

— anantaram?
  After that?

— anantaraṃ mitreṇa saha krīḍitavān. ante gṛhe pustakaṃ paṭhitvā suptavān.
  After that I played with a friend. Finally, at home I read a book and slept.

## Section E: Sample Responses

**1. Route description:**

mama gṛhāt prathamaṃ ṛjuṃ gacchāmi. tataḥ mahāmārgē dakṣiṇe parivartāmi. anantaraṃ dvau kṣetrau atikramya āpaṇaṃ paśyāmi. ante vāme parivartya devālayaḥ samīpe eva asti.

(From my home, first I go straight. Then at the main road I turn right. After that, passing two fields, I see the market. Finally, turning left, the temple is right nearby.)

**2. Three-step advice:**

*prathamaṃ paśya, tataḥ jānīhi, anantaraṃ kuru.* — First observe, then understand, then act.

I chose these three steps because most mistakes come from acting without understanding, and most misunderstanding comes from not observing carefully. The sequence forces patience — you must look before you know, and know before you do. It applies to cooking, to learning, and to life.
