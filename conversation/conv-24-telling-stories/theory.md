# Stage 24: Telling a Story

## Pattern Name: Narrative Building Blocks

---

## The Pattern

**ekadā** — "once upon a time"
**purā** — "long ago"
**kasmiṃścit nagare** — "in a certain city"
**tataḥ saḥ** — "then he..."
**iti uktvā** — "having said thus"
**evam** — "thus / in this way"
**iti kathā samāptā** — "thus the story ends"

---

## How It Works

Storytelling in Sanskrit has a toolkit of transition words and narrative devices that give your speech a rhythmic, flowing quality. *Ekadā* and *purā* set the scene in time. *Kasmiṃścit* ("in a certain...") uses the locative case with an indefinite pronoun to create that classic fable opening -- "in a certain village there lived..." The locative does all the setting work: *kasmiṃścit nagare* (in a certain city), *kasmiṃścit vane* (in a certain forest).

The glue of Sanskrit narrative is the gerund (also called absolutive): *uktvā* (having said), *gatvā* (having gone), *kṛtvā* (having done), *dṛṣṭvā* (having seen), *śrutvā* (having heard). These chain actions together: "having done X, he then did Y." The word *iti* is the quotative particle -- it marks the end of quoted speech, like a closing quotation mark. And *tataḥ* ("then, after that") keeps the narrative moving forward. Stack these together and you can tell a complete story with surprisingly few verbs.

## Example Phrases

1. **एकदा एकः राजा आसीत्।**
   *ekadā ekaḥ rājā āsīt.*
   Once upon a time, there was a king.

2. **पुरा कस्मिंश्चित् ग्रामे एकः कृषकः अवसत्।**
   *purā kasmiṃścit grāme ekaḥ kṛṣakaḥ avasat.*
   Long ago, in a certain village, a farmer lived.

3. **सः तत् दृष्ट्वा आश्चर्यम् अनुभूतवान्।**
   *saḥ tat dṛṣṭvā āścaryam anubhūtavān.*
   Having seen that, he felt astonishment.

4. **"अहं न बिभेमि" इति उक्त्वा सः अगच्छत्।**
   *"ahaṃ na bibhemi" iti uktvā saḥ agacchat.*
   Having said "I am not afraid," he went.

5. **ततः सा वनं गत्वा फलानि असंगृह्णात्।**
   *tataḥ sā vanaṃ gatvā phalāni asaṃgṛhṇāt.*
   Then, having gone to the forest, she gathered fruits.

6. **तत् श्रुत्वा राजा प्रसन्नः अभवत्।**
   *tat śrutvā rājā prasannaḥ abhavat.*
   Having heard that, the king became pleased.

7. **एवं सः धनवान् अभवत्।**
   *evaṃ saḥ dhanavān abhavat.*
   Thus he became wealthy.

8. **"भवतु" इति उक्त्वा सः गृहं प्रत्यागच्छत्।**
   *"bhavatu" iti uktvā saḥ gṛhaṃ pratyāgacchat.*
   Having said "so be it," he returned home.

9. **ततः तौ मिलित्वा अन्नम् अभक्षयताम्।**
   *tataḥ tau militvā annam abhakṣayatām.*
   Then the two of them, having met, ate food together.

10. **इति कथा समाप्ता।**
    *iti kathā samāptā.*
    Thus the story is concluded.

## A Complete Mini-Story: The Farmer and the Fox

This story uses patterns from many previous stages. See how the lego pieces fit together.

> **पुरा कस्मिंश्चित् ग्रामे एकः कृषकः अवसत्।** *(Stage 24: narrative opening)*
> *purā kasmiṃścit grāme ekaḥ kṛṣakaḥ avasat.*
> Long ago, in a certain village, a farmer lived.
>
> **तस्य क्षेत्रं सर्वेभ्यः क्षेत्रेभ्यः उत्तमम् आसीत्।** *(Stage 19: ablative comparison)*
> *tasya kṣetraṃ sarvebhyaḥ kṣetrebhyaḥ uttamam āsīt.*
> His field was better than all other fields.
>
> **एकदा एकः शृगालः आगत्य फलानि अखादत्।** *(Stage 24: gerund āgatya)*
> *ekadā ekaḥ śṛgālaḥ āgatya phalāni akhādat.*
> One day, a fox came and ate the fruits.
>
> **तत् दृष्ट्वा कृषकः अचिन्तयत् — "यः मम फलानि चोरयति सः दण्डनीयः अस्ति।"** *(Stage 20: yaḥ...saḥ; Stage 22: gerundive)*
> *tat dṛṣṭvā kṛṣakaḥ acintayat — "yaḥ mama phalāni corayati saḥ daṇḍanīyaḥ asti."*
> Having seen that, the farmer thought: "He who steals my fruits should be punished."
>
> **"मया एतत् समस्या समाधातव्या" इति उक्त्वा सः जालं स्थापितवान्।** *(Stage 22: obligation; Stage 24: iti uktvā)*
> *"mayā etat samasyā samādhātavyā" iti uktvā saḥ jālaṃ sthāpitavān.*
> Having said "I must solve this problem," he set a trap.
>
> **अपरस्मिन् दिने शृगालः जाले बद्धः अभवत्। सः अवदत् — "कृपया मां मुञ्च। अहं पुनः न आगमिष्यामि।"** *(Stage 24: narrative past)*
> *aparasmin dine śṛgālaḥ jāle baddhaḥ abhavat. saḥ avadat — "kṛpayā māṃ muñca. ahaṃ punaḥ na āgamiṣyāmi."*
> The next day, the fox was caught in the trap. He said: "Please release me. I will not come again."
>
> **कृषकः अमन्यत — "मम मतेन एषः सत्यं वदति।"** *(Stage 21: manye, mama matena)*
> *kṛṣakaḥ amanyata — "mama matena eṣaḥ satyaṃ vadati."*
> The farmer thought: "In my opinion, he speaks the truth."
>
> **यदि सः शृगालं न अमोक्ष्यत् तर्हि शृगालः मृतः अभविष्यत्।** *(Stage 23: counterfactual)*
> *yadi saḥ śṛgālaṃ na amokṣyat tarhi śṛgālaḥ mṛtaḥ abhaviṣyat.*
> If he had not released the fox, the fox would have died.
>
> **किन्तु दयां कृत्वा सः शृगालं मुक्तवान्। ततः शृगालः वनं प्रत्यगच्छत्।** *(Stage 24: gerund kṛtvā)*
> *kintu dayāṃ kṛtvā saḥ śṛgālaṃ muktavān. tataḥ śṛgālaḥ vanaṃ pratyagacchat.*
> But having shown mercy, he released the fox. Then the fox returned to the forest.
>
> **एवं कृषकस्य दयया शृगालः जीवितवान्। इति कथा समाप्ता।** *(Stage 24: evam, iti kathā samāptā)*
> *evaṃ kṛṣakasya dayayā śṛgālaḥ jīvitavān. iti kathā samāptā.*
> Thus, by the farmer's compassion, the fox lived. The story ends here.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Asking for a story**

-- एकां कथां वद न।
   *ekāṃ kathāṃ vada na.*
   Tell me a story, won't you?

-- शृणु। पुरा एकः वानरः आसीत्। सः नद्यां स्वमुखं दृष्ट्वा अचिन्तयत् — "एषः कः अस्ति?" इति।
   *śṛṇu. purā ekaḥ vānaraḥ āsīt. saḥ nadyāṃ svamukhaṃ dṛṣṭvā acintayat — "eṣaḥ kaḥ asti?" iti.*
   Listen. Long ago, there was a monkey. Seeing his own face in the river, he thought -- "who is this?"

-- ततः किम् अभवत्?
   *tataḥ kim abhavat?*
   Then what happened?

-- ततः सः जलं प्रहृतवान्, मुखं विलुप्तम्, सः हसितवान्। इति कथा समाप्ता!
   *tataḥ saḥ jalaṃ prahṛtavān, mukhaṃ viluptam, saḥ hasitavān. iti kathā samāptā!*
   Then he struck the water, the face vanished, and he laughed. Thus the story ends!

**Dialogue 2: Retelling an event**

-- ह्यः किम् अभवत्? कथय।
   *hyaḥ kim abhavat? kathaya.*
   What happened yesterday? Tell me.

-- ह्यः अहं विपणिं गत्वा फलानि क्रीतवान्। गृहम् आगत्य भोजनम् अपचम्।
   *hyaḥ ahaṃ vipaṇiṃ gatvā phalāni krītavān. gṛham āgatya bhojanam apacam.*
   Yesterday, having gone to the market, I bought fruits. Having come home, I cooked food.

-- ततः?
   *tataḥ?*
   Then?

-- ततः मित्रम् आहूय सह भोजनम् अकुर्वम्। एवम् उत्तमः दिनः आसीत्।
   *tataḥ mitram āhūya saha bhojanam akurvam. evam uttamaḥ dinaḥ āsīt.*
   Then, having invited a friend, we ate together. Thus it was an excellent day.

## Variations

1. **Different story openers:**
   *asti kasmiṃścit deśe...* ("there is, in a certain land...") -- the Panchatantra style.
   *bhūtapūrvaṃ...* ("in times past...") -- more formal.

2. **Chaining multiple gerunds:**
   *saḥ snātvā, bhuktvā, paṭhitvā, śayitavān* — "He bathed, ate, studied, and slept." (Four actions chained with gerunds before the final verb.)

3. **Closing formulas:**
   *evaṃ kathā parisamāptā* — "Thus the story is fully concluded."
   *iyaṃ kathā śubhaṃ dadātu* — "May this story bring auspiciousness."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using gerunds for simultaneous actions:** Gerunds (*gatvā, kṛtvā*) mean "having done X, then..." -- they show sequence, not simultaneity. If two things happen at the same time, you need a present participle (*gacchan* = "while going"), not a gerund.

2. **Forgetting iti after quoted speech:** In Sanskrit, *iti* comes after the quote, not before. English says: He said, "I will go." Sanskrit says: "ahaṃ gamiṣyāmi" *iti* saḥ avadat. Dropping *iti* leaves the quote hanging without a closing bracket.
