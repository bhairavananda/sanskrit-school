# Stage 24: Telling a Story — Workbook Questions

**Total: 40 marks**

---

## Section A: Translate to Sanskrit (10 marks, 2 each)

1. Once upon a time, there was a lion in a certain forest.
2. Having seen the river, she was happy.
3. "I will come tomorrow," having said thus, he went home.
4. Then the king, having heard that, became angry.
5. Thus the story ends.

---

## Section B: Translate to English (10 marks, 2 each)

1. पुरा कस्मिंश्चित् नगरे एका राज्ञी अवसत्।
   *purā kasmiṃścit nagare ekā rājñī avasat.*

2. सः वनं गत्वा वृक्षम् आरूढवान्।
   *saḥ vanaṃ gatvā vṛkṣam ārūḍhavān.*

3. "अहं न बिभेमि" इति उक्त्वा सा अग्रे अगच्छत्।
   *"ahaṃ na bibhemi" iti uktvā sā agre agacchat.*

4. तत् श्रुत्वा सर्वे जनाः प्रसन्नाः अभवन्।
   *tat śrutvā sarve janāḥ prasannāḥ abhavan.*

5. एवं सः बुद्धिमान् बालकः सर्वान् रक्षितवान्।
   *evaṃ saḥ buddhimān bālakaḥ sarvān rakṣitavān.*

---

## Section C: Fill in the Blank (10 marks, 2 each)

1. __________ कस्मिंश्चित् वने एकः व्याघ्रः अवसत्। (Long ago)
   *__________ kasmiṃścit vane ekaḥ vyāghraḥ avasat.*

2. तत् __________ राजा कुपितः अभवत्। (having heard — supply the gerund of śru)
   *tat __________ rājā kupitaḥ abhavat.*

3. "मम समीपम् आगच्छ" __________ सः अवदत्। (supply the quotative particle)
   *"mama samīpam āgaccha" __________ saḥ avadat.*

4. __________ सर्वे सुखिनः अभवन्। (Then — supply the narrative connector)
   *__________ sarve sukhinaḥ abhavan.*

5. एवं __________ कथा __________। (thus the story ends — supply both words)
   *evaṃ __________ kathā __________.*

---

## Section D: Build a Mini-Dialogue (6 marks, 3 each)

1. **Prompt:** Tell a 4-5 line mini-story about an animal (your choice). Use *ekadā* or *purā* to open, at least two gerunds (*gatvā, dṛṣṭvā, kṛtvā*, etc.), *tataḥ* for transitions, and *iti kathā samāptā* to close.

2. **Prompt:** Write a 3-4 line dialogue where one person asks "what happened yesterday?" (*hyaḥ kim abhavat?*) and the other retells a simple event using gerunds and *tataḥ*.

---

## Section E: Free Response (4 marks, 2 each)

1. Write a 5-6 line mini-story in Sanskrit about a person who goes on a journey. Use at least three gerunds, one piece of quoted speech with *iti*, and close with *iti kathā samāptā*. Try to include at least one pattern from a previous stage (comparison, opinion, obligation, or counterfactual).

2. Retell something that happened to you recently (real or imagined) in 3-4 Sanskrit sentences, using narrative connectors (*tataḥ*, *ekadā*) and gerunds.
