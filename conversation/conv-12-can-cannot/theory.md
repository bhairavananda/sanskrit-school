# Stage 12: Can & Cannot

## The Pattern

**aham X kartum śaknomi** — "I can do X"

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| अहम् X कर्तुं शक्नोमि | aham X kartuṃ śaknomi | I can do X |
| न शक्नोमि | na śaknomi | I can't |
| किं त्वम् Y कर्तुं शक्नोषि? | kiṃ tvam Y kartuṃ śaknoṣi? | Can you do Y? |
| सः Z कर्तुं शक्नोति | saḥ Z kartuṃ śaknoti | He can do Z |

---

## How It Works

To say "can" in Sanskrit, pair the verb **śak** (to be able) with an infinitive — a verb form ending in **-tum**. The infinitive is simple to build: take the root and add **-tum**. So **kar** (do) becomes **kartum** (to do), **gam** (go) becomes **gantum** (to go), **vac** (speak) becomes **vaktum** (to speak), **khād** (eat) becomes **khāditum** (to eat), **paṭh** (read) becomes **paṭhitum** (to read).

The conjugation of **śak**: **śaknomi** (I can), **śaknoṣi** (you can), **śaknoti** (he/she can), **śaknuvaḥ** (we two can), **śaknumaḥ** (we all can). Negate with **na**: **na śaknomi** = "I can't." The structure is always: person + action-infinitive + śak-form. So "I can go" = *ahaṃ gantuṃ śaknomi*.

## Example Phrases

1. अहं कर्तुं शक्नोमि।
   ahaṃ kartuṃ śaknomi.
   *I can do it.*

2. अहं न शक्नोमि।
   ahaṃ na śaknomi.
   *I can't.*

3. किं त्वं गन्तुं शक्नोषि?
   kiṃ tvaṃ gantuṃ śaknoṣi?
   *Can you go?*

4. सः संस्कृतं वक्तुं शक्नोति।
   saḥ saṃskṛtaṃ vaktuṃ śaknoti.
   *He can speak Sanskrit.*

5. अहं तत्र गन्तुं न शक्नोमि।
   ahaṃ tatra gantuṃ na śaknomi.
   *I can't go there.*

6. सा गातुं शक्नोति।
   sā gātuṃ śaknoti.
   *She can sing.*

7. वयं पठितुं शक्नुमः।
   vayaṃ paṭhituṃ śaknumaḥ.
   *We can read.*

8. त्वं किं कर्तुं शक्नोषि?
   tvaṃ kiṃ kartuṃ śaknoṣi?
   *What can you do?*

9. अहं साहाय्यं कर्तुं शक्नोमि।
   ahaṃ sāhāyyaṃ kartuṃ śaknomi.
   *I can help. (I can do help.)*

10. सः खादितुं न शक्नोति।
    saḥ khādituṃ na śaknoti.
    *He cannot eat.*

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **अ:** किं त्वं श्वः आगन्तुं शक्नोषि?
> A: kiṃ tvaṃ śvaḥ āgantuṃ śaknoṣi?
> *Can you come tomorrow?*

> **ब:** न शक्नोमि। श्वः मम कार्यम् अस्ति।
> B: na śaknomi. śvaḥ mama kāryam asti.
> *I can't. I have work tomorrow.*

> **अ:** परश्वः शक्नोषि वा?
> A: paraśvaḥ śaknoṣi vā?
> *Can you the day after?*

> **ब:** आम्, परश्वः शक्नोमि!
> B: ām, paraśvaḥ śaknomi!
> *Yes, the day after I can!*

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **अ:** त्वं संस्कृतं वक्तुं शक्नोषि वा?
> A: tvaṃ saṃskṛtaṃ vaktuṃ śaknoṣi vā?
> *Can you speak Sanskrit?*

> **ब:** किञ्चित् शक्नोमि। त्वम्?
> B: kiñcit śaknomi. tvam?
> *A little I can. You?*

> **अ:** अहम् अपि किञ्चित् शक्नोमि। आवां सह अभ्यासं करवावः!
> A: aham api kiñcit śaknomi. āvāṃ saha abhyāsaṃ karavāvaḥ!
> *I can a little too. Let's practice together!*

## Variations

1. **śaknomi alone as a complete answer:** Just like "I can" in English, you can answer *śaknomi* (I can) or *na śaknomi* (I can't) without repeating the infinitive. Very natural.

2. **pārayāmi as an alternative:** The verb **pṛ** (pārayāmi) also means "I am able." *ahaṃ kartuṃ pārayāmi* = "I am able to do it." More formal, but good to recognize.

3. **Common infinitives to memorize:** kartum (to do), gantum (to go), vaktum (to speak), khāditum (to eat), paṭhitum (to read), lekhitum (to write), gātum (to sing), draṣṭum (to see), śrotum (to hear).

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Don't forget -tum:** The infinitive must end in *-tum*. You can't say *ahaṃ gacchāmi śaknomi* (mashing two conjugated verbs together). It's *ahaṃ gantuṃ śaknomi* — one infinitive + one conjugated verb.

2. **śaknomi vs. śaknoṣi:** Watch your endings. *śaknomi* = I can, *śaknoṣi* = you can. Mixing these up changes who is able to do the thing.
