# Stage 28: Reported Speech

**Pattern: saḥ avadat yat... / ...iti saḥ avadat**

---

## The Pattern

Sanskrit gives you **two ways** to report what someone said:

| Method | Template | Style |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Direct (with *iti*) | **"..."** ***iti*** **saḥ avadat** | Classic, literary |
| Indirect (with *yat*) | **saḥ avadat** ***yat...*** | Conversational |

Think of *iti* as a closing quotation mark that comes AFTER the quote. Think of *yat* as the English word "that" in "he said that..."

---

## How It Works

**Direct speech with *iti*:** You put the exact words first, then *iti*, then the speaking verb. The quoted words stay exactly as the speaker said them — first person, present tense, everything. "ahaṃ gacchāmi" iti saḥ avadat = He said, "I am going."

**Indirect speech with *yat*:** You start with the speaking verb, add *yat*, then rephrase. Pronouns shift (aham → saḥ), and the perspective changes to the reporter's viewpoint. saḥ avadat yat saḥ gacchati = He said that he goes. This is the more natural pattern in everyday Sanskrit conversation.

For **reported questions**, use *kim* after the asking verb: saḥ apṛcchat kim tvaṃ gacchasi? = He asked whether you are going.

---

## Example Phrases

1. **"अहं श्वः आगमिष्यामि" इति सा अवदत्**
   "ahaṃ śvaḥ āgamiṣyāmi" iti sā avadat
   *She said, "I will come tomorrow."*

2. **सः अवदत् यत् सः श्वः आगमिष्यति**
   saḥ avadat yat saḥ śvaḥ āgamiṣyati
   *He said that he would come tomorrow.*

3. **श्रुतं मया यत् परीक्षा स्थगिता**
   śrutaṃ mayā yat parīkṣā sthagitā
   *I heard that the exam is postponed.*

4. **"किं भवान् सिद्धः?" इति सा अपृच्छत्**
   "kiṃ bhavān siddhaḥ?" iti sā apṛcchat
   *She asked, "Are you ready?"*

5. **ते वदन्ति यत् वृष्टिः आगमिष्यति**
   te vadanti yat vṛṣṭiḥ āgamiṣyati
   *They say that rain will come.*

6. **तस्य मते एतत् कार्यं कठिनम् अस्ति**
   tasya mate etat kāryaṃ kaṭhinam asti
   *In his opinion, this work is difficult.*

7. **"मम सहायं कुरु" इति बालः अक्रन्दत्**
   "mama sahāyaṃ kuru" iti bālaḥ akrandat
   *The boy cried, "Help me!"*

8. **सः अवदत् यत् आगमिष्यति किन्तु न आगच्छत्**
   saḥ avadat yat āgamiṣyati kintu na āgacchat
   *He said he would come but didn't come.*

9. **गुरुः अपृच्छत् किं छात्राः पाठं पठितवन्तः**
   guruḥ apṛcchat kiṃ chātrāḥ pāṭhaṃ paṭhitavantaḥ
   *The teacher asked whether the students had read the lesson.*

10. **"सर्वं कुशलम्" इति सः प्रत्यवदत्**
    "sarvaṃ kuśalam" iti saḥ pratyavadat
    *He replied, "All is well."*

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: The rumor**

**राहुलः:** श्रुतं मया यत् नूतनः आचार्यः आगतः। किम् एतत् सत्यम्?
rāhulaḥ: śrutaṃ mayā yat nūtanaḥ ācāryaḥ āgataḥ. kim etat satyam?
*Rahul: I heard that a new teacher has arrived. Is this true?*

**मीरा:** आम्, सीता अवदत् यत् सः अद्य प्रथमं पाठम् अपाठयत्।
mīrā: ām, sītā avadat yat saḥ adya prathamaṃ pāṭham apāṭhayat.
*Meera: Yes, Sita said that he taught the first lesson today.*

**राहुलः:** "सः अतीव रोचते" इति कश्चित् अवदत्।
rāhulaḥ: "saḥ atīva rocate" iti kaścit avadat.
*Rahul: Someone said, "He is very likeable."*

---

**Dialogue 2: Broken promise**

**अनिलः:** किम् अभवत्? रामः कुत्र अस्ति?
anilaḥ: kim abhavat? rāmaḥ kutra asti?
*Anil: What happened? Where is Ram?*

**सुमा:** सः अवदत् यत् अष्टवादने आगमिष्यति। किन्तु इदानीं दशवादनम्, सः न आगच्छत्।
sumā: saḥ avadat yat aṣṭavādane āgamiṣyati. kintu idānīṃ daśavādanam, saḥ na āgacchat.
*Suma: He said he would come at eight. But now it's ten, and he didn't come.*

**अनिलः:** "मा चिन्तयतु" इति सः सदा वदति, किन्तु सदा विलम्बते!
anilaḥ: "mā cintayatu" iti saḥ sadā vadati, kintu sadā vilambate!
*Anil: He always says "don't worry," but he's always late!*

## Variations

- **mama mate** / **tasya mate** / **teṣāṃ mate** — "in my / his / their opinion" — a softer way to attribute a view without direct quotation.
- **kathayati yat...** — using *kathayati* (tells/narrates) instead of *vadati* (says) for storytelling contexts.
- **iti kila** — "so they say" / "reportedly" — *kila* adds hearsay flavor, signaling you're not vouching for the truth.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Putting *iti* before the quote.** In Sanskrit, *iti* is a CLOSER, not an opener. The quote always comes first: ✓ "gacchāmi" iti saḥ avadat. ✗ iti "gacchāmi" saḥ avadat.

2. **Forgetting to shift pronouns in indirect speech.** If Ram said "I (aham) will go," your indirect report should be "Ram said that *he* (saḥ) will go" — just like English. Direct with *iti* keeps the original pronouns; indirect with *yat* shifts them.
