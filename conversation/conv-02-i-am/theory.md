# Stage 2: I Am / You Are

**Pattern Name:** aham ... asmi / tvam ... asi / saḥ-sā ... asti

---

## The Pattern

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| अहम् X अस्मि | aham X asmi | I am X |
| त्वम् Y असि | tvam Y asi | You are Y |
| सः Z अस्ति | saḥ Z asti | He is Z |
| सा Z अस्ति | sā Z asti | She is Z |

Point at yourself — **aham** + **asmi**. Point at the person you're talking to — **tvam** + **asi**. Point at someone else — **saḥ** (he) or **sā** (she) + **asti**.

---

## How It Works

The verb "to be" (**as**) changes its ending depending on who you're talking about. Think of it like snapping a different tail onto the same root: **asmi** (I am), **asi** (you are), **asti** (he/she/it is). The pronoun tells you who, the verb ending confirms it.

When describing yourself or someone else, the describing word often adjusts for gender. A hungry man is **kṣudhitaḥ**, a hungry woman is **kṣudhitā**. For now, just notice the pattern: **-aḥ** for masculine, **-ā** for feminine. Neuter descriptions (like occupations) often stay the same.

---

## Example Phrases

1. अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।
   aham chātraḥ asmi.
   "I am a student." (male speaker)

2. अहम् छात्रा अस्मि।
   aham chātrā asmi.
   "I am a student." (female speaker)

3. त्वम् कः असि?
   tvam kaḥ asi?
   "Who are you?" (to a male)

4. त्वम् सुखी असि?
   tvam sukhī asi?
   "Are you happy?" (to a female)

5. सः वैद्यः अस्ति।
   saḥ vaidyaḥ asti.
   "He is a doctor."

6. सा अध्यापिका अस्ति।
   sā adhyāpikā asti.
   "She is a teacher."

7. अहम् क्षुधितः अस्मि।
   aham kṣudhitaḥ asmi.
   "I am hungry." (male)

8. अहम् श्रान्तः अस्मि।
   aham śrāntaḥ asmi.
   "I am tired." (male)

9. त्वम् सिद्धः असि।
   tvam siddhaḥ asi.
   "You are ready." (to a male)

10. सा प्रसन्ना अस्ति।
    sā prasannā asti.
    "She is happy."

---

## Mini-Dialogue 1

**रामः (rāmaḥ):** त्वम् कः असि?
tvam kaḥ asi?
"Who are you?"

**कृष्णः (kṛṣṇaḥ):** अहम् कृष्णः अस्मि। अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।
aham kṛṣṇaḥ asmi. aham chātraḥ asmi.
"I am Krishna. I am a student."

**रामः:** अहम् रामः अस्मि। अहम् अध्यापकः अस्मि।
aham rāmaḥ asmi. aham adhyāpakaḥ asmi.
"I am Rama. I am a teacher."

## Mini-Dialogue 2

**सीता (sītā):** त्वम् कुशलिनी असि?
tvam kuśalinī asi?
"Are you well?" (to a female)

**गीता (gītā):** आम्, अहम् कुशलिनी अस्मि। त्वम्?
ām, aham kuśalinī asmi. tvam?
"Yes, I am well. You?"

**सीता:** अहम् किञ्चित् श्रान्ता अस्मि।
aham kiñcit śrāntā asmi.
"I am a little tired."

**गीता:** सः कः अस्ति?
saḥ kaḥ asti?
"Who is he?"

**सीता:** सः मम भ्राता अस्ति।
saḥ mama bhrātā asti.
"He is my brother."

---

## Variations

1. **Dropping the pronoun:** Because the verb ending already tells you who, you can drop **aham** and just say **chātraḥ asmi** — "Am a student." Natural and common.

2. **Plural:** For "we are," use **vayam ... smaḥ**. For "you all are," use **yūyam ... stha**. For "they are," use **te ... santi**.

3. **Negative:** Slip in **na** — **aham chātraḥ na asmi** — "I am not a student."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using asti for everyone.** Remember: **asmi** = I, **asi** = you, **asti** = he/she/it. The ending must match the person.

2. **Ignoring gender on adjectives.** If you're female, say **śrāntā asmi** (not **śrāntaḥ asmi**). The **-aḥ / -ā** distinction matters.
