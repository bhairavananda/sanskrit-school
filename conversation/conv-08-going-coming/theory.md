# Stage 8: Going & Coming

## The Pattern

**aham tatra gacchāmi** — "I'm going there"

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| अहम् तत्र गच्छामि | aham tatra gacchāmi | I'm going there |
| अत्र आगच्छ | atra āgaccha | Come here |
| आवां गच्छावः | āvāṃ gacchāvaḥ | Let's go (us two) |
| वयम् गच्छामः | vayam gacchāmaḥ | Let's go (us all) |

---

## How It Works

The root **gam** (to go) and **āgam** (to come) are your movement verbs. "Come" is literally "come-toward" — the **ā-** prefix flips direction. You already know **atra** (here) and **tatra** (there); add **kutra** (where?) and you can navigate anywhere.

Here's the key conjugation of **gam** you need: **gacchāmi** (I go), **gacchasi** (you go), **gacchati** (he/she goes), **gacchāvaḥ** (we two go), **gacchāmaḥ** (we all go). The dual form **gacchāvaḥ** is perfect for "let's go" when you're talking to one person. When there's a group, use **gacchāmaḥ**. The imperative "go!" is **gaccha**, and "come!" is **āgaccha**.

---

## Example Phrases

1. अहम् गृहं गच्छामि।
   aham gṛhaṃ gacchāmi.
   *I'm going home.*

2. त्वम् कुत्र गच्छसि?
   tvam kutra gacchasi?
   *Where are you going?*

3. सः आपणं गच्छति।
   saḥ āpaṇaṃ gacchati.
   *He is going to the shop.*

4. अत्र आगच्छ!
   atra āgaccha!
   *Come here!*

5. आवां तत्र गच्छावः।
   āvāṃ tatra gacchāvaḥ.
   *Let's go there (us two).*

6. वयम् उद्यानं गच्छामः।
   vayam udyānaṃ gacchāmaḥ.
   *Let's go to the garden.*

7. सा कुत्र गच्छति?
   sā kutra gacchati?
   *Where is she going?*

8. अहम् तत्र न गच्छामि।
   aham tatra na gacchāmi.
   *I'm not going there.*

9. शीघ्रम् आगच्छ!
   śīghram āgaccha!
   *Come quickly!*

10. अत्र आगच्छतु।
    atra āgacchatu.
    *Let him/her come here.* (polite)

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **अ:** त्वम् कुत्र गच्छसि?
> A: tvam kutra gacchasi?
> *Where are you going?*

> **ब:** अहम् आपणं गच्छामि। त्वम् आगच्छसि वा?
> B: aham āpaṇaṃ gacchāmi. tvam āgacchasi vā?
> *I'm going to the shop. Are you coming?*

> **अ:** आम्, आवां गच्छावः!
> A: ām, āvāṃ gacchāvaḥ!
> *Yes, let's go (the two of us)!*

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **अ:** सीता कुत्र गच्छति?
> A: sītā kutra gacchati?
> *Where is Sita going?*

> **ब:** सा गृहं गच्छति।
> B: sā gṛhaṃ gacchati.
> *She's going home.*

> **अ:** अहम् अपि गृहं गच्छामि। वयं सर्वे गच्छामः!
> A: aham api gṛhaṃ gacchāmi. vayaṃ sarve gacchāmaḥ!
> *I'm also going home. Let's all go!*

---

## Variations

1. **gacchatu / āgacchatu** — The polite imperative ("please go" / "please come"). Use this with elders or strangers instead of the blunt *gaccha*.

2. **saḥ āgacchati** — "He is coming." You can use *āgam* in regular present tense, not just as a command. *sā āgacchati* = "She is coming."

3. **kutra gantavyam?** — "Where should one go?" This uses the future passive participle for a "should/must" sense. A handy one-liner for asking directions.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **gacchāmi vs. gacchāmaḥ:** Don't mix up "I go" (*gacchāmi* — just me) with "we go" (*gacchāmaḥ* — all of us). That final **-aḥ** makes it plural.

2. **āgaccha means "come," not "go":** The prefix **ā-** reverses direction. *gaccha* = go away from me; *āgaccha* = come toward me. If you tell someone *gaccha* when you mean "come here," you're sending them away!
