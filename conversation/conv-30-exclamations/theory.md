# Stage 30: Exclamations & Reactions

**Pattern: aho! / sādhu! / adbhutam! / dhik! / hā hanta!**

---

## The Pattern

Exclamations are the **emotional heartbeat** of conversation. Drop these in naturally and you stop sounding like a textbook and start sounding like a person.

| Exclamation | IAST | Emotion |
|-------------|------|---------|
| Wow! / Oh! | aho! | Wonder, surprise |
| Bravo! Well done! | sādhu! sādhu! | Praise, approval |
| Amazing! | adbhutam! | Amazement |
| Shame! Alas! | dhik! | Disgust, contempt |
| Alas! Oh no! | hā! / hā hanta! | Grief, distress |
| Hey! Ho! | bhoḥ! | Calling attention |
| Excellent! | śobhanam! | Approval, delight |
| What a pity! | kaṣṭam! | Sympathy, pain |
| Long live! Bless you! | ciraṃ jīva! | Blessing, celebration |
| Impossible! No way! | na śakyam! / asambhavam! | Disbelief |

---

## How It Works

Most of these are **frozen forms** — you don't conjugate or decline them, you just throw them into conversation as-is. Grammatically, they fall into three buckets:

**Accusative neuters** used as exclamatory expressions: *adbhutam*, *kaṣṭam*, *śobhanam*, *asambhavam*. These are technically "that (is) amazing / painful / excellent / impossible" with the verb *asti* dropped. You already know this pattern — it's just an adjective in the neuter accusative standing alone.

**Indeclinables** (avyaya): *aho*, *dhik*, *hā*, *hanta*, *bhoḥ* — these never change form. *Bhoḥ* is the vocative particle, used before names or titles to call someone: *bhoḥ rāma!* = "Hey Ram!" *Dhik* takes the accusative of the person you're shaming: *dhik tvām!* = "Shame on you!"

**Imperative verbs** used as blessings: *ciraṃ jīva* = "live long" (*jīva* is the imperative of *jīv*).

## Example Phrases

1. **अहो! किम् एतत् सत्यम्?**
   aho! kim etat satyam?
   *Wow! Is this true?*

2. **साधु साधु! सुन्दरं गीतम्**
   sādhu sādhu! sundaraṃ gītam
   *Bravo! Beautiful song!*

3. **अद्भुतम्! त्वं परीक्षायाम् उत्तीर्णः!**
   adbhutam! tvaṃ parīkṣāyām uttīrṇaḥ!
   *Amazing! You passed the exam!*

4. **धिक् त्वाम्! त्वं मिथ्या अवदः**
   dhik tvām! tvaṃ mithyā avadaḥ
   *Shame on you! You lied!*

5. **हा हन्त! सः गतवान्**
   hā hanta! saḥ gatavān
   *Alas! He is gone.*

6. **भोः राम! अत्र आगच्छ**
   bhoḥ rāma! atra āgaccha
   *Hey Ram! Come here!*

7. **शोभनम्! तव उत्तरं सम्यक् अस्ति**
   śobhanam! tava uttaraṃ samyak asti
   *Excellent! Your answer is correct.*

8. **कष्टम्! तस्य पिता रुग्णः अस्ति**
   kaṣṭam! tasya pitā rugṇaḥ asti
   *What a pity! His father is ill.*

9. **चिरं जीव, पुत्र!**
   ciraṃ jīva, putra!
   *Long live, son! / Bless you, son!*

10. **असम्भवम्! सः एतत् कर्तुं न शक्नोति**
    asambhavam! saḥ etat kartuṃ na śaknoti
    *Impossible! He cannot do this!*

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Great news**

**सीता:** अद्य मम परीक्षा-फलम् आगतम्। अहम् उत्तीर्णा!
sītā: adya mama parīkṣā-phalam āgatam. aham uttīrṇā!
*Sita: My exam results came today. I passed!*

**रामः:** अद्भुतम्! साधु साधु! किं प्रथमा श्रेणी?
rāmaḥ: adbhutam! sādhu sādhu! kiṃ prathamā śreṇī?
*Ram: Amazing! Bravo! First rank?*

**सीता:** आम्, प्रथमा श्रेणी!
sītā: ām, prathamā śreṇī!
*Sita: Yes, first rank!*

**रामः:** अहो! शोभनम्! चिरं जीव!
rāmaḥ: aho! śobhanam! ciraṃ jīva!
*Ram: Oh! Excellent! Bless you!*

---

**Dialogue 2: Bad news**

**अनिलः:** श्रुतं किम्? रोहितस्य गृहे चौर्यम् अभवत्।
anilaḥ: śrutaṃ kim? rohitasya gṛhe cauryam abhavat.
*Anil: Did you hear? There was a theft at Rohit's house.*

**प्रिया:** हा हन्त! कष्टम्! सः कुशली अस्ति किम्?
priyā: hā hanta! kaṣṭam! saḥ kuśalī asti kim?
*Priya: Oh no! How awful! Is he okay?*

**अनिलः:** आम्, सर्वे कुशलिनः, किन्तु बहु धनं हृतम्।
anilaḥ: ām, sarve kuśalinaḥ, kintu bahu dhanaṃ hṛtam.
*Anil: Yes, everyone is safe, but a lot of money was stolen.*

**प्रिया:** धिक् एतत्! असम्भवम्!
priyā: dhik etat! asambhavam!
*Priya: How terrible! Unbelievable!*

## Variations

- **aho bata!** — intensified "oh!" expressing deep wonder or sorrow (bata amplifies any emotion).
- **sādhu uktam!** — "well said!" — a refined way to praise someone's words specifically.
- **diṣṭyā!** — "by good fortune! / thank goodness!" — used when something good happens after worry or danger.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Overusing one exclamation for everything.** Just like you wouldn't say "wow" for both good news and bad news in English, don't use *aho* for everything. Match the exclamation to the emotion: *śobhanam* for praise, *kaṣṭam* for pity, *dhik* for disgust.

2. **Forgetting that *dhik* takes the accusative.** It's *dhik tvām* (shame on you), not *dhik tvaṃ*. The person being shamed goes in the accusative case.
