# Stage 17: When & While — Workbook Answers

---

## Section A: Translate to Sanskrit

1. yadā ghaṇṭā vādyate tadā kakṣā ārabhate.
2. rātri-bhojana-samaye parivāraḥ saha upaviśati.
3. yāvat bālāḥ krīḍanti tāvat mātā pacati.
4. yatra vṛkṣāḥ tatra chāyā.
5. yadā yadā saṅgītaṃ śṛṇomi tadā tadā sukhī bhavāmi.

---

## Section B: Translate to English

1. When it becomes night, then the stars are seen.
2. At morning time, the air is cool.
3. While the teacher speaks, the students listen.
4. Where there is smoke, there is fire.
5. Whenever she laughs, everyone laughs.

## Section D: Sample Mini-Dialogues

**1. Morning routines:**

— tvaṃ prātaḥ kadā uttiṣṭhasi?
  When do you wake up in the morning?

— yadā ghaṇṭā ṣaṭ bhavati tadā uttiṣṭhāmi. prātaḥ-snāna-samaye mantram paṭhāmi. tvaṃ ca?
  When it becomes six o'clock, I wake up. At bath time, I recite a mantra. And you?

— aham pañcavādane uttiṣṭhāmi. yadā sūryaḥ udeti tadā yogābhyāsaṃ karomi.
  I wake up at five. When the sun rises, I do yoga practice.

— sundaram! prātaḥ-bhojana-samaye kiṃ khādasi?
  Lovely! What do you eat at breakfast time?

**2. Favourite place:**

— mama priyaṃ sthānaṃ parvataḥ.
  My favourite place is the mountain.

— yatra parvataḥ tatra śāntiḥ, nanu?
  Where there are mountains, there is peace, right?

— āṃ! yāvat ahaṃ tatra vasāmi tāvat sarvāṇi duḥkhāni vismarāmi.
  Yes! While I stay there, I forget all sorrows.

— yatra vṛkṣāḥ ca nadyaḥ ca tatra svargaḥ eva.
  Where there are trees and rivers, there is heaven itself.

## Section E: Sample Responses

**1. Typical evening:**

yadā kāryaṃ samāpyate tadā gṛhaṃ gacchāmi. sandhyā-samaye dīpaṃ jvālayāmi mantram ca paṭhāmi. yāvat bhojanaṃ pacati tāvat pustakaṃ paṭhāmi. yadā rātriḥ bhavati tadā parivāreṇa saha kathāṃ karomi.

(When work finishes, I go home. At twilight I light a lamp and recite a mantra. While the food cooks, I read a book. When it becomes night, I converse with the family.)

**2. Custom yatra...tatra proverb:**

*yatra hāsyaṃ tatra ārogyam.* — Where there is laughter, there is health.

This means that humour and joy are themselves a form of medicine. A place filled with laughter tends to be a place where people are well — not just in body, but in spirit. The *yatra...tatra* construction compresses a whole philosophy into six syllables.
