# Stage 17: When & While

## The Pattern

**yadā X tadā Y** — "when X, then Y"
**X-samaye** — "at the time of X"
**yāvat X tāvat Y** — "while X, Y" / "as long as X, Y"
**yatra X tatra Y** — "where X, there Y"

Time and place — the frame around every story. With these correlative pairs, you can schedule, narrate, and describe the world in motion.

---

## How It Works

You have already met correlative pairs (*yataḥ...tataḥ*, *yadi...tarhi*). Now you get the temporal and spatial ones. **Yadā...tadā** is the workhorse for "when...then." It covers single events ("when he arrived, I left"), habitual actions ("when I wake, I drink water"), and future plans ("when summer comes, we'll travel").

**Yāvat...tāvat** is for duration — "as long as / while." *Yāvat* sets up a span of time, and *tāvat* tells you what fills it. This pair can also mean "until": *yāvat sūryaḥ na udeti tāvat andhakāraḥ* — "until the sun rises, there is darkness."

**X-samaye** uses the locative case of *samaya* ("time/occasion") to mark a point in time: *bhojana-samaye* = "at mealtime." It's a compact, elegant alternative to a full *yadā* clause.

**Yatra...tatra** gives you spatial correlation: "where X, there Y." Handy for describing places, giving directions, and making philosophical observations ("where there is dharma, there is victory").

## Example Phrases

1. यदा सूर्यः उदेति तदा पक्षिणः गायन्ति।
   *yadā sūryaḥ udeti tadā pakṣiṇaḥ gāyanti.*
   When the sun rises, then the birds sing.

2. भोजनसमये मौनं पालयतु।
   *bhojana-samaye maunaṃ pālayatu.*
   At mealtime, maintain silence.

3. यावत् अहं पठामि तावत् त्वं क्रीड।
   *yāvat ahaṃ paṭhāmi tāvat tvaṃ krīḍa.*
   While I study, you play.

4. यत्र विद्या तत्र विनयः।
   *yatra vidyā tatra vinayaḥ.*
   Where there is knowledge, there is humility.

5. यदा गुरुः आगच्छति तदा सर्वे उत्तिष्ठन्ति।
   *yadā guruḥ āgacchati tadā sarve uttiṣṭhanti.*
   When the teacher arrives, then everyone stands.

6. सन्ध्यासमये दीपं ज्वालयामि।
   *sandhyā-samaye dīpaṃ jvālayāmi.*
   At twilight, I light a lamp.

7. यावत् जीवं तावत् पठेत्।
   *yāvat jīvaṃ tāvat paṭhet.*
   As long as one lives, one should study.

8. यत्र नदी तत्र हरितम्।
   *yatra nadī tatra haritam.*
   Where there is a river, there is greenery.

9. यदा वर्षा समाप्ता तदा वयं बहिः गच्छामः।
   *yadā varṣā samāptā tadā vayaṃ bahiḥ gacchāmaḥ.*
   When the rain stops, then we go out.

10. परीक्षासमये शान्तिः आवश्यकी।
    *parīkṣā-samaye śāntiḥ āvaśyakī.*
    At exam time, calmness is necessary.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Daily routine**

— त्वं प्रातः कदा उत्तिष्ठसि?
  *tvaṃ prātaḥ kadā uttiṣṭhasi?*
  When do you wake up in the morning?

— यदा सूर्यः उदेति तदा उत्तिष्ठामि।
  *yadā sūryaḥ udeti tadā uttiṣṭhāmi.*
  When the sun rises, then I wake up.

— प्रातःभोजनसमये किं खादसि?
  *prātaḥbhojana-samaye kiṃ khādasi?*
  What do you eat at breakfast time?

— यावत् भोजनं पचति तावत् चायं पिबामि।
  *yāvat bhojanaṃ pacati tāvat cāyaṃ pibāmi.*
  While the food cooks, I drink tea.

**Dialogue 2: Describing a place**

— तव ग्रामः कीदृशः?
  *tava grāmaḥ kīdṛśaḥ?*
  What is your village like?

— यत्र मम ग्रामः तत्र सुन्दरा नदी अस्ति।
  *yatra mama grāmaḥ tatra sundarā nadī asti.*
  Where my village is, there is a beautiful river.

— यदा त्वं तत्र गच्छसि तदा किं करोषि?
  *yadā tvaṃ tatra gacchasi tadā kiṃ karoṣi?*
  When you go there, what do you do?

— सन्ध्यासमये नद्यां स्नानं करोमि। शान्तिः एव शान्तिः।
  *sandhyā-samaye nadyāṃ snānaṃ karomi. śāntiḥ eva śāntiḥ.*
  At twilight I bathe in the river. Just peace.

## Variations

1. **yasmin samaye...tasmin samaye** — A more explicit "at which time...at that time," using locative pronouns:
   *yasmin samaye sā prāviśat tasmin samaye sarve atuṣyan.* — At the time she entered, everyone was delighted.

2. **yāvat...na...tāvat** for "until":
   *yāvat saḥ na āgacchati tāvat pratīkṣāmahe.* — Until he comes, we wait.

3. **yadā yadā...tadā tadā** for "whenever...always":
   *yadā yadā duḥkhaṃ bhavati tadā tadā dhairyaṃ dhārayāmi.* — Whenever sorrow comes, I always hold courage.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing *yadā* (when) with *yadi* (if):** These are different constructions. *Yadā* describes something that happens or will happen in time. *Yadi* introduces a condition that may or may not be true. "When summer comes" (certain, temporal) = *yadā*. "If summer comes early" (uncertain, conditional) = *yadi*.

2. **Dropping the correlative partner:** Each *yadā* wants a *tadā*, each *yāvat* wants a *tāvat*, each *yatra* wants a *tatra*. Dropping the second word is like leaving a sentence hanging. In casual speech you can sometimes get away with it, but while learning, always close the pair.
