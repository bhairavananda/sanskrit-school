# Stage 26: Still / Already / No Longer / Not Yet

**Pattern: adhunāpi · pūrvam eva · na punaḥ · na yāvat**

---

## The Pattern

Four temporal state markers that let you talk about how things change over time:

> **adhunāpi / idānīm api** (अधुनापि / इदानीम् अपि) = still (even now)
> **pūrvam eva / prāg eva** (पूर्वम् एव / प्राग् एव) = already (before + indeed)
> **na punaḥ / na adhunā** (न पुनः / न अधुना) = no longer, not anymore
> **na yāvat / na tāvat** (न यावत् / न तावत्) = not yet

## How It Works

Notice something? The particles from Stage 25 are doing the heavy lifting here. **adhunāpi** is just adhunā (now) + api (even/also) — literally "even now," which is exactly what "still" means. **pūrvam eva** is pūrvam (before) + eva (indeed) — "before itself," i.e., "already." These aren't new grammar — they're Stage 25 particles combined with time words.

**na punaḥ** (not again) and **na adhunā** (not now) both express "no longer." **na yāvat** (not as far as / not until) and **na tāvat** (not so much / not until then) express "not yet." These four markers cover any situation where something has changed, hasn't changed, ended, or hasn't started.

## Example Phrases

1. त्वम् अधुनापि खादसि किम्? (tvam adhunāpi khādasi kim?)
   — "Are you still eating?"

2. अहं पूर्वम् एव समापितवान्। (ahaṃ pūrvam eva samāpitavān.)
   — "I already finished." *(masc. speaker)*

3. सः न पुनः अत्र वसति। (saḥ na punaḥ atra vasati.)
   — "He no longer lives here."

4. सा न यावत् आगता। (sā na yāvat āgatā.)
   — "She hasn't arrived yet."

5. अहम् अधुनापि पठामि। (aham adhunāpi paṭhāmi.)
   — "I'm still learning."

6. भोजनं पूर्वम् एव शीतलम्। (bhojanaṃ pūrvam eva śītalam.)
   — "The food is already cold."

7. तौ न पुनः परस्परं वदतः। (tau na punaḥ parasparaṃ vadataḥ.)
   — "Those two no longer speak to each other."

8. वर्षा न तावत् आरब्धा। (varṣā na tāvat ārabdhā.)
   — "The rain hasn't started yet."

9. किं त्वम् अधुनापि कुप्यसि? (kiṃ tvam adhunāpi kupyasi?)
   — "Are you still angry?"

10. सा प्राग् एव प्रस्थिता। (sā prāg eva prasthitā.)
    — "She has already departed."

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **माता** — भोजनं समाप्तं किम्? (bhojanaṃ samāptaṃ kim?)
> "Is the food finished?"
>
> **पुत्रः** — न तावत्। अधुनापि पचामि। (na tāvat. adhunāpi pacāmi.)
> "Not yet. I'm still cooking."
>
> **माता** — अतिथिः पूर्वम् एव आगतः! (atithiḥ pūrvam eva āgataḥ!)
> "The guest has already arrived!"
>
> **पुत्रः** — अहो! शीघ्रं करोमि। (aho! śīghraṃ karomi.)
> "Oh! I'll do it quickly."

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **छात्रः** — गुरुः आगतः किम्? (guruḥ āgataḥ kim?)
> "Has the teacher come?"
>
> **मित्रम्** — न यावत्। किन्तु सः खलु सदा समये आगच्छति। (na yāvat. kintu saḥ khalu sadā samaye āgacchati.)
> "Not yet. But he certainly always comes on time."
>
> **छात्रः** — अहं तु अधुनापि पाठं न पठितवान्! (ahaṃ tu adhunāpi pāṭhaṃ na paṭhitavān!)
> "But I still haven't read the lesson!"
>
> **मित्रम्** — तर्हि अधुना एव पठ! (tarhi adhunā eva paṭha!)
> "Then read it right now!"

## Variations

- **idānīm api** (इदानीम् अपि) is a synonym for adhunāpi — both mean "still." idānīm is slightly more literary.
- **prāg eva** (प्राग् एव) is a synonym for pūrvam eva — both mean "already." prāk is a bit more formal.
- **na punar eva** (न पुनर् एव) intensifies "no longer" — "never again," "absolutely not anymore."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Forgetting that adhunāpi is one compound idea.** Don't separate the meaning — adhunā api doesn't mean "now also" in two pieces. Together it means "still." Context will usually make this clear, but keep the compound sense in your head.

2. **Mixing up na yāvat and na punaḥ.** "Not yet" (na yāvat) implies it WILL happen. "No longer" (na punaḥ) implies it WON'T happen again. "She hasn't come yet" = she's expected. "She no longer comes" = she's done coming.
