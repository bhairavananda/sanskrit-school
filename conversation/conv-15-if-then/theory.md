# Stage 15: If & Then

## The Pattern

**yadi X tarhi Y** — "if X, then Y"
**yadi X syāt tarhi Y syāt** — "if X were to be, then Y would be"
**cet** — enclitic "if"

You now have the power to talk about possibilities, hypotheticals, warnings, and promises. This is where conversations get interesting.

---

## How It Works

The basic conditional uses another correlative pair: **yadi** ("if") opens the condition, and **tarhi** ("then") introduces the result. For straightforward, real-world conditions — "if it rains, I'll stay home" — just use the normal verb forms you already know.

For hypothetical or unlikely scenarios, Sanskrit uses the **optative mood** (also called the potential mood). The key verb here is **syāt** — the optative of *as* ("to be"), meaning "would be / might be / should be." When you see *yadi...syāt...tarhi...syāt*, you know you're in "what if" territory. Other verbs also have optative forms: *gacchet* ("would go"), *kuryāt* ("would do"), *vadet* ("would say").

The enclitic **cet** is a compact alternative to *yadi*. Like *hi*, it sits in second position — after the first word of its clause. *Tvaṃ cet āgacchasi* = "if you come."

## Example Phrases

1. यदि वर्षा भवति तर्हि अहं गृहे तिष्ठामि।
   *yadi varṣā bhavati tarhi ahaṃ gṛhe tiṣṭhāmi.*
   If it rains, then I stay at home.

2. यदि त्वं पठेः तर्हि सफलः भवेः।
   *yadi tvaṃ paṭheḥ tarhi saphalaḥ bhaveḥ.*
   If you were to study, you would succeed.

3. त्वं चेत् आगच्छसि तर्हि आनन्दः भवति।
   *tvaṃ cet āgacchasi tarhi ānandaḥ bhavati.*
   If you come, then there is joy.

4. यदि धनं स्यात् तर्हि गृहं क्रीणीयाम्।
   *yadi dhanaṃ syāt tarhi gṛhaṃ krīṇīyām.*
   If there were money, I would buy a house.

5. यदि सः आगच्छेत् तर्हि कथयामि।
   *yadi saḥ āgacchet tarhi kathayāmi.*
   If he comes (were to come), I will tell.

6. यदि भोजनं न भवति तर्हि बहिः गच्छामः।
   *yadi bhojanaṃ na bhavati tarhi bahiḥ gacchāmaḥ.*
   If there is no food, then we go out.

7. समयः चेत् अस्ति तर्हि पुस्तकं पठ।
   *samayaḥ cet asti tarhi pustakaṃ paṭha.*
   If there is time, then read the book.

8. यदि अहं पक्षी स्याम् तर्हि आकाशे उड्डयेयम्।
   *yadi ahaṃ pakṣī syām tarhi ākāśe uḍḍayeyam.*
   If I were a bird, I would fly in the sky.

9. यदि श्वः वृष्टिः न भवति तर्हि वयं वनं गच्छामः।
   *yadi śvaḥ vṛṣṭiḥ na bhavati tarhi vayaṃ vanaṃ gacchāmaḥ.*
   If it doesn't rain tomorrow, then we go to the forest.

10. यदि कृपया पृच्छसि तर्हि जनाः साहाय्यं कुर्वन्ति।
    *yadi kṛpayā pṛcchasi tarhi janāḥ sāhāyyaṃ kurvanti.*
    If you ask politely, then people help.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Making plans**

— श्वः किं करवाम?
  *śvaḥ kiṃ karavāma?*
  What shall we do tomorrow?

— यदि सूर्यः भवति तर्हि नदीं गच्छामः।
  *yadi sūryaḥ bhavati tarhi nadīṃ gacchāmaḥ.*
  If it's sunny, then we go to the river.

— वर्षा चेत् भवति?
  *varṣā cet bhavati?*
  And if it rains?

— तर्हि गृहे एव पुस्तकानि पठामः!
  *tarhi gṛhe eva pustakāni paṭhāmaḥ!*
  Then we just read books at home!

**Dialogue 2: A hypothetical**

— यदि त्वं राजा स्याः तर्हि किं कुर्याः?
  *yadi tvaṃ rājā syāḥ tarhi kiṃ kuryāḥ?*
  If you were a king, what would you do?

— यदि अहं राजा स्याम् तर्हि सर्वेभ्यः विद्यां ददाम्।
  *yadi ahaṃ rājā syām tarhi sarvebhyaḥ vidyāṃ dadām.*
  If I were a king, I would give education to all.

— सुन्दरम्। तर्हि प्रजाः सुखिन्यः स्युः।
  *sundaram. tarhi prajāḥ sukhinyaḥ syuḥ.*
  Beautiful. Then the people would be happy.

## Variations

1. **yadā...tadā for "whenever":** Don't confuse *yadi* (if) with *yadā* (when/whenever). *Yadā* describes things that actually happen repeatedly; *yadi* describes possibilities. We will cover *yadā...tadā* fully in Stage 17.

2. **cet with negation — nocet:** *Na + cet* contracts to **nocet** ("if not / otherwise"):
   *śīghraṃ gaccha, nocet vilambaḥ bhaviṣyati.* — Go quickly, otherwise there will be a delay.

3. **tarhi dropped:** In casual speech, *tarhi* can be omitted when the meaning is clear:
   *yadi āgacchasi, ānanditaḥ bhavāmi.* — If you come, I'll be delighted.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using indicative verbs in hypotheticals:** "If I were a bird" is unreal — use the optative (*syām*, *uḍḍayeyam*), not the present tense (*asmi*, *uḍḍayāmi*). Indicative + *yadi* implies you think the condition might actually be true.

2. **Placing *cet* first:** Like *hi*, the word *cet* is an enclitic and must come second in its clause. Say *tvaṃ cet gacchasi*, never *cet tvaṃ gacchasi*.
