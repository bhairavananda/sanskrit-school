# Stage 22: Should & Must

## Pattern Name: Obligation, Prohibition & Permission

---

## The Pattern

**tvayā X kartavyam** — "you should do X" (lit. "X is to-be-done by you")
**mayā Y karaṇīyam** — "I must do Y"
**mā kuru** — "don't do (it)"
**X karotu** — "let him do X"

---

## How It Works

Sanskrit flips the English construction on its head. Instead of "you should do X," it says "X is to-be-done by you." The doer goes into the instrumental case (*tvayā* = "by you," *mayā* = "by me"), and the action becomes an adjective ending in *-tavya*, *-anīya*, or *-ya*. All three suffixes mean roughly the same thing: "should/must be done." So *kartavyam* = "to be done," *karaṇīyam* = "to be done," *kāryam* = "to be done." Pick whichever sounds best.

For prohibition ("don't!"), Sanskrit uses *mā* with the verb in a specific form. The simplest version: *mā kuru* ("don't do"), *mā gaccha* ("don't go"). For third-person imperatives ("let him..."), just use the imperative third person: *karotu* ("let him do"), *gacchatu* ("let him go"), *tiṣṭhatu* ("let him stay"). These are gentle commands or permissions -- not barking orders, more like "may he..."

## Example Phrases

1. **त्वया गृहकार्यं कर्तव्यम्।**
   *tvayā gṛhakāryaṃ kartavyam.*
   You should do the homework.

2. **मया अद्य पत्रं लेखनीयम्।**
   *mayā adya patraṃ lekhanīyam.*
   I must write a letter today.

3. **सर्वैः सत्यं वक्तव्यम्।**
   *sarvaiḥ satyaṃ vaktavyam.*
   Everyone should speak the truth.

4. **मा चिन्तां कुरु।**
   *mā cintāṃ kuru.*
   Don't worry.

5. **सः गच्छतु। अहं तिष्ठामि।**
   *saḥ gacchatu. ahaṃ tiṣṭhāmi.*
   Let him go. I'll stay.

6. **त्वया जलं पातव्यम्। बहु उष्णम् अस्ति।**
   *tvayā jalaṃ pātavyam. bahu uṣṇam asti.*
   You should drink water. It is very hot.

7. **एतत् भोजनं न खादनीयम्। तत् पुरातनम् अस्ति।**
   *etat bhojanaṃ na khādanīyam. tat purātanam asti.*
   This food should not be eaten. It is old.

8. **बालकेन औषधं सेवनीयम्।**
   *bālakena auṣadhaṃ sevanīyam.*
   The child must take the medicine.

9. **मा कोपं कुरु। शान्तिं धारय।**
   *mā kopaṃ kuru. śāntiṃ dhāraya.*
   Don't be angry. Maintain peace.

10. **सा पठतु। वयं श्रुणुमः।**
    *sā paṭhatu. vayaṃ śṛṇumaḥ.*
    Let her read. We will listen.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Doctor's advice**

-- वैद्य, मम शिरः पीड्यते। किं कर्तव्यम्?
   *vaidya, mama śiraḥ pīḍyate. kiṃ kartavyam?*
   Doctor, my head hurts. What should I do?

-- त्वया विश्रामः कर्तव्यः। औषधम् अपि सेवनीयम्।
   *tvayā viśrāmaḥ kartavyaḥ. auṣadham api sevanīyam.*
   You should rest. You must also take medicine.

-- मा बहु चिन्तां कुरु। शीघ्रम् एव स्वस्थः भविष्यसि।
   *mā bahu cintāṃ kuru. śīghram eva svasthaḥ bhaviṣyasi.*
   Don't worry too much. You will get well quickly.

**Dialogue 2: What to do about a guest**

-- अतिथिः आगच्छति। किं करणीयम्?
   *atithiḥ āgacchati. kiṃ karaṇīyam?*
   A guest is coming. What should be done?

-- त्वया भोजनं पचनीयम्। रामः गृहं शोधयतु।
   *tvayā bhojanaṃ pacanīyam. rāmaḥ gṛhaṃ śodhayatu.*
   You should cook food. Let Rama clean the house.

-- आम्, सर्वैः मिलित्वा कार्यं कर्तव्यम्।
   *ām, sarvaiḥ militvā kāryaṃ kartavyam.*
   Yes, everyone should work together.

## Variations

1. **Using arha (deserve/ought to) with infinitive:**
   *tvaṃ gantum arhasi* — "You ought to go." (More polite, suggesting worthiness.)

2. **Softened obligation with yuktam (proper/fitting):**
   *yuktaṃ yat tvaṃ paṭhasi* — "It is fitting that you study."

3. **Permissive with astu ("let it be, fine"):**
   *astu, saḥ gacchatu* — "Fine, let him go."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Putting the agent in the nominative:** You must say *tvayā kartavyam* (instrumental), not *tvaṃ kartavyam*. The person who "should" do the thing goes into instrumental because technically the sentence is passive: "the thing is to-be-done by you."

2. **Using mā with present tense:** *Mā* for prohibition properly takes specific verb forms (often the augmentless aorist or imperative). In conversational Sanskrit, *mā kuru* and *mā gaccha* are widely accepted. But avoid *mā karoti* -- that doesn't work. *Mā* goes with second person commands.
