# Stage 11: Knowing & Not Knowing

## The Pattern

**aham jānāmi** — "I know"

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| अहं जानामि | ahaṃ jānāmi | I know |
| अहं न जानामि | ahaṃ na jānāmi | I don't know |
| किं त्वं जानासि? | kiṃ tvaṃ jānāsi? | Do you know? |
| मम न ज्ञातम् | mama na jñātam | I didn't know that |

---

## How It Works

The root **jñā** (to know) gives you one of the most useful verbs in any language. Its present tense forms: **jānāmi** (I know), **jānāsi** (you know), **jānāti** (he/she knows), **jānīmaḥ** (we know). Negate with **na** right before the verb: **na jānāmi** = "I don't know."

The past passive participle **jñātam** means "known" or "was known." When you say **mama na jñātam**, it literally means "it was not known by me" — a natural way to say "I didn't know that" or "I had no idea." You can also ask **kim jānāsi?** to mean "do you know?" — and this doubles as a lead-in for sharing interesting information, just like in English.

---

## Example Phrases

1. अहं जानामि।
   ahaṃ jānāmi.
   *I know.*

2. अहं न जानामि।
   ahaṃ na jānāmi.
   *I don't know.*

3. किं त्वं जानासि?
   kiṃ tvaṃ jānāsi?
   *Do you know?*

4. सः सर्वं जानाति।
   saḥ sarvaṃ jānāti.
   *He knows everything.*

5. मम न ज्ञातम्।
   mama na jñātam.
   *I didn't know that.*

6. अहम् एतत् जानामि।
   aham etat jānāmi.
   *I know this.*

7. त्वं संस्कृतं जानासि वा?
   tvaṃ saṃskṛtaṃ jānāsi vā?
   *Do you know Sanskrit?*

8. सा मार्गं न जानाति।
   sā mārgaṃ na jānāti.
   *She doesn't know the way.*

9. वयं जानीमः।
   vayaṃ jānīmaḥ.
   *We know.*

10. कः जानाति?
    kaḥ jānāti?
    *Who knows?*

## Mini-Dialogue 1

> **अ:** त्वं रामस्य गृहं जानासि वा?
> A: tvaṃ rāmasya gṛhaṃ jānāsi vā?
> *Do you know Rama's house?*

> **ब:** आम्, अहं जानामि। तत्र गच्छावः?
> B: ām, ahaṃ jānāmi. tatra gacchāvaḥ?
> *Yes, I know. Shall we go there?*

> **अ:** आम्, मार्गं दर्शय।
> A: ām, mārgaṃ darśaya.
> *Yes, show the way.*

## Mini-Dialogue 2

> **अ:** किं त्वं जानासि? श्वः परीक्षा अस्ति!
> A: kiṃ tvaṃ jānāsi? śvaḥ parīkṣā asti!
> *Do you know? Tomorrow there's an exam!*

> **ब:** मम न ज्ञातम्! सत्यम्?
> B: mama na jñātam! satyam?
> *I didn't know! Really?*

> **अ:** आम्, सत्यम्। अहम् अपि अद्य एव ज्ञातम्।
> A: ām, satyam. aham api adya eva jñātam.
> *Yes, really. I also just found out today.*

## Variations

1. **jānāmi / na jānāmi as complete sentences:** These work perfectly on their own. Someone asks a question, you simply say *jānāmi* (I know) or *na jānāmi* (no idea). Clean and natural.

2. **idānīm jñātam** — "Now I know" or "Now I understand." Use this when someone explains something and you get it. *idānīm* = now.

3. **kasya jñātam?** — "Who knew?" A rhetorical question, just like in English. Works for expressing surprise.

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **jānāmi vs. jñātam:** *jānāmi* is present tense — "I know (right now)." *jñātam* is a past participle — "it was known / I came to know." Don't use *jñātam* for ongoing knowledge. "I know Sanskrit" = *ahaṃ saṃskṛtaṃ jānāmi* (not *jñātam*).

2. **na placement:** Put **na** directly before the verb: *na jānāmi*. Putting it elsewhere can change the meaning or sound odd. *ahaṃ jānāmi na* is not standard.
