# Stage 23: Would Have / But Instead

## Pattern Name: Counterfactuals & Missed Chances

---

## The Pattern

**aham agamiṣyam kintu...** — "I would have gone but..."
**yadi aham ajñāsyam tarhi...** — "If I had known then..."
**tat sthāne aham X akārṣam** — "Instead I did X"

---

## How It Works

Up until now, you've talked about what is, what was, what will be, and what should be. Now comes the slippery one: what *would have been*. Sanskrit handles this with the conditional mood (called *lṛṅ lakāra*), which looks a lot like the future tense but with a past-tense prefix (*a-*). So *gamiṣyati* (he will go) becomes *agamiṣyat* (he would have gone). The *a-* prefix (called augment) plus the future-looking stem creates this "past-future" meaning -- something that was going to happen but didn't.

Pair this with *yadi...tarhi* ("if...then") and you get full counterfactual sentences: "If X had happened, then Y would have happened." The word *kintu* ("but") links what would have happened to what actually happened instead. And *tat sthāne* ("in place of that" / "instead") introduces the actual outcome. These patterns are gold for storytelling, explaining regret, and talking about why plans changed.

## Example Phrases

1. **अहम् अगमिष्यम् किन्तु वर्षा आगता।**
   *aham agamiṣyam kintu varṣā āgatā.*
   I would have gone but it rained.

2. **यदि अहम् अजानिष्यम् तर्हि अवश्यम् आगमिष्यम्।**
   *yadi aham ajāniṣyam tarhi avaśyam āgamiṣyam.*
   If I had known, I would certainly have come.

3. **सा अपठिष्यत् किन्तु रुग्णा आसीत्।**
   *sā apaṭhiṣyat kintu rugṇā āsīt.*
   She would have studied but she was sick.

4. **तत् स्थाने अहं गृहे अतिष्ठम्।**
   *tat sthāne ahaṃ gṛhe atiṣṭham.*
   Instead, I stayed at home.

5. **यदि सः समये आगमिष्यत् तर्हि रथयानं प्राप्स्यत्।**
   *yadi saḥ samaye āgamiṣyat tarhi rathayānaṃ prāpsyat.*
   If he had come on time, he would have caught the train.

6. **वयम् अक्रीडिष्याम किन्तु क्षेत्रम् आर्द्रम् आसीत्।**
   *vayam akrīḍiṣyāma kintu kṣetram ārdram āsīt.*
   We would have played but the field was wet.

7. **यदि मूल्यं न्यूनम् अभविष्यत् तर्हि अक्रेष्यम्।**
   *yadi mūlyaṃ nyūnam abhaviṣyat tarhi akreṣyam.*
   If the price had been low, I would have bought it.

8. **अहं तत्र गन्तुम् ऐच्छम् किन्तु समयः न आसीत्।**
   *ahaṃ tatra gantum aiccham kintu samayaḥ na āsīt.*
   I wanted to go there but there was no time.

9. **तत् स्थाने वयं गृहे भोजनम् अपचाम।**
   *tat sthāne vayaṃ gṛhe bhojanam apacāma.*
   Instead, we cooked food at home.

10. **यदि त्वम् अवदिष्यः तर्हि अहम् अश्रोष्यम्।**
    *yadi tvam avadiṣyaḥ tarhi aham aśroṣyam.*
    If you had spoken, I would have listened.

## Mini-Dialogues

**Dialogue 1: Missed party**

-- ह्यः उत्सवे कुत्र आसीः? सर्वे तत्र आसन्।
   *hyaḥ utsave kutra āsīḥ? sarve tatra āsan.*
   Where were you at yesterday's party? Everyone was there.

-- अहम् अगमिष्यम् किन्तु मम माता रुग्णा आसीत्।
   *aham agamiṣyam kintu mama mātā rugṇā āsīt.*
   I would have gone but my mother was sick.

-- अहो! यदि अहम् अज्ञास्यम् तर्हि तव गृहम् आगमिष्यम्।
   *aho! yadi aham ajñāsyam tarhi tava gṛham āgamiṣyam.*
   Oh! If I had known, I would have come to your house.

-- चिन्ता मास्तु। तत् स्थाने अहं मातुः सेवाम् अकरवम्।
   *cintā māstu. tat sthāne ahaṃ mātuḥ sevām akaravam.*
   Don't worry. Instead, I took care of my mother.

**Dialogue 2: Wrong turn**

-- वयं मार्गं विस्मृतवन्तः। यदि मानचित्रं दृष्टम् अभविष्यत् तर्हि न भ्रमिष्याम।
   *vayaṃ mārgaṃ vismṛtavantaḥ. yadi mānacitram dṛṣṭam abhaviṣyat tarhi na bhramiṣyāma.*
   We forgot the way. If we had looked at the map, we wouldn't have wandered.

-- सत्यम्। तत् स्थाने वयम् अन्यं ग्रामम् अपश्याम। सः तु सुन्दरः आसीत्!
   *satyam. tat sthāne vayam anyaṃ grāmam apaśyāma. saḥ tu sundaraḥ āsīt!*
   True. Instead, we saw another village. It was beautiful!

## Variations

1. **With icchā for unfulfilled wishes:**
   *aham āgantum aiccham kintu na aśaknuvam* — "I wished to come but I couldn't."

2. **Using ced instead of yadi (more literary):**
   *saḥ ced āgamiṣyat tarhi sukhaṃ bhaviṣyat* — "Had he come, there would have been happiness."

3. **Expressing regret with hā / aho:**
   *hā, yadi ahaṃ tatra abhaviṣyam!* — "Alas, if only I had been there!"

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing future and conditional:** The conditional *agamiṣyat* looks like the future *gamiṣyati* but has the *a-* augment prefix and different endings. Without the *a-* prefix, you're saying "he will go" instead of "he would have gone." The augment is the whole difference between planning and daydreaming.

2. **Forgetting to pair yadi with tarhi:** The "if...then" structure needs both parts. Saying *yadi aham ajānīṣyam, āgamiṣyam* without *tarhi* is understood but sounds incomplete. In conversation, always include the *tarhi* to signal you're delivering the punchline.
