# Pūjā-Vāk — Workbook (Answers)

## Section A: Saṅkalpa Construction

**A1.**

| Element | Vibhakti | Function |
|---------|----------|----------|
| asmin śubha-muhūrte | **Saptamī** (locative) | Time — "at this auspicious moment" |
| śrīmahākālyāḥ | **Ṣaṣṭhī** (genitive) | Deity — "of Śrī Mahākālī" (the pūjā belongs to her) |
| prītyartham | **Accusative + artham** (purpose) | Purpose — "for the sake of [her] pleasure" |
| mama | **Ṣaṣṭhī** (genitive) | Beneficiary — "my" / "of me" |
| saha-kuṭumbānām | **Ṣaṣṭhī** (genitive plural) | Beneficiary — "of [my] family members together" |
| kṣema-sthairya-vīrya-vijayārtham | **Accusative + artham** (purpose) | Purpose — "for the sake of welfare, stability, vigor, and victory" |
| dakṣiṇakālī-mahāpūjām | **Dvitīyā** (accusative) | Object — "the great pūjā of Dakṣiṇakālī" (what is being performed) |
| aham | **Prathamā** (nominative) | Subject — "I" (the performer) |
| kariṣye | **lṛṭ lakāra** (simple future, first person singular) | Verb — "I shall perform" |

**A2.** Model saṅkalpa:

> *asmin śubha-dine śrīmahāgaṇapateḥ prītyarthaṃ mama saha-kuṭumbānāṃ vighna-nivṛtty-arthaṃ nava-kārya-siddhyarthaṃ ca śrīgaṇeśa-pūjāṃ ahaṃ kariṣye*

Key elements:
- asmin śubha-dine — "on this auspicious day" (saptamī)
- śrīmahāgaṇapateḥ — "of Śrī Mahāgaṇapati" (ṣaṣṭhī of a consonant-stem)
- prītyartham — "for the sake of pleasure" (purpose)
- mama saha-kuṭumbānām — "of me together with family" (ṣaṣṭhī)
- vighna-nivṛtty-artham — "for the removal of obstacles" (purpose compound)
- nava-kārya-siddhyartham ca — "and for success in the new undertaking" (purpose compound)
- śrīgaṇeśa-pūjām — "the pūjā of Śrī Gaṇeśa" (dvitīyā — object)
- aham kariṣye — "I shall perform" (nominative + lṛṭ)

## Section B: Nyāsa Grammar

**B1.**

| Bīja | Body Part | Dative Dual |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| āṃ | index fingers | **tarjanībhyāṃ** |
| īṃ | ring fingers | **anāmikābhyāṃ** |
| ūṃ | palm and back | **karatalakarapṛṣṭhābhyāṃ** |

(All formed with the feminine dative dual ending **-ābhyām**, since these body-part nouns are feminine ā-stems or feminine compound stems.)

**B2.**

1. namaḥ → **b. Surrender / offering**
2. svāhā → **c. Oblation (originally into Vedic fire)**
3. huṃ → **d. Protection / warding**
4. vauṣaṭ → **e. Consecration / empowerment**
5. phaṭ → **a. Weapon-deployment / dispersal**

**B3.**

1. hṛdayāya → **Caturthī ekavacana, napuṃsakaliṅga** (neuter a-stem dative singular: hṛdaya + -āya)
2. śirase → **Caturthī ekavacana, napuṃsakaliṅga** (neuter s-stem dative singular: śiras + -e)
3. śikhāyai → **Caturthī ekavacana, strīliṅga** (feminine ā-stem dative singular: śikhā + -āyai)
4. kavacāya → **Caturthī ekavacana, napuṃsakaliṅga** (neuter a-stem dative singular: kavaca + -āya)
5. netratrayāya → **Caturthī ekavacana, napuṃsakaliṅga** (neuter a-stem compound: netra-traya + -āya)

**B4.** In mātṛkā-nyāsa, the letters are not placed at the sthāna where they are *produced* (that is the mouth-geography from Varṇa-Vidyā). They are placed at the body-location where they are *installed as operative forces.* The ka-varga (kaṇṭhya/ākāśa letters) goes to the right arm because:

- The right arm is the *creative/active limb* — the hand that performs yāga, that offers, that gives. Ākāśa (space) is the first element, the womb of manifestation. Placing the space-element letters in the creative limb means: the arm that acts draws its power from the most primal element.
- The nyāsa system distributes the five vargas across the four limbs + torso to create a *body-yantra.* Each limb becomes one element. This is architecturally different from the mouth-cakra — the mouth maps sound-production; the body-nyāsa maps ritual function.
- The throat already has its own role in the body-yantra (it falls within the pa-varga/torso zone). Installing ka-varga there would create redundancy and leave the creative limb without mantric charge.

## Section C: Dhyāna-Śloka Analysis

**C1.**

1. **sindūrāruṇa-vigrahām**
   - (a) sindūra (vermillion) + aruṇa (dawn-red) + vigraha (form/body) + -ām
   - (b) Bahuvrīhi — "she who possesses a vermillion-red form" (the compound describes an attribute of the deity, not a standalone entity)
   - (c) Dvitīyā strīliṅga (accusative feminine singular)
   - (d) "Her whose body is the color of vermillion and dawn"

2. **trinayanām**
   - (a) tri (three) + nayana (eyes) + -ām
   - (b) Bahuvrīhi — "she who has three eyes"
   - (c) Dvitīyā strīliṅga
   - (d) "Her who has three eyes"

3. **māṇikya-mauli-sphurat-tārā-nāyaka-śekharām**
   - (a) māṇikya (ruby) + mauli (crown) + sphurat (shining, present participle) + tārā-nāyaka (lord of stars = moon) + śekhara (crest) + -ām
   - (b) Nested bahuvrīhi — "she whose crest-jewel is the moon shining from a ruby crown"
   - (c) Dvitīyā strīliṅga
   - (d) "Her whose crown-ornament is the moon radiating from a jeweled diadem"

4. **smitamukhīm**
   - (a) smita (smile, past participle of √smi) + mukhī (feminine of mukha, face) + -m
   - (b) Bahuvrīhi — "she whose face is smiling"
   - (c) Dvitīyā strīliṅga
   - (d) "Her whose face bears a smile"

5. **āpīna-vakṣoruhām**
   - (a) āpīna (full/swollen) + vakṣaḥ-ruha (breast, literally "chest-growing") + -ām
   - (b) Bahuvrīhi — "she whose breasts are full"
   - (c) Dvitīyā strīliṅga
   - (d) "Her whose breasts are full"

**C2.** All five are in the accusative feminine because they are all objects of the implied verb **dhyāyāmi** ("I meditate on"). The entire dhyāna-śloka is one enormous accusative noun-phrase — a chain of bahuvrīhi descriptors all modifying the same "tām" (her). The grammar constructs the visualization: each compound adds one more detail to the image being built in the mind.

**C3.** (Answers will vary. Model for Kālī):

> *khaḍga-khaṇḍita-dānava-śoṇita-dhārāṃ*
> *muṇḍamālā-vibhūṣita-kaṇṭhām ahaṃ dhyāyāmi kālīm*

- khaḍga-khaṇḍita-dānava-śoṇita-dhārām = bahuvrīhi: "her from whose sword flows streams of demon-blood"
- muṇḍamālā-vibhūṣita-kaṇṭhām = bahuvrīhi: "her whose throat is adorned with a garland of skulls"
- kālīm = accusative of kālī, object of dhyāyāmi

## Section D: Upacāra Formulas

**D1.**

1. śrī-**śivāya** namaḥ (śiva → dative: śivāya)
2. śrī-**lalitāyai** namaḥ (lalitā → dative: lalitāyai)
3. śrī-**gaṇeśāya** namaḥ (gaṇeśa → dative: gaṇeśāya)
4. śrī-**kālyai** namaḥ (kālī → dative: kālyai)
5. śrī-**viṣṇave** namaḥ (viṣṇu → dative: viṣṇave)

**D2.** The student used **śivam** — this is the **dvitīyā** (accusative), meaning "Śiva" as the *object* of an action. But in the offering formula, the deity is the **recipient** of the offering, not the object. The deity name must be in the **caturthī** (dative): **śivāya** — "to/for Śiva."

The formula is: "I offer [this thing — accusative] **to** [this deity — dative]." Samarpayāmi takes a dative recipient and an accusative object. Using accusative for the deity would grammatically make Śiva the *thing being offered,* not the one receiving the offering.

**D3.** The verb *samarpayāmi* means "I offer / I present." It is a transitive verb (sakarmaka kriyā) that requires a direct object — the thing being offered. In Sanskrit, the direct object of a transitive verb takes the **dvitīyā** (accusative) case. Each upacāra word (gandham, puṣpam, dīpam, etc.) is the direct object of samarpayāmi, answering the question "what am I offering?" The answer — the substance being given — is always accusative.

## Section E: Āvaraṇa Bīja Analysis

**E1.**

| Āvaraṇa | Bīja | Prefix Added |
|----------|------|-------------|
| 2nd | aiṃ klīṃ sauḥ | (base — no prefix) |
| 4th | haiṃ hklīṃ hsauḥ | **h-** (ha) prefixed to each bīja |
| 5th | hsaiṃ hsklīṃ hssauḥ | **hs-** (ha + sa) prefixed to each bīja |

**Ha** represents **Śiva** in the tantric context — it is the last consonant, pure breath, the exhale of consciousness. The progression signifies:
- **No prefix** (2nd): Pure Śakti — the Bālā mantra in its base form
- **h-** prefix (4th): Śiva's breath enters each bīja — he begins to animate the Śakti-mantras from within
- **hs-** prefix (5th): Full **haṃsa** (ha + sa = Śiva + Śakti breathing together) permeates every bīja — the complete union

The inward journey through the Śrīcakra is a progressive revelation of Śiva within Śakti.

**E2.**
- **Aiṃ** represents **Jñāna-śakti** (knowledge/speech) — it is the Vāgbhavā bīja, pure vāk.
- **Hrīṃ** represents **Māyā-śakti** — the power of veiling and revealing, the cosmic illusion that is also the cosmic play.

The substitution happens because as one moves deeper into the Śrīcakra (from the outer enclosures toward the center), the practitioner moves from the realm of *speech and knowledge about the deity* (aiṃ/vāgbhavā — you can talk about her) into the realm of *direct encounter with her māyā* (hrīṃ — you are inside her illusion/play). The outer enclosures are approachable through vāk (speech/intellect). The inner enclosures require encountering māyā directly.

**E3.** An upasarga (prefix) added to a dhātu does not change the *root meaning* — it modifies its *direction, scope, or intensity:*
- √gam = to go
- ā + √gam = to come (direction changed)
- sam + ā + √gam = to come together (scope expanded)

Similarly, adding h- to a bīja does not change the *base bīja* (aiṃ remains aiṃ) — it modifies its *operative dimension:*
- aiṃ = vāk-śakti operating as Śakti alone
- h + aiṃ = vāk-śakti now infused with Śiva's breath
- hs + aiṃ = vāk-śakti permeated by the full Śiva-Śakti union

In both cases, the prefix is not a separate word — it fuses with the base to create a new operative unit. And in both cases, the system is *recursive:* you can stack prefixes (sam + ā + √gam; hs + aiṃ) to deepen the modification. The grammar of language and the grammar of mantra use the same structural principle.

## Section F: Dīkṣitar's Vibhakti Architecture

**F1.**

| Āvaraṇa | Kamalāmbā form | Vibhakti | Meaning of the relationship |
|----------|----------------|----------|-----------------------------|
| 1st | kamalāmbā | **Prathamā** (nominative) | Identity — "she IS" — pure being/appearance |
| 2nd | kamalāmbām | **Dvitīyā** (accusative) | Object — "worship HER" — she becomes the focus of devotion |
| 3rd | kamalāmbayā | **Tṛtīyā** (instrumental) | Means — "BY/through her" — she becomes the instrument |
| 4th | kamalāmbāyai | **Caturthī** (dative) | Recipient — "FOR/to her" — she receives the offering |
| 5th | kamalāmbāyāḥ | **Pañcamī** (ablative) | Source — "FROM her" — she is the origin |
| 6th | kamalāmbāyāḥ | **Ṣaṣṭhī** (genitive) | Possession — "OF her" — everything belongs to her |
| 7th | kamalāmbāyām | **Saptamī** (locative) | Location — "IN her" — she is the field of all experience |
| 8th | kamalāmbike | **Sambodhana** (vocative) | Address — "O Kamalāmbikā!" — direct encounter |

**F2.** The forms are identical (kamalāmbāyāḥ) because feminine ā-stems share the ending -āyāḥ for both pañcamī and ṣaṣṭhī. Disambiguation comes from context and syntax:

- **Pañcamī (ablative — "from her"):** Used with verbs/concepts of origin, separation, fear, protection-from. Example: *kamalāmbāyāḥ sakalaṃ jagat jātam* — "The entire world was born **from** Kamalāmbā."
- **Ṣaṣṭhī (genitive — "of her"):** Used for possession, relationship, belonging. Example: *kamalāmbāyāḥ pādapadmaṃ namāmi* — "I bow to the lotus-feet **of** Kamalāmbā."

The ablative answers "from where?" The genitive answers "whose?" In Dīkṣitar's kṛtis, the 5th āvaraṇa kṛti emphasizes that everything *originates from* her (source/ablative), while the 6th emphasizes that everything *belongs to* her (possession/genitive). Two different theological statements, same grammatical form, distinguished by lyrical context.
