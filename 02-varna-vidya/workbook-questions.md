5. ra

1. Kaṇṭhya (throat)
2. Tālavya (palate)
3. Mūrdhanya (dome)
4. Dantya (teeth)

**A3.** Which sthāna do the following compound vowels combine? (5 marks):
1. e
2. ai
3. o
4. au
5. Why are these called "compound" vowels?

## Section D: Bīja Analysis (20 marks)

**D1.** Dissect each bīja into its component letters. For each letter, give the sthāna and element (10 marks):
1. Hrīṃ
2. Krīṃ
3. Klīṃ
4. Śrīṃ
5. Aiṃ

**D2.** The only difference between Krīṃ and Klīṃ is one letter: ra vs la (5 marks):
1. What is the sthāna of ra?
2. What is the sthāna of la?
3. What element does ra carry?
4. What element does la carry?
5. How does this single-letter difference explain the difference between Kālī's function (liberation through fire) and Kāma's function (attraction into form)?

**D3.** Analyze the Bālā mantra — Aiṃ Klīṃ Sauḥ (5 marks):
1. Which three śaktis do the three bījas represent?
2. What is unusual about Aiṃ compared to the other bījas? (Hint: look at the consonant component)
3. Sauḥ ends with visarga (ḥ) rather than anusvāra (ṃ). What is the difference between these two seals?
4. The three bījas correspond to the three kūṭas of the Pañcadaśī. Name them.
5. Why does the Vāgbhava (speech) kūṭa use the compound vowel "ai" specifically?

## Section F: Synthesis (20 marks)

**F1.** The word "aham" (I) is said to contain the entire alphabet. Explain how, using the positions of a, ha, and m in the varṇamālā (5 marks).

**F2.** A student says: "Bīja mantras are just random sacred syllables — their power comes from tradition and faith, not from the letters themselves." Using what you have learned in this stage, construct a counter-argument based on the sthāna system (5 marks).

**F3.** Take any one name of your iṣṭadevatā from Stage 1. Perform a complete analysis (5 marks):
- Break it into syllables
- Identify the sthāna and element of each consonant
- Identify the vowel type (short/long/compound) and its quality
- Describe the "sonic geography" — how does the name move through the mouth?

**F4.** Why does Pāṇini's grammar begin with the Maheshvara Sūtrāṇi rather than with grammar rules? What does this say about the relationship between sound and structure in the Sanskrit tradition? (5 marks)
