# Varṇa-Vidyā — Workbook (Answers)

## Section A: Sthāna Identification

**A1.**
1. ka → **Kaṇṭhya** (throat)
2. ṭa → **Mūrdhanya** (dome of palate)
3. pa → **Oṣṭhya** (lips)
4. śa → **Tālavya** (palate)
5. ra → **Mūrdhanya** (dome of palate)

**A2.**
1. Kaṇṭhya → **Ākāśa** (space)
2. Tālavya → **Vāyu** (air)
3. Mūrdhanya → **Agni** (fire)
4. Dantya → **Āpas** (water)
5. Oṣṭhya → **Pṛthivī** (earth)

**A3.**
1. e → **Kaṇṭha + Tālu** (throat + palate)
2. ai → **Kaṇṭha + Tālu** (throat + palate, further intensified)
3. o → **Kaṇṭha + Oṣṭha** (throat + lips)
4. au → **Kaṇṭha + Oṣṭha** (throat + lips, further intensified)
5. They are called "compound" because they arise from the fusion of two simple vowels at two different sthānas: e = a (kaṇṭha) + i (tālu), o = a (kaṇṭha) + u (oṣṭha). They are sandhi within the vowel system itself — two places of articulation producing one sound.

## Section B: The Sparśa Matrix

**B1.**

| | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kaṇṭha | ka | **kha** | ga | **gha** | ṅa |
| Tālu | **ca** | cha | ja | jha | **ña** |
| Danta | ta | tha | **da** | dha | na |

**B2.**
1. ka vs kha → ka is **1st column** (unvoiced, unaspirated), kha is **2nd column** (unvoiced, aspirated). Difference: **aspiration** — kha adds a puff of breath (prāṇa) to the same stop.
2. ga vs gha → ga is **3rd column** (voiced), gha is **4th column** (voiced, aspirated). Difference: gha adds aspiration to the voiced sound — both voice AND breath.
3. ka vs ga → ka is **1st column** (unvoiced), ga is **3rd column** (voiced). Difference: **voicing** — ga engages the vocal cords, ka does not. Both are at the same sthāna (kaṇṭha).
4. ta vs na → ta is **1st column** (stop), na is **5th column** (nasal). Difference: ta stops air completely at the teeth; na lets air flow through the nose while the tongue is in the same position. The nasal is the "dissolution" of the stop.
5. pa vs ma → pa is **1st column** (stop), ma is **5th column** (nasal). Same relationship as ta/na but at the lips. Pa seals; ma hums.

**B3.** The ka-varga arc:
- **ka** — Pure stop. No voice, no breath. The sound hits the back of the throat like a fist. Seed. Potential. Compact.
- **kha** — Same stop + breath. The fist opens slightly. Prāṇa enters. First expansion.
- **ga** — Voice enters. The vocal cords vibrate. Life. Animation. What was silent now hums.
- **gha** — Voice + breath together. Maximum energy in the form. Full animation.
- **ṅa** — The nasal. Sound leaves the throat entirely and enters the nose. Dissolution into pure resonance. The stop has been transcended.

The arc is: **seed → breath → life → fullness → dissolution.** This mirrors sṛṣṭi (creation) to saṃhāra (dissolution) within a single sthāna.

## Section C: Pratyāhāra

**C1.**
1. **ac** = Start at **a** (sūtra 1), end at **c** (it-marker of sūtra 4) → a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ, e, o, ai, au = **all vowels**
2. **hal** = Start at **ha** (sūtra 5), end at **l** (it-marker of sūtra 14) → all consonants from ha through the complete set = **all consonants**
3. **ik** = Start at **i** (sūtra 1), end at **k** (it-marker of sūtra 2) → i, u, ṛ, ḷ = **the high/close vowels (without a)**
4. **aṇ** = Start at **a** (sūtra 1), end at **ṇ** (it-marker of sūtra 1) → a, i, u = **the three basic short vowels**
5. **ec** = Start at **e** (sūtra 3), end at **c** (it-marker of sūtra 4) → e, o, ai, au = **all diphthongs/compound vowels**

**C2.**
1. All vowels → **ac** (a through the c-marker of sūtra 4)
2. All consonants → **hal** (ha through the l-marker of sūtra 14)
3. i, u, ṛ, ḷ → **ik** (i through the k-marker of sūtra 2)
4. e, o only → **eṅ** (e through the ṅ-marker of sūtra 3)
5. śa, ṣa, sa → **śar** (śa through the r-marker of sūtra 13) — note: this captures śa, ṣa, sa but NOT ha

**C3.** "Ādir antyena sahetā" means: "The first [sound in the sequence], together with the final [it-marker], [denotes all sounds between them]."

This system is more efficient than listing because:
- One two-letter code can denote 2 to 40+ sounds
- When Pāṇini writes a rule about "all vowels," he writes "ac" (2 letters) instead of listing 9 vowels
- The entire Aṣṭādhyāyī (~4,000 sūtras) depends on this compression — without pratyāhāras, every rule would be several times longer
- The ordering of sounds in the 14 sūtras is not alphabetical or arbitrary — it is *optimized* so that every phonetically meaningful group can be captured by some pratyāhāra. The arrangement is itself a feat of compression engineering.

## Section D: Bīja Analysis

**D1.**
1. **Hrīṃ** = ha (kaṇṭha / ākāśa) + ra (mūrdhan / agni) + ī (tālu / vāyu) + ṃ (nāsikā / bindu)
2. **Krīṃ** = ka (kaṇṭha / ākāśa) + ra (mūrdhan / agni) + ī (tālu / vāyu) + ṃ (nāsikā / bindu)
3. **Klīṃ** = ka (kaṇṭha / ākāśa) + la (danta / āpas) + ī (tālu / vāyu) + ṃ (nāsikā / bindu)
4. **Śrīṃ** = śa (tālu / vāyu) + ra (mūrdhan / agni) + ī (tālu / vāyu) + ṃ (nāsikā / bindu)
5. **Aiṃ** = ai (kaṇṭha-tālu / compound) + ṃ (nāsikā / bindu)

**D2.**
1. ra → **Mūrdhanya** (dome of palate)
2. la → **Dantya** (teeth)
3. ra → **Agni** (fire)
4. la → **Āpas** (water) — some traditions assign earth since la is the grounding letter; in the 5-element sthāna mapping, dantya = āpas
5. Fire transforms and consumes — Kālī's function is to burn through illusion, to liberate by destroying what binds. Water/earth conforms and attracts — Kāma's function is to draw things together, to embody desire in form. The sthāna shift from mūrdhan (fire, the highest point in the mouth) to danta (water/earth, lower and structural) mirrors the difference between a force that *ascends and burns* vs one that *descends and manifests.* One letter. One sthāna. Entirely different theology.

**D3.**
1. Aiṃ = **Jñāna-śakti** (knowledge). Klīṃ = **Icchā-śakti** (will/desire). Sauḥ = **Kriyā-śakti** (action).
2. Aiṃ has **no consonant** — it is a pure compound vowel (ai) + bindu. All other major bījas begin with a consonant. Aiṃ is pure vāk (speech) without any stop or friction. This is fitting: Sarasvatī/Vāgbhavā IS speech itself, not speech *about* something.
3. Anusvāra (ṃ) is **implosion** — sound collapses inward into nasal resonance and then silence. The point absorbs. Visarga (ḥ) is **exhalation** — sound releases outward as breath. Sauḥ ends with visarga because Kriyā-śakti is *outward-directed* action, Śakti exhaling herself into creation. The other bījas end with anusvāra because they seal the energy inward.
4. Aiṃ = **Vāgbhava kūṭa** (speech). Klīṃ = **Kāmarāja kūṭa** (desire). Sauḥ = **Śakti kūṭa** (power/action).
5. "Ai" is the most intensified form of the speech-vowels. Simple a (kaṇṭha) + i (tālu) = e (compound). But ai intensifies further — it is e *raised.* It is the vowel that combines throat (where sound is born) and palate (where it becomes articulate) at maximum intensity. This is why it carries Vāgbhavā: the *fullest possible expression of the speech-producing mechanism.*

## Section E: Reading Names

**E1.**
1. **Śiva** (śi-va): śa (tālu/palate) → i (tālu) → va (danta-oṣṭha/teeth-lips). Movement: palatal friction dissolves into a labial glide. The sound relaxes from high tension (sibilant) into flowing breath. Sonic signature: *release, ease.*
2. **Rudra** (rud-ra): ru (mūrdhan, with danta for d) → dra (danta + mūrdhan). Movement: the tongue strikes the dome, drops to the teeth, rolls back to the dome. Forceful, percussive. Sonic signature: *impact, howling.*
3. **Lalitā** (la-li-tā): la (danta) → li (danta → tālu) → tā (danta). Movement: the tongue stays at the teeth/palate zone throughout — light taps, never going deep to the throat or sealing at the lips. Sonic signature: *playfulness, lightness.*

**E2.**
1. **Durgā** (dur-gā): du (danta) → r (mūrdhan) → gā (kaṇṭha). Movement: teeth → dome → throat. The name moves *inward and upward* — from the structural (teeth) through fire (dome) to the deepest place (throat). Reaching the inaccessible.
2. **Gaṇeśa** (ga-ṇe-śa): ga (kaṇṭha) → ṇa (mūrdhan) → e (kaṇṭha-tālu) → śa (tālu). Movement: deep throat → dome → throat-palate compound → palate. The name arcs upward from the throat through the dome and settles at the palate. The lord of beginnings starts at the root.
3. **Viṣṇu** (viṣ-ṇu): va (danta-oṣṭha) → i (tālu) → ṣa (mūrdhan) → ṇu (mūrdhan + oṣṭha). Movement: lips-teeth → palate → dome → dome-lips. A wide sweep through the mouth — the all-pervading one touches almost every position.
4. **Sarasvatī** (sa-ra-sva-tī): sa (danta) → ra (mūrdhan) → sva (danta + danta-oṣṭha) → tī (danta + tālu). Movement: teeth → dome → teeth-lips → teeth-palate. The tongue stays primarily in the dental zone — precise, articulate, clear. Fitting for the goddess of structured speech.
5. **Tripurasundarī** (tri-pu-ra-sun-da-rī): ta (danta) → ra (mūrdhan) → i (tālu) → pu (oṣṭha) → ra (mūrdhan) → su (danta) → n (danta) → da (danta) → rī (mūrdhan + tālu). Movement: the name traverses the entire mouth repeatedly — teeth, dome, palate, lips, dome, teeth, teeth, teeth, dome. The three cities (tri-pura) are literally three sweeps through the sthāna geography.

**E3.** The 48 letters:
- 16 vowels: a, ā, i, ī, u, ū, ṛ, ṝ, ḷ, ḹ, e, ai, o, au, aṃ, aḥ
- 25 sparśa (stops): ka through ma (5 vargas × 5)
- 4 antastha (semivowels): ya, ra, la, va
- 3 ūṣma (sibilants): śa, ṣa, sa

Total: 16 + 25 + 4 + 3 = **48**

(Some counts give 50 by adding kṣa and jña as compound letters, or 51 by adding ha separately from the sibilants. The Lalitā Sahasranāma follows the 48-letter count, which treats ha as part of the ūṣma group implicitly or counts visarga separately.)

## Section F: Synthesis

**F1.** In the varṇamālā:
- **a** is the **first vowel** — the beginning of the entire alphabet
- **ha** is the **last consonant** — the final letter before the alphabet ends
- **m** (anusvāra) is the **bindu** — the point where all articulated sound dissolves into nasal resonance and then silence

"Aham" thus spans from the first sound to the last sound, sealed by the point of dissolution. The word for "I" literally contains the entire alphabet compressed into three phonemes. The self (aham) IS the totality of sound (the mātṛkā).

**F2.** Counter-argument: Bīja mantras are *constructed* from specific letters chosen for their sthāna (place of articulation) and prayatna (manner of production). Each sthāna corresponds to an element and produces a specific quality of vibration in the body. The difference between krīṃ and klīṃ is a single letter — ra vs la — but this shifts the sthāna from mūrdhan (fire/transformation) to danta (water-earth/grounding), producing an entirely different function (Kālī's liberating fire vs Kāma's embodying attraction). If bījas were arbitrary, swapping one letter would make no difference. But the tradition treats ra→la as the difference between two entirely different deities. The letters are the technology. The sthāna system is the engineering specification.

**F3.** (Answers will vary by student's iṣṭadevatā. Model answer for "Kṛṣṇa"):

**Kṛṣṇaḥ** (kṛṣ-ṇaḥ):
- **kṛ**: ka (kaṇṭha/ākāśa — throat stop) + ṛ (mūrdhan/agni — vocalic r at the dome). The hard stop opens into fire-vibration.
- **ṣ**: ṣa (mūrdhan/agni — retroflex sibilant). Fire-friction at the dome. The name stays in the fire-zone.
- **ṇaḥ**: ṇa (mūrdhan/agni — retroflex nasal) + ḥ (visarga — pure breath release).

Sonic geography: The name begins at the deepest point (ka, throat) and immediately rises to the dome of the palate (ṛ, ṣ, ṇ) — and stays there. Three consecutive mūrdhanya sounds. Then the visarga releases everything as breath. The name is dominated by retroflex/fire energy — the "dark one" (kṛṣṇa = dark) is sonically a fire-name. Darkness that *burns.* The visarga at the end is an exhale — the fire doesn't seal (no bindu), it breathes out.

**F4.** Pāṇini begins with the Maheshvara Sūtrāṇi because *the sounds come before the rules.* Grammar does not invent sounds — it describes how pre-existing sounds combine. The sūtrāṇi establish the raw material (the phoneme inventory) in an order optimized for the rules that follow. This reflects the Sanskrit tradition's understanding that sound (śabda) is prior to meaning (artha) and structure (vyākaraṇa). The grammar is *discovered* in the sound-system, not imposed upon it. Starting with the Maheshvara Sūtrāṇi says: before you can understand how language works, you must know what sounds exist and how they are organized. Sound first. Structure second. Meaning emerges from the meeting of the two.
