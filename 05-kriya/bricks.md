# Chapter 4: Kriyāpada -- Verbs

## Goal

Understand how Sanskrit verbs are formed from roots (dhātus), learn the system of 10 verb classes (gaṇas), 10 tense/mood markers (lakāras), and the 18 personal endings (tiṅ pratyayas). Conjugate common dhātus in laṭ lakāra (present tense) and lṛṭ lakāra (simple future tense) in both parasmaipada and ātmanepada.

---

## 1. What is a Kriyāpada?

A **kriyāpada** (क्रियापदम्) is a verb form -- a word that expresses an action (kriyā). In the sentence:

> rāmaḥ pustakam **paṭhati**

"paṭhati" (he reads) is the kriyāpada. Every kriyāpada is built from three components:

1. **Dhātu** (धातु) -- the verb root
2. **Vikaraṇa pratyaya** -- the class marker (inserted between root and ending)
3. **Tiṅ pratyaya** -- the personal ending (encodes person, number, and pada)

---

## 3. The 10 Gaṇas (Verb Classes)

Dhātus are classified into **10 gaṇas** (गण, "groups"). Each gaṇa has a characteristic **vikaraṇa pratyaya** (class marker) that is inserted between the dhātu and the tiṅ pratyaya. The gaṇa determines how the dhātu is modified before adding personal endings.

| Gaṇa | Name | Vikaraṇa | Example Dhātu | Laṭ 3rd sg. |
|------|------|----------|---------------|-------------|
| 1 | Bhvādi | -a- (with guṇa) | bhū (to be) | bhavati |
| 2 | Adādi | (none) | ad (to eat) | atti |
| 3 | Juhotyādi | (reduplication) | hu (to sacrifice) | juhoti |
| 4 | Divādi | -ya- | div (to play) | dīvyati |
| 5 | Svādi | -nu- | su (to press) | sunoti |
| 6 | Tudādi | -a- (no guṇa) | tud (to strike) | tudati |
| 7 | Rudhādi | -na-/-n- (infix) | rudh (to obstruct) | ruṇaddhi |
| 8 | Tanādi | -u- | tan (to stretch) | tanoti |
| 9 | Kryādi | -nā-/-nī- | krī (to buy) | krīṇāti |
| 10 | Curādi | -aya- | cur (to steal) | corayati |

### Key Points

- **Gaṇas 1, 4, 6, 10** are called **sarvadhātuka** classes with thematic vowel -a-.
- **Gaṇa 1** applies guṇa to the root vowel (bhū -> bhav-); **gaṇa 6** does not.
- **Gaṇa 10** (curādi) adds -aya- and applies vṛddhi to the root vowel. The 10th gaṇa forms are identical to causative (ṇijanta) forms.
- **Gaṇa 2** (adādi) is athematic -- endings attach directly to the root with no vikaraṇa.
- The gaṇa of a dhātu is fixed and must be learned from the Dhātupāṭha.

## 4. The 10 Lakāras (Tenses and Moods)

Sanskrit has **10 lakāras** -- markers for tense (kāla) and mood (artha). They are remembered by the mnemonic beginning with "la":

| # | Lakāra | Name | Type | Meaning |
|---|--------|------|------|---------|
| 1 | **laṭ** | vartamāna | Tense | Present tense |
| 2 | **laṅ** | anadyatana-bhūta | Tense | Past tense (not of today) |
| 3 | **liṭ** | parokṣa-bhūta | Tense | Past not witnessed (perfect) |
| 4 | **luṅ** | sāmānya-bhūta | Tense | General past (aorist) |
| 5 | **lṛṭ** | sāmānya-bhaviṣyat | Tense | Simple future |
| 6 | **luṭ** | anadyatana-bhaviṣyat | Tense | Periphrastic future |
| 7 | **loṭ** | āśīḥ / vidhī | Mood | Imperative (command/request) |
| 8 | **liṅ** (vidhiliṅ) | vidhi | Mood | Optative / potential |
| 9 | **liṅ** (āśīrliṅ) | āśīḥ | Mood | Benedictive (blessing) |
| 10 | **lṛṅ** | saṅketa | Mood | Conditional |

### The Kāla Framework

Grammarians define a concept called **anadyatana** ("not of today"). The "current day" (adyatana) runs from last midnight to tonight's midnight. Actions within this window use laṭ (present); actions before it use laṅ (past); actions after it use lṛṭ (future).

| Time Frame | Lakāra | Example |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Within today (present) | laṭ | paṭhati (he reads) |
| Before today (general past) | laṅ | apaṭhat (he read) |
| After today (simple future) | lṛṭ | paṭhiṣyati (he will read) |
| Witnessed past (remote) | liṭ | papāṭha (he read [long ago]) |
| Unwitnessed past (parokṣa) | liṭ | papāṭha (he had read) |

**For this course**, we focus on **laṭ** (present) and **lṛṭ** (simple future).

## 5. Tiṅ Pratyaya -- The 18 Personal Endings

Every finite verb in Sanskrit carries a **tiṅ pratyaya** that encodes:
- **Puruṣa** (person): prathama (3rd), madhyama (2nd), uttama (1st)
- **Vacana** (number): ekavacana (singular), dvivacana (dual), bahuvacana (plural)
- **Pada** (voice): parasmaipada (P) or ātmanepada (Ā)

Sanskrit counts persons differently from English:
- **Prathama puruṣa** (3rd person) -- he/she/it/they (the one spoken about)
- **Madhyama puruṣa** (2nd person) -- you (the one spoken to)
- **Uttama puruṣa** (1st person) -- I/we (the speaker)

### Laṭ Lakāra Tiṅ Pratyaya Table

| Puruṣa | Vacana | Parasmaipada (P) | Ātmanepada (Ā) |
|--------|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Prathama (3rd) | Eka. | -ti | -te |
| Prathama (3rd) | Dvi. | -taḥ | -ete |
| Prathama (3rd) | Bahu. | -anti | -ante |
| Madhyama (2nd) | Eka. | -si | -se |
| Madhyama (2nd) | Dvi. | -thaḥ | -ethe |
| Madhyama (2nd) | Bahu. | -tha | -dhve |
| Uttama (1st) | Eka. | -mi | -e |
| Uttama (1st) | Dvi. | -vaḥ | -vahe |
| Uttama (1st) | Bahu. | -maḥ | -mahe |

### Parasmaipada vs. Ātmanepada

- **Parasmaipada** (परस्मैपद): "word for another" -- the action benefits another. Most transitive verbs default here.
- **Ātmanepada** (आत्मनेपद): "word for oneself" -- the action benefits the agent.
- Some dhātus are **ubhayapadin** -- they can take either set of endings depending on context.

The pada of a dhātu is specified in the Dhātupāṭha and must be learned. Examples:
- **paṭh** (to read) -- parasmaipadin
- **labh** (to obtain) -- ātmanepadin
- **kṛ** (to do) -- ubhayapadin
- **bhū** (to be) -- parasmaipadin
- **vṛdh** (to grow) -- ātmanepadin

## 6. Laṭ Lakāra -- Present Tense Conjugation

### How a Verb Form is Built (Gaṇa 1 Example)

Take **bhū** (to be), gaṇa 1:

1. Start with dhātu: **bhū**
2. Apply guṇa: bhū -> **bhav** (ū -> av by guṇa, characteristic of gaṇa 1)
3. Add vikaraṇa -a-: **bhava**
4. Add tiṅ pratyaya: **bhava + ti = bhavati**

## 7. Lṛṭ Lakāra -- Simple Future Tense

### Formation

The future tense is formed by inserting the pratyaya **-iṣya-** (parasmaipada) or **-iṣya-** (ātmanepada) between the dhātu (with guṇa/vṛddhi as applicable) and the tiṅ endings. The formula:

> dhātu (+ guṇa) + **-iṣya-** + tiṅ pratyaya

The tiṅ endings in lṛṭ are the same as in laṭ.

## 8. Forming Simple Sentences

Sanskrit word order is typically: **Subject (kartā) + Object (karma) + Verb (kriyā)**

### Present Tense Examples

| Sanskrit | Translation |
|----------|-------------|
| rāmaḥ paṭhati | Rāma reads |
