# Pūjā-Vāk — The Sanskrit of Ritual

## Goal
Understand how Sanskrit actually functions inside a pūjā — not as "prayers recited in Sanskrit" but as a precise technology where grammar, mantra-structure, and ritual action are inseparable. Learn to read saṅkalpa, nyāsa, dhyāna-śloka, and āvaraṇa-pūjā as applied Sanskrit grammar.

## Position in the Syllabus
Insert after Prārthanā (Stage 14). You can now compose original prayers. This stage shows you how the tradition composes its prayers — with exact grammatical and mantric architecture.

## Source Material
Primary examples drawn from:
- Kāmākṣī Navāvaraṇa Pūjā Paddhati (Kāñcī Sampradāya)
- Śrīvidyā Pūjā Vidhi
- Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava (NST)
- Dīkṣitar's Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa Kṛtis

## Part 1: The Grammar of Ritual — What Sanskrit Is Doing in a Pūjā

A pūjā is not a devotional freestyle. It is a structured sequence where each Sanskrit utterance performs a specific function. The language is not *describing* worship — it IS the worship. Seven distinct modes of Sanskrit appear in a standard pūjā:

| Mode | Sanskrit Function | Grammar Deployed | Example |
|------|------------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Saṅkalpa** | Declaration of intent | Future participle, dative of purpose, locative of time/place | "I shall perform the worship of X, for the sake of Y, at this time and place" |
| **Nyāsa** | Installation of mantras in the body | Dative ("to this limb") + mantra + "namaḥ" | "aṅguṣṭhābhyāṃ namaḥ" — "salutation to the two thumbs" |
| **Dhyāna** | Visualization verse | Accusative (the one visualized), present participles, bahuvrīhi compounds | "I meditate on [her] who holds X, who sits on Y, who shines like Z" |
| **Āvāhana** | Invocation | Imperative or optative, vocative | "Come, O Devī! Be present here!" |
| **Upacāra** | Offering | Dative ("to X") + accusative ("this offering") + verb of offering | "śrīlalitāyai namaḥ — gandhaṃ samarpayāmi" |
| **Stotra** | Praise | Nominative chains, vocative, varied vibhaktis | Free composition within metrical form |
| **Visarjana** | Departure | Imperative, optative | "Go to your own place, O Devī" |

Each mode has its own grammar. Learning pūjā-Sanskrit means learning when to use which mode.

## Part 2: Saṅkalpa — The Grammar of Intent

The saṅkalpa is the most grammatically precise utterance in the entire pūjā. It declares who is performing what worship, for whom, when, where, and for what purpose. Every word is inflected to carry its exact semantic role.

### Structure

A saṅkalpa typically follows this skeleton:

> [Time — locative] [Place — locative] [Performer — nominative] [Deity — genitive/dative] [Rite — accusative] [Purpose — dative/artham] [Verb — kariṣye/kariṣyāmi]

### Example: Śrīvidyā Pūjā Saṅkalpa

> *asmin śubha-muhūrte, śrīmahātripurasundaryāḥ ārādhanārtham, bhukti-mukti-phalapradaṃ śrīcakra-navāvaraṇa-pūjām ahaṃ kariṣye*

| Word/Phrase | Case | Function |
|------------|------|----------|
| asmin śubha-muhūrte | **Saptamī** (locative) | "At this auspicious moment" — time |
| śrīmahātripurasundaryāḥ | **Ṣaṣṭhī** (genitive) | "Of Śrī Mahātripurasundarī" — the deity worshipped |
| ārādhanārtham | **Accusative + artham** | "For the sake of worship" — purpose |
| bhukti-mukti-phalapradaṃ | **Dvitīyā** (accusative) | "That which bestows the fruits of enjoyment and liberation" — describing the pūjā |
| śrīcakra-navāvaraṇa-pūjām | **Dvitīyā** (accusative) | "The nine-enclosure worship of the Śrīcakra" — the object of kariṣye |
| ahaṃ | **Prathamā** (nominative) | "I" — the performer |
| kariṣye | **Verb** (lṛṭ lakāra, first person singular) | "I shall perform" — simple future |

### What to Notice

- The saṅkalpa uses **lṛṭ** (simple future) — not loṭ (imperative), not liṅ (optative). The performer is not requesting or hoping. They are *declaring what they will do.* This is the grammar of sovereign intention.
- The deity appears in **ṣaṣṭhī** (genitive) — "of Tripurasundarī" — because the pūjā *belongs to* the deity, not the other way around. The performer serves; the deity possesses.
- **artham** (purpose) takes a genitive or a compound — ārādhana + artham = "for the sake of worship." This is a standard Sanskrit construction for expressing purpose.
- Every samāsa in the saṅkalpa is a **tatpuruṣa** or **karmadhāraya** — determinative compounds. Saṅkalpas don't use bahuvrīhi (descriptive) or dvandva (coordinative). The language of intent is precise, not decorative.

## Part 3: Nyāsa — Grammar Installed in the Body

Nyāsa ("placing") is the ritual installation of mantric syllables at specific locations on the body. Grammatically, it deploys the **caturthī** (dative) case systematically — every phrase says "to [this body-part], namaḥ."

### Kara-Nyāsa (Hand Placement)

| Mantra | Body Part (Dative) | Translation |
|--------|--------------------|-------------|
| aṃ **aṅguṣṭhābhyāṃ** namaḥ | Two thumbs (dvivacana dative) | "Salutation to the two thumbs" |
| āṃ **tarjanībhyāṃ** namaḥ | Two index fingers | "Salutation to the two index fingers" |
| iṃ **madhyamābhyāṃ** namaḥ | Two middle fingers | "Salutation to the two middle fingers" |
| īṃ **anāmikābhyāṃ** namaḥ | Two ring fingers | "Salutation to the two ring fingers" |
| uṃ **kaniṣṭhikābhyāṃ** namaḥ | Two little fingers | "Salutation to the two little fingers" |
| ūṃ **karatalakarapṛṣṭhābhyāṃ** namaḥ | Palm and back of hand | "Salutation to palm-and-back" |

### What to Notice — Grammatically

- Every body-part is in **caturthī dvivacana** (dative dual) — because you have two hands. The suffix **-ābhyām** is the dual dative/instrumental ending. You are literally practicing dvivacana declension during nyāsa.
- The bīja changes with each placement (aṃ, āṃ, iṃ, īṃ, uṃ, ūṃ) — this is the vowel sequence from the varṇamālā applied systematically to the body. The nyāsa IS the alphabet walking through the fingers.
- **namaḥ** at the end is an indeclinable (avyaya) functioning as the verb of offering. Every nyāsa sentence is: [bīja] + [body-part in dative] + namaḥ.

### Aṅga-Nyāsa (Limb Placement)

| Mantra | Body Part (Dative) | Grammar Note |
|--------|--------------------|-------------|
| hrīṃ **hṛdayāya** namaḥ | Heart (caturthī ekavacana) | hṛdaya + -āya = neuter dative singular |
| śrīṃ **śirase** svāhā | Head (caturthī) | śiras + -e = neuter s-stem dative |
| krīṃ **śikhāyai** namaḥ | Tuft (caturthī) | śikhā + -āyai = feminine ā-stem dative |
| klīṃ **kavacāya** hum | Armor/torso (caturthī) | kavaca + -āya = neuter dative |
| aiṃ **netratrayāya** vauṣaṭ | Three eyes (caturthī) | netra-traya + -āya = compound neuter dative |
| sauḥ **astrāya** phaṭ | Weapon (caturthī) | astra + -āya = neuter dative |

### What to Notice

- The seed-syllable changes with each limb and corresponds to the deity's specific bījas (hrīṃ for heart, etc.)
- The **ending word changes** — namaḥ, svāhā, hum, vauṣaṭ, phaṭ. These are not synonyms. Each is a specific ritual closing with its own function:
  - **namaḥ** = surrender/offering
  - **svāhā** = oblation (originally the Vedic offering-call into fire)
  - **hum** = protection/warding
  - **vauṣaṭ** = empowerment (Vedic: the call at the moment of offering)
  - **phaṭ** = dispersal/weapon-deployment
- The grammar is **always dative** — the body-part is the *recipient* of the mantra. You are giving the mantra to that location. The dative IS the ritual act of installation.

### Mātṛkā-Nyāsa: The Alphabet Installed

In mātṛkā-nyāsa, all 50 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet are placed on the body — each letter at a specific location, in sequence, using the same dative construction:

| Letters | Body Location | Pattern |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| a, ā | Crown (brahmarandhra) | First vowels at the highest point |
| i, ī | Right eye, left eye | The "seeing" vowels |
| u, ū | Right ear, left ear | The "hearing" vowels |
| ka-varga | Right arm (shoulder to fingertips) | Ākāśa-letters in the creative limb |
| ca-varga | Right leg (hip to toes) | Vāyu-letters |
| ṭa-varga | Left leg | Agni-letters |
| ta-varga | Left arm | Āpas-letters |
| pa-varga | Torso (navel to throat) | Pṛthivī-letters in the trunk |
| ya ra la va | Spine and joints | Transitional letters at the joints |
| śa ṣa sa ha | Heart, navel, mūlādhāra, all-pervading | Heat-letters at the fire-centers |

After mātṛkā-nyāsa, the body IS the alphabet. Every subsequent mantra activates the locations where its letters were installed. The mantra plays the body like an instrument.

This is why nyāsa is performed before every pūjā — it is not a warm-up. It is the *tuning* of the instrument.

## Part 4: Dhyāna-Śloka — The Grammar of Seeing

A dhyāna (meditation/visualization) verse describes the deity's form so precisely that the practitioner can *see* it. Grammatically, dhyāna-ślokas are dominated by:

- **Bahuvrīhi compounds** (possessive descriptors): "she whose eyes are like lotuses," "he whose throat is blue"
- **Present participles** (śatṛ/śānac): "sitting," "holding," "shining"
- **Accusative** of the person meditated upon (the object of dhyāyāmi, "I meditate on")

### Example: Lalitā Dhyāna from the Kamakshi Paddhati

> *sindūrāruṇa-vigrahāṃ trinayanāṃ māṇikya-mauli-sphurat-*
> *tārā-nāyaka-śekharāṃ smitamukhīm āpīna-vakṣoruhām*

| Element | Compound | Type | Meaning |
|---------|----------|------|---------|
| sindūrāruṇa-vigrahām | sindūra-aruṇa-vigraha + ām | Bahuvrīhi (accusative f.) | "Her whose form is vermillion-dawn-red" |
| trinayanām | tri-nayana + ām | Bahuvrīhi (acc. f.) | "Her who has three eyes" |
| māṇikya-mauli-sphurat-tārā-nāyaka-śekharām | Nested bahuvrīhi | Bahuvrīhi (acc. f.) | "Her whose crest-jewel is the moon shining from a ruby crown" |
| smitamukhīm | smita-mukhī + m | Bahuvrīhi (acc. f.) | "Her whose face is smiling" |
| āpīna-vakṣoruhām | āpīna-vakṣaḥ-ruha + ām | Bahuvrīhi (acc. f.) | "Her whose breasts are full" |

### What to Notice

Every descriptor is a **bahuvrīhi in the accusative feminine** — because the entire verse is the object of "dhyāyāmi" ("I meditate on [her]"). The dhyāna-śloka is one enormous noun-phrase: a chain of accusative bahuvrīhis all modifying the same unstated "tām" (her). The grammar IS the visualization — each compound adds one more detail to the image being constructed in the mind.

## Part 5: The Āvaraṇa Bīja System — Grammar of Progressive Revelation

In the Śrīcakra navāvaraṇa pūjā, each of the nine enclosures has its own bīja. These bījas are not random — they form a grammatical *derivation sequence* where each bīja builds on the previous:

| Āvaraṇa | Bīja | What Changed |
|----------|------|-------------|
| 1. Trailokyamohana | **aṃ āṃ sauḥ** | Seed-form: the simplest vowels + śakti-bīja |
| 2. Sarvāśāparipūraka | **aiṃ klīṃ sauḥ** | Bālā herself enters — the three śakti bījas |
| 3. Sarvasaṃkṣobhaṇa | **hrīṃ klīṃ sauḥ** | hrīṃ replaces aiṃ — māyā enters |
| 4. Sarvasaubhāgyadāyaka | **haiṃ hklīṃ hsauḥ** | **ha** prefixed to each bīja — Śiva's breath enters |
| 5. Sarvārthasādhaka | **hsaiṃ hsklīṃ hssauḥ** | **ha+sa** prefixed — full haṃsa (Śiva+Śakti) permeates |
| 6. Sarvarakṣākara | **hrīṃ klīṃ bleṃ** | Pattern breaks — bleṃ = grounding, protection |
| 7. Sarvarogahara | **hrīṃ śrīṃ sauḥ** | klīṃ → śrīṃ: desire becomes fullness |
| 8. Sarvasiddhiprada | **hsaiṃ hsklīṃ hssauḥ** | Mirrors 5th — haṃsa returns |
| 9. Sarvānandamaya | **Full Pañcadaśī** | All previous compressions expand into the complete vidyā |

### The Linguistic Principle

This is **upasarga-like prefixation applied to bījas:**

- Āvaraṇas 1-3: The base bījas establish themselves (aṃ → aiṃ → hrīṃ)
- Āvaraṇa 4: **h-** prefixes each bīja — Śiva (ha = breath = the last consonant) enters every syllable
- Āvaraṇa 5: **hs-** prefixes — the full haṃsa (ha + sa = Śiva + Śakti breathing together) permeates

This is exactly how Sanskrit builds meaning through prefixation: √gam (go) → **ā**-gam (come) → **sam-ā**-gam (come together). The bīja system works the same way: base bīja → h + bīja → hs + bīja. Each prefix deepens the energy.

The h-infusion means: *what was Śakti alone (aiṃ klīṃ sauḥ = Bālā = Śakti's mantra) now has Śiva breathing into it.* In the Hādī tradition, this reveals what the mūla-vidyā already states (ha is its first syllable). In the Kādī tradition, it compensates for what the Vāgbhava kūṭa withholds (ka leads, not ha).

## Part 6: Dīkṣitar's Vibhakti System — Case as Architecture

Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar composed 11 kṛtis for the Kamalāmbā navāvaraṇa cycle. Each kṛti uses a specific **vibhakti** (grammatical case) that matches the āvaraṇa number:

| Kṛti | Āvaraṇa | Vibhakti | How the Case Functions |
|------|---------|----------|-----------------------|
| Dhyāna | (pre-) | Mixed | Setting the scene |
| 1st āvaraṇa | Bhūpura | **Prathamā** (nominative) | "Kamalāmbā **sambhavati**" — she IS, she appears. Pure being. |
| 2nd āvaraṇa | 16-petal | **Dvitīyā** (accusative) | "Kamalāmb**ām** bhajare" — worship HER. She becomes the object. |
| 3rd āvaraṇa | 8-petal | **Tṛtīyā** (instrumental) | "Kamalāmb**ayā**" — BY her, THROUGH her. She becomes the means. |
| 4th āvaraṇa | 14-triangle | **Caturthī** (dative) | "Kamalāmb**āyai**" — FOR her, TO her. She becomes the recipient. |
| 5th āvaraṇa | 10-triangle (outer) | **Pañcamī** (ablative) | "Kamalāmb**āyāḥ**" — FROM her. She becomes the source. |
| 6th āvaraṇa | 10-triangle (inner) | **Ṣaṣṭhī** (genitive) | "Kamalāmb**āyāḥ**" — OF her. She becomes the possessor. |
| 7th āvaraṇa | 8-triangle | **Saptamī** (locative) | "Kamalāmb**āyām**" — IN her. She becomes the field. |
| 8th āvaraṇa | Inner triangle | **Sambodhana** (vocative) | "Kamalāmb**ike**!" — O Kamalāmbikā! Direct address. |
| 9th āvaraṇa | Bindu | Return to **Prathamā** | Full circle — she IS again, now at the center |

### What This Teaches

Dīkṣitar is demonstrating that the eight vibhaktis are not just grammar — they are the **eight possible relationships between the worshipper and the deity:**

1. She IS (identity — nominative)
2. I worship HER (object — accusative)
3. Through HER (means — instrumental)
4. For HER (offering — dative)
5. From HER (source — ablative)
6. Of HER (belonging — genitive)
7. In HER (location — locative)
8. O! (direct encounter — vocative)

The pūjā walks the worshipper through all eight relationships, one āvaraṇa at a time. By the bindu (center), you have exhausted every possible grammatical relationship to the deity. There is nothing left but identity — prathamā again.

This is the most sophisticated integration of grammar and worship in any Indian tradition. Every Sanskrit student should study these kṛtis.

## Part 7: Upacāra — The Offering Formula

The standard offering sentence in pūjā follows a rigid template:

> **[deity in dative] namaḥ — [offering in accusative] samarpayāmi**

Example:
> *śrīlalitā-mahātripurasundaryai namaḥ — gandhaṃ samarpayāmi*
> "Salutation to Śrī Lalitā Mahātripurasundarī — I offer sandalwood paste."

| Component | Case | Word | Function |
|-----------|------|------|----------|
| śrīlalitā-mahātripurasundaryai | **Caturthī** (dative, f.) | The full name of the deity | Recipient of offering |
| namaḥ | Indeclinable | "Salutation" | Ritually marks the offering |
| gandham | **Dvitīyā** (accusative, n.) | Sandalwood paste | The thing offered |
| samarpayāmi | Verb (causative, first person singular, present) | "I offer/present" | The act |

### The 16 Upacāras as Sanskrit Vocabulary

| # | Upacāra | Sanskrit | Case in Use |
|---|---------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Invocation | āvāhanam | acc. ("I perform invocation") |
| 2 | Seat | āsanam | acc. ("I offer a seat") |
| 3 | Water for feet | pādyam | acc. |
| 4 | Water for hands | arghyam | acc. |
| 5 | Water to sip | ācamanīyam | acc. |
| 6 | Bath | snānam / abhiṣekam | acc. |
| 7 | Cloth | vastram | acc. |
| 8 | Sacred thread | yajñopavītam | acc. |
| 9 | Sandalwood | gandham / candanam | acc. |
| 10 | Flowers | puṣpam / puṣpāṇi | acc. (sg. or pl.) |
| 11 | Incense | dhūpam | acc. |
| 12 | Lamp | dīpam | acc. |
| 13 | Food | naivedyam | acc. |
| 14 | Betel | tāmbūlam | acc. |
| 15 | Circumambulation | pradakṣiṇam | acc. |
| 16 | Prostration | namaskāram | acc. |

Every upacāra word is in the **accusative** — because it is the object being offered. The verb *samarpayāmi* takes an accusative object and a dative recipient. The grammar of offering is: "I give [this — acc.] to [you — dat.]."

## Summary

| Pūjā Component | Primary Vibhakti | What It Teaches |
|----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Saṅkalpa | Locative (time/place), Accusative (the rite), Genitive (the deity) | Future tense, purpose-compounds, complete sentence structure |
| Nyāsa | Dative (recipient of the mantra) | Dative declension across genders, dvivacana, the vowel-sequence |
| Dhyāna | Accusative (the visualized deity) | Bahuvrīhi compounds, participial adjectives |
| Upacāra | Dative (deity) + Accusative (offering) | The transitive offering construction |

| Āvaraṇa bījas | (Mantric — beyond grammar) | Prefix-derivation applied to bījas |
