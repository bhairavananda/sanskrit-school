## Section A: Key Concepts

**A1.** **Vyakaranam** means "grammar" — literally "analysis" or "that which separates and clarifies." It is the systematic study of the rules governing the Sanskrit language. (2 marks)

**A2.** Vyakaranam represents the **mouth** (mukha) of the Vedapurusa. This is significant because just as the mouth is essential for speech and expression, grammar is essential for the correct expression and understanding of Vedic knowledge. (2 marks)

**A3.** The great ancient grammarian is **Panini** (पाणिनि). His foundational text is the **Astadhyayi** (अष्टाध्यायी), consisting of approximately 4,000 sutras. (2 marks)

**A4.** The two prerequisites are:
1. **Ability to read and write Devanagari script**
2. **At least one year of prior Sanskrit study** (2 marks)

## Section D: Short Answer

**D1.** **Svara** (स्वर) refers to vowels — sounds that can be pronounced independently (a, i, u, etc.). **Vyanjana** (व्यञ्जन) refers to consonants — sounds that require a vowel to be fully pronounced (ka, kha, ga, etc.). (2 marks)

**D2.** **Sabda-rupa** (शब्दरूप) is the declension table for nouns and pronouns, showing forms across cases (vibhakti) and numbers. **Dhatu-rupa** (धातुरूप) is the conjugation table for verbs, showing forms across persons (purusa), numbers (vacana), and tenses/moods (lakara). (2 marks)

**D3.** **Sandhi** literally means "joining" or "union." It refers to the euphonic changes that occur when sounds meet at the boundary of words or morphemes. It is important in Sanskrit because Sanskrit is traditionally written and spoken with words flowing together, and sandhi rules govern how these junctions behave — incorrect sandhi leads to incorrect meaning or ungrammatical forms. (3 marks)

**D4.** The course begins with varna-vicara because **letters and sounds are the building blocks** of the entire language. Without understanding how individual sounds are classified (vowel vs. consonant, point of articulation, etc.), a student cannot understand sandhi rules (which depend on knowing which sounds combine and how), nor can they properly read declension and conjugation tables. The progression is logical: **sounds -> combinations -> word forms -> sentences**. (3 marks)

**E1.** Sample answer:

**Vyakaranam is essential because Sanskrit is a highly structured language where every sound, ending, and combination follows precise rules. Without grammar, a reader cannot determine the meaning of a sentence — the same root word changes meaning entirely based on its grammatical form. Grammar also prevents errors in sacred and literary texts where precision is paramount. Finally, understanding grammar reveals the extraordinary intellectual achievement of Panini and the ancient grammarians, transforming Sanskrit study from rote memorization into genuine comprehension.**
