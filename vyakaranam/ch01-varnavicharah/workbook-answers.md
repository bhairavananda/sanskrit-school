# Chapter 1: Varṇavicāraḥ — Workbook (Answers)

## Section A: Varṇa Basics

**A1.**
1. Varṇa — **An independent, indivisible unit of sound; the smallest component of language that cannot be broken further.**
2. Akṣara — **"That which does not perish" (na kṣarati); a letter that combines with others to form meaningful words.**
3. Svara — **A vowel; a letter that can be pronounced independently without the help of any other letter (svayaṃ rājante iti svarāḥ).**
4. Vyañjana — **A consonant; a letter that depends on a svara for pronunciation (svara-dharmam anugacchanti).**

**A2.** Total: approximately **63 letters**.
- **Svarāḥ: 13** (basic) + 3 pluta = 16
- **Vyañjanāni: 33** (25 sparśa + 4 antaḥstha + 4 ūṣman)
- **Ayogavāhāḥ: 4** (visarga, anusvāra, jihvāmūlīya, upadhmānīya)
- **Yamāḥ: 8**

(Accept reasonable variations in total depending on counting method.)

**A3.**
- A mātrā is **the unit of time for pronouncing a letter, approximately equal to one involuntary blink of the eye (~1 second).**
- **Hrasva = 1 mātrā, Dīrgha = 2 mātrās, Pluta = 3 mātrās.**

## Section B: Svara Classification

**B1.**
1. a → **Hrasva**
2. ī → **Dīrgha**
3. ai → **Dīrgha**
4. u → **Hrasva**
5. ṝ → **Dīrgha**

**B2.**
1. e → **Sandhyakṣara**
2. ū → **Samānākṣara**
3. o → **Sandhyakṣara**
4. ṛ → **Samānākṣara**

**B3.**
- **e = a + i** (one mātrā + one mātrā = two mātrā dīrgha)
- **au = ā + u** (or a + u, counted as dīrgha)

**B4.** **No.** The Śikṣā Śāstras state that the dīrgha form of ḷ is not attested even in Vedic literature.

## Section C: Vyañjana Classification

**C1.**

| Varga | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th |
|-------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| **ka-varga** | **ka** | **kha** | **ga** | **gha** | **ṅa** |
| **ca-varga** | **ca** | **cha** | **ja** | **jha** | **ña** |
| **ṭa-varga** | **ṭa** | **ṭha** | **ḍa** | **ḍha** | **ṇa** |
| **ta-varga** | **ta** | **tha** | **da** | **dha** | **na** |
| **pa-varga** | **pa** | **pha** | **ba** | **bha** | **ma** |

**C2.**
1. ka → **Alpaprāṇa** (1st letter)
2. gha → **Mahāprāṇa** (4th letter)
3. ṇa → **Alpaprāṇa** (5th letter / anunāsika)
4. pha → **Mahāprāṇa** (2nd letter)
5. śa → **Mahāprāṇa** (ūṣman)

**C3.**
1. ga → **Mṛdu** (3rd letter — voiced)
2. cha → **Kaṭhina** (2nd letter — voiceless)
3. ya → **Mṛdu** (antaḥstha)
4. sa → **Kaṭhina** (ūṣman, though some classify ūṣman separately)

**C4.** **Ha is mahāprāṇa (heavy breath) but mṛdu (soft/voiced).** This makes it unique among the ūṣman letters — śa, ṣa, and sa are kaṭhina (hard/voiceless), but ha is the exception.

**C5.**
- Antaḥstha: **ya, ra, la, va**
- Ūṣman: **śa, ṣa, sa, ha**

## Section D: Sthāna — Place of Articulation

**D1.**
1. ka → **Kaṇṭha (throat)**
2. ca → **Tālu (palate)**
3. ṭa → **Mūrdhan (roof of mouth)**
4. pa → **Oṣṭha (lips)**
5. ya → **Tālu (palate)**
6. sa → **Danta (teeth)**

**D2.**
1. Kaṇṭha → **Svara: a, ā; Varga: ka-varga (ka, kha, ga, gha, ṅa); also ha and visarga**
2. Mūrdhan → **Svara: ṛ, ṝ; Varga: ṭa-varga (ṭa, ṭha, ḍa, ḍha, ṇa); also ra, ṣa**
3. Kaṇṭha-tālu → **e, ai**
4. Dantoṣṭha → **va**

**D3.**
1. Sparśa consonants → **Spṛṣṭa (complete contact)**
2. Antaḥstha consonants → **Īṣat-spṛṣṭa (slight contact)**

## Section E: Ayogavāhāḥ and Yamāḥ

**E1.**
1. **Visarga (ḥ)** — voiceless breath after a vowel
2. **Anusvāra (ṃ)** — nasal resonance
3. **Jihvāmūlīya (ẖ)** — half-visarga before ka/kha
4. **Upadhmānīya (x͎)** — half-visarga before pa/pha

(Accept also: anunāsika/candrabindu as a variant listing.)

**E2.** Yamāḥ are **additional sounds that arise when the 5th letter (anunāsika) of a varga is followed by one of the first four letters of any varga.** They are **found primarily in Vedic recitation** and are not used in classical Sanskrit.

**E3.** **No.** Ayogavāhāḥ are not included in the Māheśvara Sūtras because they are neither svaras nor vyañjanas — they are classified separately and follow their own rules in grammar.

## Section F: Māheśvara Sūtras and Pratyāhāra

**F1.**
1. a i u **ṇ**
2. ṛ ḷ **k**
3. e o **ṅ**
4. **ai au** c
5. ha ya va ra **ṭ**
6. la **ṇ**
7. ña ma ṅa ṇa na **m**

**F2.** A pratyāhāra is **an abbreviatory notation that denotes a set of letters.** Rule: **Take the first letter of any sūtra as the starting point and combine it with the it-marker (last letter) of any subsequent sūtra. The pratyāhāra includes all letters from the starting letter up to (but not including) the it-marker.**

**F3.**
1. ac → **a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ, e, o, ai, au** (all vowels)
2. hal → **all consonants from ha (sūtra 5) through to ha (sūtra 14)** — i.e., ha, ya, va, ra, la, ña, ma, ṅa, ṇa, na, jha, bha, gha, ḍha, dha, ja, ba, ga, ḍa, da, kha, pha, cha, ṭha, tha, ca, ṭa, ta, ka, pa, śa, ṣa, sa, ha
3. yaṇ → **ya, va, ra, la** (the 4 semivowels)
4. ik → **i, u, ṛ, ḷ**
5. eṅ → **e, o**
6. jaś → **ja, ba, ga, ḍa, da** (3rd letter of each varga)

**F4.**
1. All vowels → **ac**
2. All consonants → **hal**
3. All semivowels → **yaṇ**

**F5.** **eṅ** — formed by taking e (from sūtra 3) to ṅ (the it-marker of sūtra 3). It contains **e and o**.

**F6.** **No.** A pratyāhāra cannot be formed between two it-markers alone. The starting element must be an actual letter (not an it-marker), and the ending must be an it-marker from a subsequent sūtra. The it-markers themselves are labels, not alphabet letters, and cannot serve as the starting point.
