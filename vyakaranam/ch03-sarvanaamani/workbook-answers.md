## Section D: Pronominal vs. Regular Declension

**D1.**

1. **deve** uses the regular -a stem saptamī ending **-e**, while **sarvasmin** uses the sarvānāma-pattern ending **-smin**. Pronominal words take special endings in caturthī, pañcamī, and saptamī singular.

2. **devāya** uses the regular caturthī ending **-āya**, while **tasmai** uses the pronominal ending **-smai**. This is a hallmark difference of sarvānāma declension.

3. **devāt** uses the regular pañcamī ending **-āt**, while **yasmāt** uses the pronominal ending **-smāt**. Same principle as above.

4. **devānām** uses the regular ṣaṣṭhī plural ending **-ānām**, while **teṣām** uses **-eṣām**. Pronominal forms show a vowel change in the plural.

5. **devam** is dvitīyā (accusative) singular of a regular noun, while **sarvam** as prathamā singular neuter is a nominative form. Though both end in -am, they represent different cases — the neuter prathamā/dvitīyā of sarvānāma words ends in -am (not -aḥ as in masculine).

**D2.**
1. sarva, caturthī, ekavacana, puṃliṅga: **sarvasmai**
2. sarva, pañcamī, ekavacana, puṃliṅga: **sarvasmāt**
3. sarva, saptamī, ekavacana, puṃliṅga: **sarvasmin**
4. sarva, prathamā, ekavacana, napuṃsakaliṅga: **sarvam**
5. sarva, ṣaṣṭhī, bahuvacana, puṃliṅga: **sarveṣām**

## Section E: Translation

**E1.** English to Sanskrit:

1. He studies Sanskrit. → **saḥ sanskṛtam paṭhati.**
2. She goes to the temple. → **sā mandiram gacchati.**
3. This is my book. → **etat mama pustakam (asti).**
4. Who is that boy? → **kaḥ saḥ bālakaḥ?**
5. I give water to him. → **aham tasmai jalam dadāmi.**
6. Whose house is this? → **kasya etat gṛham?**
7. All people study. → **sarve janāḥ paṭhanti.**
8. He who studies, he knows. → **yaḥ paṭhati saḥ jānāti.**

**E2.** Sanskrit to English:

1. sā bālikā sundarī asti. → **That girl is beautiful.**
2. eṣaḥ mama mitraḥ. → **This is my friend.**
3. kasya etat pustakam? → **Whose book is this?**
4. sarvāṇi puṣpāṇi sundarāṇi santi. → **All flowers are beautiful.**
5. aham tvayā saha gacchāmi. → **I go with you.**
6. yeṣāṃ vidyā asti te sukhino bhavanti. → **Those who have knowledge, they become happy.**
7. kim etat? → **What is this?**
