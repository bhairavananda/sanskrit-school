2. na**maḥ** — (salutation — udātta on second syllable)
3. **so**māya — (to Soma — udātta on first syllable)
4. ru**drā**ya — (to Rudra — udātta on second syllable)
5. tri**yam**bakam — (three-eyed one — udātta on second syllable)
6. su**gan**dhim — (fragrant — udātta on second syllable)
7. **puṣ**ṭi — (nourishment — udātta on first syllable)

**B2.** State the svara rule that applies in each situation (5 marks):
1. A word has three syllables. Only the middle one is udātta. What are the other two?

5. What happens when two udātta syllables from different words would be adjacent in saṃhitā?

## Section C: Saṃhitā vs Pada (20 marks)

**C1.** The following pada-pāṭha shows the Mahāmṛtyuñjaya mantra word-by-word. Write the saṃhitā form by applying sandhi rules (10 marks):

Pada-pāṭha:
```
oṃ | tryambakaṃ | yajāmahe | sugandhiṃ | puṣṭi-vardhanam |
urvārukam | iva | bandhanāt | mṛtyoḥ | mukṣīya | mā | amṛtāt |
```

Saṃhitā-pāṭha (fill in — apply the sandhi):
```
oṃ tryambakaṃ yajāmahe sugandhiṃ puṣṭi-vardhanam |
urvārukam iva bandhanāt mṛtyoḥ mukṣīya _____________ |
```

(Focus on the last section: what happens when mā + amṛtāt combine?)

**C2.** Explain why the tradition preserves *both* saṃhitā and pada forms (5 marks):
1. What does saṃhitā preserve that pada does not?
2. What does pada reveal that saṃhitā obscures?
3. Which form is used in actual ritual recitation?
4. Which form is used for grammatical analysis?
5. How does the relationship between saṃhitā and pada relate to the sandhi you learned in earlier stages?

**C3.** A corruption enters the text: one word is changed. Explain why this corruption would be detected more easily in krama-pāṭha than in saṃhitā-pāṭha (5 marks).
